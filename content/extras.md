# The Gospel
> Every conversation is headed here.

Winning the argument saves no one. Christ does. Say the whole message plainly, call for a response, and leave the results to God.

## Before you speak
- **God saves; you are faithful:** your job is to say the truth clearly. God gives the growth. (1 Cor 3:6–7; Rom 1:16; John 6:44)
- **Law before grace:** a person has to see their sin and God's judgment before the cross makes sense to them. (Rom 3:19–20; Gal 3:24)
- **Jesus is Lord:** the call is to repent and follow Christ, not to repeat a prayer or add Jesus to a life that stays the same. (Luke 9:23; Rom 10:9; Acts 20:21)
- **No false promises:** the gospel offers forgiveness and Christ Himself, not health, wealth or an easier life. (Phil 3:8; Mark 8:36; 2 Tim 3:12)

## God is holy
God created all things and made us for Himself. He is holy and just, He cannot overlook sin, and He will judge every person. (Gen 1:1; Isa 6:3; Hab 1:13; Acts 17:24–31; Heb 9:27)
**Ask:** *"What do you think happens after we die?"*

## We are sinners under judgment
Every one of us has broken God's law. We are not basically good people who slip up; we are dead in sin, and the wages of sin is death. No amount of good works can pay it off. (Rom 3:10–12; Rom 3:23; Rom 6:23; Eph 2:1–3; Isa 64:6)
**Ask:** *"Have you kept God's law perfectly?"*

## Christ died and rose
God the Son became man, lived the sinless life we have not lived, and died in the place of sinners, bearing the wrath they deserve. He was buried, rose bodily on the third day, and is Lord of all. (John 1:14; Isa 53:5–6; 2 Cor 5:21; 1 Pet 3:18; Rom 5:8; 1 Cor 15:3–4; Phil 2:9–11)
Only God can redeem a soul, and only a man can die in man's place, so the Savior is both. (Ps 49:7–9; Ps 49:15; 1 Tim 2:5–6; Heb 2:14–17)
**Ask:** *"Why did Jesus have to die?"*

## Repent and believe
God commands all people everywhere to repent. Whoever turns from sin and trusts in Christ alone is forgiven and has eternal life: by grace, through faith, not by works. Following Him costs everything, and no one who comes to Him is turned away. (Mark 1:15; Acts 17:30; John 3:16; Eph 2:8–9; Rom 10:9–13; Luke 14:27–28; John 6:37)
**Ask:** *"What is keeping you from turning to Christ today?"*

## The short version: the Romans Road
When you only have a minute, walk these in order, and call them to repent. (Rom 3:23; Rom 6:23; Rom 5:8; Acts 17:30; Rom 10:9–10; Rom 10:13; Rom 8:1)

## How to walk someone through it
For a step-by-step way to do this in a real conversation, using the Ten Commandments first and then the cross, open [The Way of the Master](/topic/the-way-of-the-master).

## If they respond
Pray with them. Get them a Bible and get them into a church that preaches it, where they can be baptized by immersion as a believer. Follow up. Don't pronounce them saved. Point them to Christ, and let assurance grow from following Him. (Acts 2:41–42; Matt 28:19–20; 2 Cor 13:5; 1 John 2:3–6; John 8:31)

## If they don't
Stay warm and leave the door open. You plant and water; God gives the growth. (1 Cor 3:6–7; 1 Pet 3:15–16; 2 Tim 2:24–26)

# The Way of the Master
> Use the law to reach the conscience, then give the gospel.

God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble (James 4:6), so: law to the proud, grace to the humble. The Ten Commandments work like a mirror: they show a person their sin so that the cross makes sense. Jesus did this with the rich young ruler. (Rom 3:19–20; Rom 7:7; Gal 3:24; Mark 10:17–22)

## 1. Start with an everyday question
Move from ordinary talk to spiritual things with one question.
**Ask:** *"What do you think happens after we die?"*

## 2. Are you a good person?
Almost everyone says yes. Let them say it. (Prov 20:6)
**Ask:** *"Would you consider yourself a good person?"*

## 3. Have you kept the Ten Commandments?
Walk through a few, one at a time, and let them answer each. (Ex 20:1–17)
- **Lying:** *"Have you ever told a lie? What does that make you?"*
- **Stealing:** *"Have you ever taken anything that wasn't yours, whatever its value?"*
- **God's name:** *"Have you ever used God's name as a curse word?"*
- **Lust:** Jesus said that looking with lust is adultery in the heart. *"Have you ever done that?"* (Matt 5:27–28)
- **Hatred:** Jesus put anger under the command "You shall not murder" (Matt 5:21–22), and John says whoever hates his brother is a murderer (1 John 3:15).

## 4. Judgment: innocent or guilty?
Sum up what they admitted in their own words, then ask. (Heb 9:27; Rom 2:15–16; James 2:10)
**Ask:** *"If God judges you by that standard, will you be innocent or guilty?"*

## 5. Destiny: heaven or hell?
Don't soften it, and don't gloat. Say it because you care where they end up. (Rev 21:8; 1 Cor 6:9–10)
**Ask:** *"Would that be heaven or hell? Does that concern you?"*

## 6. Now give the good news
You stand guilty in God's courtroom, and Someone steps in and pays your fine in full. That is what Christ did on the cross, and He rose from the dead. (Rom 5:8; 2 Cor 5:21; 1 Pet 2:24; 1 Cor 15:3–4)

## 7. Repent and trust
Believing a parachute exists won't save you; you have to put it on. Turn from sin and put your trust in Christ alone, today. (Acts 3:19; Acts 20:21; Rom 13:14; 2 Cor 6:2)
**Ask:** *"When are you going to do that?"*

## Keep in mind
- **Already humble?** If someone is broken over their sin, skip the law and go straight to grace.
- **Aim at the conscience:** answering objections clears the path, but the law and the gospel do the work. Don't spend an hour on arguments and never get here. (Rom 2:15)
- **Tone:** kind, patient, and plainly concerned for them. (2 Tim 2:24–26)

Then go back to [The Gospel](/topic/the-gospel) for the full message and what to do when they respond.

# Worldviews: how do you know?
> Everyone starts somewhere. Only God's word gives you ground to stand on.

A presupposition is what a person takes for granted before the argument starts. The atheist is not neutral, and neither are you. He already knows God and suppresses that truth (Rom 1:18–21), so you are not handing him a God he has never met. You are showing him that he leans on God every time he reasons, judges or trusts his own mind. (Prov 1:7; Col 2:3; Ps 14:1; 1 Cor 1:20)

## What you are showing
You are not saying he knows nothing or believes in nothing. He reasons, does science and knows right from wrong, because he lives in God's world and is made in God's image. You are saying his worldview cannot **account for** any of it, and yours can. He is using the tools God gave him to argue against God.
**Say:** *"We both use reason. I'm not asking whether you reason. I'm asking why reason can be trusted if your view is true."*

## Step 1: Don't give up your ground
Do not agree to set the Bible aside and argue from "neutral" facts. No one is neutral, and Scripture is the standard, not the thing on trial. You start from what God has revealed. He starts from his own guess. (Prov 26:4; 2 Cor 10:5; 1 Pet 3:15)
**Ask:** *"Would you set aside your trust in reason to prove reason? Then why should I set aside God's word?"*

## Step 2: Ask what he would accept
Before you give evidence, find out what he counts as evidence. If he has no standard, he has no basis for saying there isn't any.
**Ask:** *"What would count as evidence for you?"*
- **"Only what can be measured or tested."** → *"Did you measure that? Which experiment showed that only tested things are true?"* That rule is not something the five senses can find.
- **"I don't know what would convince me."** → *"Then how do you know there's no evidence?"*
- **"God is outside the universe, so He can't be tested."** → Evidence is more than lab results. We reason, weigh testimony and draw conclusions every day. (Acts 1:3; Heb 11:1)

## Step 3: Step into his view and show it cannot stand
Take his view on its own terms and ask what follows. One question at a time, and wait for the answer. (Prov 26:5)
- **Knowledge:** *"How do you know that? How do you know anything for certain?"* If he says we can't know anything for certain: *"Are you certain of that?"*
- **Logic:** *"Are the laws of logic made of matter? Do they change? Where do unchanging, universal laws come from in a universe of matter in motion?"* If he says God is illogical: *"That assumes logic is reliable. In your view, where does it come from?"*
- **His own mind:** *"You said 'mind.' Is that anything more than brain chemistry? If your thoughts are chemistry shaped for survival, why trust them to tell you what is true?"*
- **Science:** *"Why expect nature to act tomorrow the way it did today?"* (Gen 8:22; Col 1:17; Heb 1:3)
- **Meaning:** *"If we came from nothing and go to nothing, on what basis does anything you do matter?"* If he says nothing has meaning: *"Does that sentence have meaning?"* (Eccl 1:2; Eccl 12:13–14)

## Right and wrong
Start here when he calls something evil, unfair or hateful.
**Ask:** *"Is morality objective, or is it opinion?"*
- **"It's subjective."** → *"Then you can't say I'm wrong. You can only say you prefer something else, the way one person prefers burgers and another pizza."* Watch for him to keep saying you are wrong anyway. Point it out kindly.
- **"It comes from my experience."** → *"Someone raised to hate his neighbors has experience too. Is his hatred good?"* Experience differs from person to person, so it cannot be the standard.
- **"It comes from science."** → Science tells you what **is**. It cannot tell you what **ought** to be. *"Which experiment shows that lying is wrong?"*
- **"That's just your interpretation of the Bible."** → Everyone interprets something: he interprets data and his own experience. Disagreement does not cancel truth. *"If someone says 1 + 1 = 3, does that make the answer uncertain?"*
- **"That's true for you. This is my truth."** → An opinion is not the same as truth. Truth is what matches reality, whoever believes it. *"Can a person sincerely believe something and be wrong?"*
- **"It's objective."** → *"Then who set the standard, and why does it bind everyone?"* (Rom 2:14–15)

## Start with the Creator
Many people today have no Bible background. Words like sin, judgment and Savior mean little to someone who thinks he is an accident. Peter preached to people who knew the Scriptures and went straight to Christ and repentance (Acts 2:22–38). Paul, in Athens, began with the God who made the world and everything in it, and then said God now commands all people everywhere to repent (Acts 17:24–31). John's Gospel begins the same way: all things came into being through Christ (John 1:1–3; Col 1:16). You are not delaying the gospel. You are telling him whose world this is, so that sin makes sense and the cross is good news.
**Ask:** *"Who made you? And if you were made, who do you answer to?"*
- **Made on purpose.** He is not a cosmic accident. He was made by God, in God's image, to know Him. (Gen 1:27; Ps 100:3; Ps 139:13–14)
- **Worth.** Every person bears God's image: unborn, disabled, old, unable to contribute anything. *"If worth depends on what a person can do, is a strong man worth more than a child with Down syndrome?"* (Gen 9:6; James 3:9)
- **Guilt.** He may have been taught that guilt is only herd instinct or upbringing. *"When someone wrongs you, is that only instinct, or did he really do wrong?"* The conscience is God's law written on the heart. (Rom 2:14–15)
- **Then sin and the Savior.** The One who made him is the One he has sinned against, and the One who came to die for sinners. (Col 1:16; Col 1:19–20)

## Step 4: Bring him back to your ground
Show that what he has been borrowing belongs to God. Logic reflects God's unchanging mind, morality His holy character, the order of nature His faithfulness, and our reasoning His image in us. All things were made through the Word. (Gen 1:27; Mal 3:6; John 1:1–3; Acts 17:24–28)

## If he says
- **"So you're saying atheists can't know anything?"** → No. He knows a great deal. The question is whether his worldview can explain how.
- **"I just lack belief. I don't have to prove anything."** → *"You still reason, judge and trust your mind every day. What do those rest on?"*
- **"That's circular. You're using God to prove God."** → Every final starting point has to vouch for itself, or it isn't final. *"Can you prove your words have meaning without using words?"* The question is which starting point makes sense of everything else. His explains nothing. God's word explains logic, morality, science and him. (Heb 6:13)
- **"We both just assume our senses work."** → For him the senses are the foundation, with nothing under them. For you they are a tool, and under them is the God who made eyes, minds and the world they fit.
- **"Logic is just a fact of the universe."** → That repeats the claim; it doesn't explain it. *"Would you accept it if I said God is just a fact of the universe?"*
- **"Logic is just how humans think."** → Then it could have been otherwise, and it would not bind anyone who thinks differently, including you.
- **"Morality comes from evolution or society."** → That tells you what people **do** approve of, not what they **ought** to. Was slavery right when society approved it?
- **"I don't know where they come from, and that's fine."** → *"So you rely on them with no reason to. That is faith. I can tell you where they come from."*

## Watch out
- **Don't say "you believe in nothing."** He will name things he believes, and he'll be right. Say his worldview can't account for them.
- **Don't call him or his view dumb, and don't dismiss him.** Say "that doesn't hold together," and stay kind when he gets sharp. (2 Tim 2:24–26)
- **Don't blame evil on God's hands being tied.** When evil or hell comes up, don't say God wanted to stop it but free will prevented Him. God is sovereign over all things, He is not the author of sin, and people answer for their own sin. Use [the evil and suffering answer](/atheist/objection/if-god-good-why-there) and [the "why create me?" answer](/atheist/objection/if-god-knew-id-reject). (Eph 1:11; Gen 50:20; Acts 2:23)
- **Don't trade away creation to sound reasonable.** God made everything in six days. See [the evolution answer](/atheist/objection/evolution-explains-life-we-dont). The full case is on [The fallacy of evolution](/topic/the-fallacy-of-evolution).
- **Don't treat it as a trick.** Winning the exchange saves no one. He is not confused; he is suppressing the truth, and only God opens blind eyes. (2 Cor 4:4–6; 1 Cor 2:14)
- **Evidence still belongs.** The apostles pointed to the empty tomb and the eyewitnesses. Give the evidence as God's facts, not as something a neutral judge gets to rule on. (Acts 17:31; 1 Cor 15:3–8)
- **Don't lean on quotes and numbers you can't back up.** A line credited to Darwin or a statistic from a sermon can be wrong, and one bad quote costs you the hearing. Stay with Scripture and with questions.
- **Get to the cross.** This clears the ground. Then give [The Gospel](/topic/the-gospel).

# The problem of evil
> Why does a good God allow suffering?

This is the question behind most unbelief, and often it is not an argument but a wound. Find out which before you answer. Scripture does not explain every sorrow. It tells us who rules over suffering, where it came from, what God has done about it, and how it ends. (Deut 29:29; Rom 8:28)

## First, listen
**Ask:** *"Is this a question you're thinking through, or something you're living through?"*
If he is hurting, sit with him before you reason with him. Job's friends did well for seven days, until they opened their mouths. Weep with those who weep. (Job 2:13; Rom 12:15; John 11:35)

## What you are showing
To call anything evil, he needs a real standard of good that stands above every person and culture. In a universe of matter and chance there is suffering, but there is no "ought not." The complaint itself borrows from the God it is aimed at. See [Morality and justice](/topic/morality-and-justice).
**Ask:** *"When you say that's evil, do you mean you dislike it, or that it is really wrong? By what standard?"*

## What Scripture says
- **God made the world very good.** Evil is not part of what He made. (Gen 1:31)
- **Sin brought death and suffering in.** Man rebelled, and the whole creation was subjected to futility. (Gen 3:17–19; Rom 5:12; Rom 8:20–22)
- **God is sovereign over all of it, and He is not the author of sin.** Nothing happens outside His rule, and He tempts no one. (Eph 1:11; Isa 45:7; James 1:13; 1 John 1:5)
- **He means for good what men mean for evil.** Joseph's brothers sold him; God sent him to save lives. (Gen 50:20; Rom 8:28)
- **The cross is the clearest case.** The worst evil ever done, the murder of the Son of God, was carried out by lawless men and was God's predetermined plan to save sinners. If God can bring the greatest good out of the greatest evil, He can be trusted with the rest. (Acts 2:23; Acts 4:27–28)
- **He does not owe us an account.** When Job asked why, God answered with Himself. (Job 38:1–4; Job 42:1–6; Rom 9:20)
- **He entered it.** Our God is not far off from pain. He became a man of sorrows. (Isa 53:3–5; Heb 4:15)
- **He will end it.** Every wrong will be judged, and every tear wiped away. (Rev 21:3–4; Acts 17:31; Rom 8:18)

## If they say
- **"A good God would stop evil."** → He will, and on a set day. The harder question is what happens to us when He does, since the evil is not only out there. He waits because He is patient. (2 Pet 3:9; Rom 2:4) *"If God removed all evil at midnight tonight, where would that leave you and me?"*
- **"Why doesn't He stop it now?"** → We are not told every reason, and a God big enough to blame is big enough to have reasons we cannot see. What we are told is that He is working all things together for good for those who love Him. (Rom 8:28; Isa 55:8–9)
- **"What about natural disasters and disease?"** → They belong to a creation that groans under the curse, and Jesus says they are a call to repent, not proof that the victims were worse than we are. (Rom 8:20–22; Luke 13:1–5)
- **"What about children who suffer?"** → Do not hurry this one. God is the defender of the helpless and will judge every hand raised against a child. Our own outrage is His image in us. (Ps 10:14; Matt 18:6; Gen 18:25)
- **"I prayed and He didn't answer."** → Paul prayed three times, and Christ prayed in Gethsemane. The answer was grace to bear it, and then resurrection. (2 Cor 12:8–9; Matt 26:39)
- **"Then God is the cause of evil."** → No. Men do evil from their own hearts and are fully responsible for it. God rules over it for holy ends without doing it. Scripture holds both together in one sentence. (Acts 2:23; James 1:13–14)

## Watch out
- **Don't say God's hands were tied by our free will.** He is never helpless. Say He is sovereign and good, and that He has purposes.
- **Don't explain a particular tragedy.** You do not know why this child died or that cancer came. Say so. (Deut 29:29)
- **Don't answer a wound with an argument.** Know which one is in front of you.
- **Don't leave out the cross.** It is the only place the problem of evil is answered and not only discussed.

## Bring it to the gospel
The real question is not why bad things happen to good people. There was only one good Person, and He volunteered. He suffered for sinners so that suffering would not have the last word over them. (1 Pet 3:18; 2 Cor 5:21; Rom 8:32)
**Ask:** *"God did something about evil at the cross. Have you ever looked at what He did there for you?"*

Then give [The Gospel](/topic/the-gospel).

# Morality and justice
> Can morality exist without God?

Everyone you meet believes some things are really wrong. Your aim is to show that only the God of Scripture makes sense of that, and that the same God who gives the law will judge by it. (Rom 2:14–16; Acts 17:31)

## What you are showing
You are not saying an unbeliever cannot be kind, honest or brave. He can, because he is made in God's image and God's law is written on his heart. You are saying he cannot **account for** right and wrong. He can know it, and he cannot explain why it binds anyone. (Rom 2:14–15; Gen 1:27)
**Ask:** *"Is anything wrong for everyone, everywhere, whether they agree or not? What makes it so?"*

## Where the standard comes from
- **Good is what God is.** He does not look up to a standard above Him, and He does not invent it on a whim. Righteousness and justice are the foundation of His throne. (Ps 89:14; Deut 32:4; 1 John 1:5)
- **He has told us.** What is good is not a mystery. (Mic 6:8; Ex 20:1–17; Matt 22:37–40)
- **It is written on every heart.** That is why cultures everywhere punish murder, theft and betrayal. (Rom 2:14–15)
- **People matter because God made them.** Human worth does not come from usefulness or strength. (Gen 9:6; James 3:9)

## Why the other answers fail
- **"Each person decides."** → Then no one is ever wrong, including the man who robs him. He will not live by that. Press it to the end: if right and wrong come from the individual, "my truth," then the rapist is never wrong, because he was acting on his. *"Was he wrong, or only different from you?"*
- **"Society decides."** → Then the reformer is always the villain. Slavery was legal, and the people who opposed it were breaking society's rules. *"Was slavery right when most people approved of it?"* And if a society's agreement is what makes a thing good, then what Hitler's Germany did to the Jews was good, there and then, for them. Nobody believes that. At Nuremberg the judges did not accept "our laws allowed it"; they held those men to a law above every nation. *"Whose law was that?"*
- **"Evolution gave us morals."** → That would explain why we feel them, not why we must obey them. Survival also rewards cruelty and deceit.
- **"Whatever reduces suffering."** → Why should anyone care about a stranger's suffering? That is already a moral rule, and it needs a source.
- **"Science tells us."** → Science describes what is. It cannot tell you what ought to be.
- **"Is it good because God commands it, or does He command it because it's good?"** → Neither. It is good because it reflects who He is, and His nature does not change. (Mal 3:6; James 1:17)

## Justice needs a Judge
- **Most wrongs are never put right in this life.** Killers die in their beds. If death ends everything, justice is a wish. (Eccl 8:14; Ps 73:3–17)
- **God will bring every act into judgment.** That is good news for every victim. (Eccl 12:14; Rom 2:5–6; Rev 20:11–13)
- **It is hard news for all of us.** The standard we use on others will be used on us, and none of us has kept even our own. (Rom 2:1; Rom 3:19; Rom 3:23)
**Ask:** *"You want justice done. Do you want it done to you?"*

## If they say
- **"I don't need God to be good."** → Agreed that you can do good things. Good by whose measure? And have you done all the good you know? (James 4:17)
- **"Religious people do terrible things."** → Yes, and you can only call them terrible if there is a standard they broke. Christianity gives you the grounds to condemn its own hypocrites. (Matt 7:21–23)
- **"The God of the Old Testament commanded terrible things."** → To judge God you need a standard above Him. Where does it come from? Then open the text: the Judge of all the earth does right, and He is patient for centuries before He judges. (Gen 18:25; Gen 15:16)
- **"Morality is just empathy."** → Empathy is a feeling, and feelings differ. Some feel none. *"Is cruelty wrong for the man who feels nothing?"*

## Bring it to the gospel
At the cross God did not set justice aside to show mercy. He satisfied it. Christ bore the penalty, so that God is both just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus. (Rom 3:25–26; Isa 53:5–6; 1 John 1:9)
**Ask:** *"If God judged you by your own standard, how would you do? Would you like to know how a guilty person can be declared right?"*

Then give [The Gospel](/topic/the-gospel). For the way of arguing behind this page, see [Worldviews](/topic/worldviews-how-do-you-know).

# The exclusivity of Christ
> Why is Jesus the only way?

We did not make this claim. Jesus did. The question is never whether Christians are narrow. It is whether what He said is true. (John 14:6; Acts 4:12)

## What you are showing
Every view of God shuts some views out. The man who says all religions lead to God is telling the Muslim, the Hindu and the Christian that each is wrong about his own faith. There is no neutral, wider place to stand. So ask which claim is true.
**Ask:** *"When you say all paths lead to God, how do you know that? And doesn't that say every religion is wrong where it disagrees with you?"*

## What Jesus and the apostles said
- **"I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father but through Me."** (John 14:6)
- **No other name.** (Acts 4:12)
- **One God, one Mediator.** (1 Tim 2:5–6)
- **Whoever has the Son has life.** (John 3:36; 1 John 5:11–12)
- **The Old Testament says the same of God.** "There is no savior besides Me." (Isa 43:11; Isa 45:22)

## Why there can be only one
- **Because of what the problem is.** We are not lost for lack of advice. We are guilty before a holy God. Guilt needs a payment, not a path. (Rom 3:23; Rom 6:23)
- **Because of who can pay.** Only one who is truly man can stand in for men, and only one who is truly God can bear it. No one else is both. (Heb 2:17; Col 2:9; Ps 49:7–8)
- **Because of what the cross means.** If there were another way, the Father would have taken it. Christ asked in the garden. (Matt 26:39; Gal 2:21)
- **Because He rose.** No other founder left an empty tomb. God gave proof to everyone by raising Him. (Acts 17:31; Rom 1:4)

## The religions do not agree
They cannot all be true, because they contradict each other on the very things that matter: who God is, who Jesus is, and how a sinner is made right. Every other religion says **do**. The gospel says **done**. (Eph 2:8–9; John 19:30)

## If they say
- **"That's arrogant."** → It would be, if we were the way. We are beggars telling other beggars where the bread is. Arrogance is deciding for God how He must save. (1 Cor 4:7; Titus 3:5)
- **"What about people who never heard?"** → No one is condemned for not hearing. Men are condemned for sin against the light they have: creation and conscience. That is why we go. (Rom 1:18–20; Rom 2:12–16; Rom 10:13–15) *"You have heard. What will you do with it?"*
- **"Sincere people in other religions will be fine."** → Sincerity does not change what is true. A man can sincerely take the wrong medicine. (Prov 14:12; Rom 10:1–3)
- **"All religions teach basically the same thing."** → On ethics there is overlap. On God and salvation they are opposites. Ask him to name what Islam, Hinduism and Christianity each say about Jesus.
- **"It's exclusive, so it's intolerant."** → The way is narrow, and the invitation is as wide as the world. "Whoever will call on the name of the Lord will be saved." (Matt 7:13–14; Rom 10:13; John 6:37; Rev 22:17)
- **"That's true for you."** → Jesus did not say He was a way for some. He rose in public history, or He did not.

## Bring it to the gospel
The only way is an open way. He turns away no one who comes. (John 6:37; Matt 11:28; Isa 55:1)
**Ask:** *"If He is the only way to the Father, are you on it?"*

Then give [The Gospel](/topic/the-gospel).

# The resurrection
> Historical evidence that Jesus rose from the dead.

Christianity stands or falls here, and Paul says so. If Christ has not been raised, our faith is worthless. He has been raised, and God has given proof of it to everyone. Give the evidence as God's facts, not as an exhibit for a neutral judge. (1 Cor 15:14; 1 Cor 15:17; Acts 17:31)

## What you are showing
The resurrection was preached in the city where it happened, within weeks, in front of the people who had killed Him and could have produced the body. It was not a legend that grew far away over centuries. (Acts 2:22–24; Acts 2:32; Acts 26:26)
**Ask:** *"If Jesus really rose from the dead, would you follow Him? If not, is evidence really the issue?"*

## The facts to account for
- **He died.** Roman soldiers knew when a man was dead, and a spear confirmed it. (John 19:33–35; Mark 15:44–45)
- **He was buried in a known tomb,** belonging to a member of the council. (Mark 15:43–46)
- **The tomb was empty on the third day.** The authorities never denied it. They explained it by saying the body was stolen. (Matt 28:11–15)
- **Women found it first.** No one inventing the story in that world would have chosen witnesses the courts did not accept. (Luke 24:1–11; John 20:1–2)
- **He appeared to many, over forty days.** To individuals, to groups, to more than five hundred at once, most of them still alive when Paul wrote. They ate with Him and touched Him. (1 Cor 15:3–8; Luke 24:36–43; Acts 1:3)
- **The witnesses were changed.** Men who ran and hid went out and preached it, and suffered for it. People die for what they believe is true. No one dies for what he knows he made up. (Acts 4:18–20; Acts 5:40–42)
- **Enemies believed.** James, who had not believed in his brother, and Saul, who was hunting Christians, each said he saw the risen Christ. (John 7:5; 1 Cor 15:7–8; Gal 1:13–16)
- **It was foretold.** (Ps 16:10; Isa 53:10–11; Matt 16:21)

## The summary Paul received
The statement in 1 Corinthians 15:3–5 is one Paul says he was handed. He met Peter and James in Jerusalem within a few years of the cross. This is testimony from the beginning, from the men who were there. (1 Cor 15:3–5; Gal 1:18–19)

## If they say
- **"The disciples stole the body."** → Then they suffered and died for what they knew was a fraud, and not one of them broke. And the guards' story is "we were asleep, and we saw who did it." (Matt 28:11–15)
- **"He didn't really die."** → A man scourged, crucified and speared does not roll a stone, overpower guards and convince anyone he is the Lord of life. (John 19:33–34)
- **"They hallucinated."** → Groups do not share one hallucination, and not over forty days in different places. And the tomb would still hold the body. (1 Cor 15:6; Luke 24:39–43)
- **"They went to the wrong tomb."** → Then the authorities only had to go to the right one.
- **"It's a legend that grew later."** → The claim is in the earliest sources, in the names of living witnesses, in the city where it happened. (1 Cor 15:3–8; Luke 1:1–4)
- **"Dead men don't rise. Miracles are impossible."** → No one says men rise by nature. The claim is that God raised Him. That is only impossible if there is no God, which is the very thing in question. (Acts 26:8; Jer 32:17)
- **"The Gospels contradict each other."** → Witnesses who agree on the event and differ in what they include are what honest testimony looks like. See [Is the Bible reliable?](/topic/is-the-bible-reliable)

## Watch out
- **Evidence does not raise the dead.** Men saw Lazarus walk out and plotted to kill him. If they do not hear Moses and the Prophets, they will not be persuaded if someone rises from the dead. Pray, and preach. (John 12:9–11; Luke 16:31)
- **Don't argue "probably."** The apostles announced it. (Acts 2:36)
- **Don't stop at the fact.** Say what it means.

## What it means
- **Jesus is who He said He is.** (Rom 1:4)
- **The payment was accepted.** He was raised for our justification. (Rom 4:25)
- **Death is beaten.** Those who are His will be raised. (1 Cor 15:20–22; John 11:25–26)
- **His resurrection is not like Lazarus's.** Lazarus came out still bound in his grave clothes and later died again. Jesus left His lying in the tomb and will never die again. Lazarus shows that Christ's voice commands death; Christ's own rising is the first of a new kind of life, the one His people will share. [Abner Chou, sermon on John 11:41–44] (John 11:43–44; John 20:6–7; Rom 6:9; 1 Cor 15:20)
- **A day of judgment is fixed, and the Judge has been named.** (Acts 17:30–31)
**Ask:** *"He is alive today. What will you do with a living Lord?"*

Then give [The Gospel](/topic/the-gospel). For the short version to run live, open [the resurrection play](/atheist/case/play-4).

# Hell and eternal judgment
> Why would a loving God send people to hell?

Speak about hell the way Jesus did: plainly, often, and with tears. He said more about it than anyone in Scripture, and He is the one who went to the cross to save people from it. (Matt 10:28; Luke 19:41; Matt 23:37)

## What you are showing
The question assumes we are basically good people and God is overreacting. Scripture says the opposite about both. Once a man sees who God is and what sin is, the question changes from "How could God send anyone to hell?" to "How could God let anyone into heaven?" (Rom 3:10–12; Rom 3:23; Hab 1:13)
**Ask:** *"Do you think anything deserves judgment? Then who decides what, and how much?"*

## What Scripture says
- **Hell is real, conscious and eternal.** Jesus uses the same word for the punishment and for the life. (Matt 25:41; Matt 25:46; Rev 14:11; Rev 20:10)
- **It is judgment, not an accident.** It is the just sentence of a holy Judge on real guilt. (Rev 20:11–15; Rom 2:5–6; 2 Thess 1:8–9)
- **It is fair.** God judges each one according to his deeds and according to the light he had. (Rom 2:6; Luke 12:47–48; Gen 18:25)
- **No one is there who wanted Christ.** Men love darkness and will not come to the light. (John 3:19–20; John 5:40)
- **God takes no pleasure in it.** (Ezek 33:11; 2 Pet 3:9)
- **There is no second chance after death.** (Heb 9:27; Luke 16:26)

## Why it is eternal
- **Because of who is sinned against.** The weight of a wrong rises with the worth of the one wronged. Sin is against an infinitely holy God. (Ps 51:4; Isa 6:3–5)
- **Because sinners there do not stop sinning.** Hell does not make anyone repent. (Rev 16:9–11)
- **Because the cross shows what sin costs.** If sin were small, the Son of God would not have had to die for it. (Rom 8:32; Isa 53:10)

## If they say
- **"A loving God wouldn't do that."** → A God who shrugged at rape and murder would not be loving. Love for victims requires justice. And His love is not a guess: He gave His Son. (Rom 5:8; John 3:16; Nah 1:3)
- **"Eternal punishment for a few years of sin isn't fair."** → Time spent sinning is not how we measure a crime. A murder takes a moment. And see "Why it is eternal" above.
- **"I'm a good person."** → By whose standard? Walk through the commandments. See [The Way of the Master](/topic/the-way-of-the-master). (James 2:10; Rom 3:19–20)
- **"God sends people to hell for not believing in Jesus."** → People are condemned for their sin. Jesus is the rescue they refuse. A man does not die because he refused the doctor, but from the disease. (John 3:17–18; John 3:36)
- **"What about those who never heard?"** → See [The exclusivity of Christ](/topic/the-exclusivity-of-christ). (Rom 1:18–20)
- **"Hell is just separation from God" / "the wicked are simply destroyed."** → Jesus describes fire, darkness, weeping and a punishment as lasting as eternal life. (Matt 13:41–42; Matt 25:46; Mark 9:47–48)
- **"I'd rather be in hell with my friends."** → There is no friendship there. Every good thing you have ever enjoyed came from the God you would be without. (James 1:17; Luke 16:23–28)

## Watch out
- **Don't soften what Jesus said, and don't enjoy saying it.** Paul warned people with tears. (Acts 20:31; Rom 9:1–3)
- **Don't pronounce on a particular dead person.** Say God will do right, and turn to the living man in front of you. (Gen 18:25)
- **Don't use hell as a threat to win.** It is a warning from someone who was headed there too. (Eph 2:3–5; 2 Cor 5:11)
- **Don't leave him there.** Hell is never the end of the conversation.

## Bring it to the gospel
On the cross Christ bore the wrath of God for sinners. Everyone's sin will be punished: either in hell, or in Him. That is why no one has to go. (Isa 53:5–6; Gal 3:13; 1 Thess 1:10; Rom 8:1)
**Ask:** *"He took what sinners deserve so they could go free. Will you turn from your sin and trust Him?"*

Then give [The Gospel](/topic/the-gospel).

# Faith and reason
> Is believing in God irrational, or blind faith?

Biblical faith is not belief without evidence, and it is not a leap in the dark. It is trust in a Person who has spoken and has proved Himself trustworthy. And reason is not faith's rival. It is a gift from the God the unbeliever is using it against. (Heb 11:1; Isa 1:18; Prov 1:7)

## What you are showing
Everyone trusts something he cannot prove first: his senses, his memory, his reason, the order of nature. The unbeliever has faith too. The question is not faith or reason. It is which faith makes reason possible. See [Worldviews](/topic/worldviews-how-do-you-know).
**Ask:** *"What do you trust that you can't prove? Why do you trust your own mind?"*

## What faith is
- **Faith is trust in what God has said.** Abraham was fully assured that what God had promised, He was able to do. (Rom 4:20–21; Heb 11:1; Heb 11:6)
- **It rests on what God has done in public.** The apostles appealed to what was seen, heard and touched, and said it was not done in a corner. (1 John 1:1–3; Luke 1:1–4; Acts 26:25–26; 2 Pet 1:16)
- **It comes by hearing the word of Christ.** (Rom 10:17; John 20:30–31)
- **It is a gift.** No one reasons himself into the kingdom. God opens blind eyes. (Eph 2:8–9; 2 Cor 4:4–6; 1 Cor 2:14)

## What reason is
- **God commands us to think.** "Come now, and let us reason together." We are to love Him with all our mind. (Isa 1:18; Matt 22:37; Rom 12:2)
- **The apostles reasoned.** Paul reasoned from the Scriptures, explaining and giving evidence. We are told to be ready with a defense. (Acts 17:2–3; Acts 18:4; 1 Pet 3:15)
- **Reason has a starting point.** The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge. All the treasures of wisdom are hidden in Christ. (Prov 1:7; Col 2:3; Ps 36:9)
- **Reason is a tool, not the judge.** It is used under God's word, and it is bent by sin like the rest of us. (Rom 1:21–22; 2 Cor 10:5; Eph 4:17–18)

## If they say
- **"Faith is believing without evidence."** → That is not how the Bible uses the word. You have faith in your wife or your surgeon because of what you know of them. *"Do you trust anyone you've never tested in a lab?"*
- **"I only believe what can be proven by science."** → That sentence cannot be proven by science. And science itself runs on trust that nature is orderly and our minds can know it. (Gen 8:22; Col 1:17)
- **"Science has replaced God."** → Science explains how things in the world work. It does not explain why there is a world, why it is orderly, or why we can understand it. Modern science grew among men who expected order because they believed in a Lawgiver.
- **"Smart people don't believe."** → Many do, and always have. But truth is not settled by counting, and God chose what the world calls foolish to shame the wise. (1 Cor 1:20–27)
- **"You believe because you were raised that way."** → Where a belief came from does not tell you whether it is true. He was raised to believe things too.
- **"Show me God and I'll believe."** → He has shown Himself in what He made, in conscience, in Scripture, and in His Son. People saw Jesus raise the dead and still refused. (Rom 1:19–20; John 12:37; John 14:9)
- **"If I can't understand it, I won't accept it."** → He accepts many things he cannot fully understand. And a God we could fit inside our heads would not be God. (Isa 55:8–9; Rom 11:33)

## Watch out
- **Don't apologize for starting with Scripture.** Everyone starts somewhere.
- **Don't set faith against thinking.** Don't say "you just have to believe." Give a reason. (1 Pet 3:15)
- **Don't think a good argument saves.** The problem is not the mind alone, but the heart that suppresses the truth. (Rom 1:18; John 3:19)

## Bring it to the gospel
The most reasonable thing a creature can do is believe his Creator. God has spoken, He has come, and He has raised His Son in proof. (Heb 1:1–2; Acts 17:31)
**Ask:** *"If God has spoken, is it more reasonable to trust Him or to trust yourself?"*

Then give [The Gospel](/topic/the-gospel).

# The fallacy of evolution
> Why evolution cannot stand, and why creation is true.

God made the heavens, the earth and everything in them in six days, and He made man directly, in His image (Gen 1:1; Gen 1:27; Gen 2:7; Ex 20:11). That is our starting point, not our conclusion. Evolution is not a discovery that forced itself on honest observers. It is the origin story of a worldview that has already ruled God out. (Rom 1:20–22; 2 Pet 3:3–6)

## What you are showing
You and he look at the same fossils, the same rocks and the same DNA. The difference is not the evidence. It is the starting point each of you reads it with. No one watched the beginning, and no one can repeat it in a lab. One Witness was there, and He has told us what He did. (Job 38:4; Heb 11:3; Ps 33:6; Ps 33:9)
**Ask:** *"Were you there? Then whose account of the beginning are you trusting, and why?"*

## The starting point decides the answer
Many of evolution's own defenders say so. Harvard geneticist Richard Lewontin wrote that scientists accept material explanations because of a prior commitment to materialism, and that "we cannot allow a Divine Foot in the door" (*New York Review of Books*, January 9, 1997). That is a rule about what answers are allowed, made before the evidence is looked at.
**Ask:** *"If God did create, would your method ever be allowed to find that out?"*

## What evolution cannot give you
Ask one question at a time and wait for the answer.
- **A beginning.** Something does not come from nothing. *"Where did matter, energy and the laws they follow come from?"* (Gen 1:1; John 1:3)
- **Life.** Life has only ever been seen to come from life. Natural selection can only work on something already alive and reproducing, so it cannot explain the first living thing. *"How did the first life arise, and has anyone ever seen it happen?"*
- **Information.** DNA is a code: instructions, stored and read. Everywhere else we find a code, we know a mind wrote it. *"Have you ever seen a message write itself?"* (Ps 139:13–16)
- **Kinds.** We see change within kinds: dog breeds, finch beaks, bacteria that resist a drug. They are still dogs, finches and bacteria. That is variation in what God made "after their kind." It is not one kind turning into another. *"Is that change you've watched, or change you've been told happened?"* (Gen 1:21; Gen 1:24–25)
- **A mind you can trust.** If your brain is the product of survival and not of truth, you have no reason to trust its conclusions, including evolution. Darwin felt this. He wrote of his "horrid doubt" whether the convictions of a mind developed from lower animals "are of any value or at all trustworthy" (letter to William Graham, July 3, 1881). *"Why trust a brain that was shaped only to survive?"* See [Worldviews](/topic/worldviews-how-do-you-know).
- **Right and wrong.** If we are animals shaped by survival of the fittest, there is no "ought." The strong removing the weak is simply how the process works. He does not live that way, because he is made in God's image. *"If evolution is true, what makes cruelty wrong?"* (Gen 9:6; Rom 2:14–15)
- **Worth and purpose.** An accident has no purpose and no worth beyond what it can do. Every person you meet knows better. (Ps 100:3; Eccl 3:11; Col 1:16)

## Not enough time (Haldane's dilemma)
This argument comes from evolution's own side. Eric Hovind (Creation Today) and John Harris (Living Waters Europe) take it to college campuses, and Walter ReMine set it out in *The Biotic Message* (1993).
- **The man.** J. B. S. Haldane was one of the founders of population genetics and a committed evolutionist. In 1957 he published "The Cost of Natural Selection."
- **The cost.** For a helpful mutation to spread to a whole population, those without it have to die out, generation after generation. A population can only afford so many of those deaths at once. Haldane's estimate: about one beneficial change fixed every 300 generations.
- **The time.** Give evolution 10 million years from an ape-like ancestor to man, which is more than evolutionists ask for. At 20 years a generation that is 500,000 generations.
- **The total.** 500,000 ÷ 300 is about 1,667 beneficial changes. That is all natural selection can afford.
- **The gap.** Human and chimp DNA were long said to be 1% different. In a genome of 3 billion letters that is 30 million differences. A 2025 comparison of complete ape genomes found that around 15% of the two genomes does not line up one-to-one (*Nature*, Yoo et al.). Either way the gap is millions of letters, and selection can pay for fewer than two thousand.
- **Even with every allowance.** Suppose one beneficial change were fixed every single generation, 300 times faster than Haldane found. You would still have only 500,000.
**Ask:** *"Can 1,667 changes turn an ape-like creature into a man who speaks, writes, builds and worships?"*

What he will say back:
- **"That was solved. Kimura showed most changes are neutral."** → Look at what that answer gives up. In 1968 Motoo Kimura agreed that selection could not account for that many changes, so he proposed that most of them are neutral and spread by chance (drift). Neutral means it helps nothing. *"Then what built the human brain, speech and upright walking: selection, which is too slow, or chance, which selects nothing?"*
- **"Not every difference has to be beneficial, and the differences are split between two lines."** → Fine. Take away as many as he likes. The question is whether fewer than two thousand useful changes can make a man.
- **"Haldane's math is from 1957."** → Haldane expected it to be revised, and evolutionists have been revising it ever since. *"What is the number today, and does it get you from thousands to millions?"*
- **"One study can't overturn hundreds."** → Truth is not counted by hands. One sound measurement can overturn a consensus, and it has before. *"If the numbers are right, would it matter how many people believed otherwise?"*
- **"I'm not a biologist. I can't answer that."** → Thank him for the honest answer and don't press. *"Would you look into it? And if evolution can't do it, are you willing to ask who made you?"*

## Why creation is true
- **God says so, and He was there.** Scripture is the standard, not the thing on trial. (Gen 1:31; Ex 20:11; Ps 33:6; Ps 33:9; Neh 9:6)
- **Jesus says so.** He treated Genesis as history: God made them male and female "from the beginning of creation," not after ages of animal ancestry. (Mark 10:6; Matt 19:4–5; Luke 11:50–51)
- **Creation says so.** What God made shows His power and nature so clearly that everyone is without excuse. (Rom 1:20; Ps 19:1–4; Isa 45:18)
- **The gospel depends on it.** Adam was a real man (Luke 3:38). Through that one man sin entered the world, and death through sin (Rom 5:12). Christ is the last Adam, who undoes what the first Adam did (1 Cor 15:21–22; 1 Cor 15:45). If death was here for ages before Adam, death is not the wages of sin, and the cross answers a problem that was never real.

## Can't God have used evolution?
Some Christians say so to keep the peace. It costs too much.
- It puts death, disease and suffering before sin, in a world God called "very good." (Gen 1:31; Rom 5:12; Rom 8:20–22)
- It turns Adam into a symbol, when Luke, Paul and Jesus treat him as a man. (Luke 3:38; Rom 5:14; 1 Cor 15:45)
- It makes the days mean something the fourth commandment does not allow: Israel worked six days and rested one because God did. (Ex 20:9–11)
- It lets the changing opinions of men correct the word of God. (Isa 40:8; Mark 7:13)
- It calls good what God calls an enemy. If death built us, death is the tool of creation. Scripture says death is "the last enemy" that Christ will abolish. (1 Cor 15:26; Rev 21:4)
- It gives you a different God. An evolutionist saw this plainly. Philosopher David Hull wrote that the God implied by evolution is "careless, wasteful, indifferent, almost diabolical," not the sort of God anyone would pray to (*Nature*, August 8, 1991). The God of the Bible made a world without death, and sin ruined it. (Gen 1:31; Rom 8:20–22)

## If he says
- **"Evolution is a proven fact. All scientists agree."** → A count of hands is not a proof, and the majority has been wrong before. *"Which part has been observed: change within a kind, or one kind becoming another?"*
- **"Small changes add up. Give it millions of years."** → That is the claim, not the evidence. What we observe is variation inside a kind, using information already there. *"Has anyone watched the small changes add up to a new kind, or is that the part you take on trust?"* And see the time problem above.
- **"The fossils show it."** → Fossils show that things died and were buried, most of them suddenly, in water-laid rock, all over the earth. That fits a worldwide flood. A fossil does not come with a date or a list of its children. And soft tissue, including flexible vessels and what look like cells, has been recovered from dinosaur bone (Mary Schweitzer, *Science*, 2005). *"How does that last 65 million years?"* (Gen 7:19–23; 2 Pet 3:5–6)
- **"We share 98% of our DNA with chimps."** → Similar design points to one Designer as easily as to one ancestor. A builder uses the same materials in different houses. And the differences that remain are the difference between an ape and a man who writes symphonies and prays. (Gen 1:26–27)
- **"Radiometric dating proves billions of years."** → The measurement is of what is in the rock today. The age is a calculation that assumes how much was there at the start, that nothing was added or lost, and that the rate never changed. No one was there to check any of the three. Creation researchers also report carbon-14 in coal and diamonds said to be millions of years old, where none should be left after about 100,000 years (the RATE project, 2005). *"How do you know the starting amount?"*
- **"That's religion, not science."** → Both are claims about a past no one can repeat. And modern science grew up among men who expected order because they believed in a Lawgiver: Kepler, Boyle, Newton, Faraday, Maxwell. *"Why should a universe that came from nothing obey laws at all?"*
- **"You're using God to fill the gaps."** → No. We reason from what we do know: codes come from minds, and life comes from life. He is the one filling the gaps, with time and chance. *"Is 'given enough time, it happened' an observation or a hope?"*
- **"With infinite time, or infinite universes, anything can happen."** → No one has observed another universe, so that is faith, not science. And time cannot be infinite into the past. Picture an endless row of falling dominoes: if an infinite number had to fall before yours, yours would never fall. We have reached today, so there was a first day. (Gen 1:1)
- **"Then who made God?"** → Whatever begins needs a cause. God has no beginning. He is not one more thing inside the universe that needs explaining. He is the reason there is anything to explain. (Ps 90:2; Isa 40:28)
- **"Genesis is poetry."** → Genesis 1 is written as Hebrew narrative, with numbered days, evening and morning. Moses, Jesus and Paul all read it as history. (Ex 20:11; Mark 10:6; Rom 5:12–14)

## Watch out
- **Don't say "there's no evidence for evolution."** He will show you evidence of change within kinds and think he has won. Say: same evidence, different starting point.
- **Don't say "it's only a theory," "why are there still monkeys?" or "the second law of thermodynamics disproves it."** Creation ministries themselves tell Christians to drop these. They are easy to answer and cost you the hearing.
- **Don't repeat stories you can't source**, such as Darwin recanting on his deathbed. He did not.
- **Use Haldane's argument in its strong form.** Say "about 1,667 beneficial changes," not "30 million changes times 20 years is 600 million years." The short version treats every difference as a beneficial change in one line of descent, and anyone who has read about it will answer that most differences are neutral and are shared between two lines. Then you are defending your arithmetic and not making your point.
- **Don't say "no one has ever answered it."** They have answered, by giving up natural selection as the main cause. Say that.
- **Say where the 15% comes from.** It is the share of the two genomes that does not line up one-to-one in the 2025 comparison. He may say it is counted differently; the 1% figure makes the argument just as well.
- **Don't make it a contest.** No bets, no crowd, no gloating when he has no answer. You want him, not the win. (Col 4:5–6)
- **Don't make it the main thing.** A man can give up evolution and still be lost. The aim is the Creator he answers to, and the Savior he needs. (Acts 17:24–31)
- **Stay kind.** Many have never heard evolution questioned. Ask, don't mock. (2 Tim 2:24–26; 1 Pet 3:15)

## Bring it to the gospel
The One who made him is the One he has sinned against. The Creator entered His own creation, died for sinners and rose again, and He now commands all people everywhere to repent. (John 1:3; John 1:14; Col 1:16; Col 1:19–20; Acts 17:30–31)
**Ask:** *"If you were made, you belong to Someone. Are you ready to meet Him?"*

Then give [The Gospel](/topic/the-gospel). For the short answer to use live, open [the evolution answer](/atheist/objection/evolution-explains-life-we-dont).

# The abolition of abortion
> You shall not murder.

The child in the womb is a human being made in the image of God, and worth exactly what you are worth. That is why abortion is not to be regulated or reduced but ended: the protection every born person has belongs to the unborn too. This page is about the value of that child. It rests on Scripture, and it is brought to people with the gospel. (Ex 20:13; Gen 1:27; Gen 9:6; Prov 24:11–12)

## What you are showing
The whole question turns on one thing: what is the unborn? If it is not a human being, no justification for abortion is needed. If it is a human being, no justification is enough. Scripture and plain observation give the same answer: from conception this is a living human being, a neighbor, and God's image bearer. (Gen 1:27; Ps 139:13–16)
**Ask:** *"What is it? If it is a human being, what would justify killing it?"*

## A human being from conception
- **This is biology, not opinion.** When a sperm fertilizes an egg, a new, living, distinct human being exists, with his own DNA. The embryology textbooks used in universities, such as *Langman's Medical Embryology*, begin human development at fertilization.
- **Zygote, embryo, fetus, infant, toddler, teenager: these are stages, not kinds.** Each names how far along a human being is, the way "toddler" and "teenager" do. Nothing becomes human by growing. He is human, and he grows. "Fetus" is Latin for offspring.
- **Only four things differ between him and you.** Pastor Jeff Durbin puts it this way: size, level of development, environment and degree of dependence. *"When do we kill people because of their size?"* A seven-year-old is not fully developed. A newborn is wholly dependent. Each of us will be dependent again if we live long enough.
- **You were once exactly that.** Every person you will ever meet was once a human embryo. *"You are here because your mother did not choose otherwise. Why wouldn't we do for them what we would want done for us?"* (Matt 7:12)

## What God says about the unborn
- **God forms each one.** He knits the child together in the womb and writes its days before one of them exists. (Ps 139:13–16; Job 31:15; Isa 44:24)
- **God knows them as persons.** He knew Jeremiah before he was born. John leaped in the womb at the presence of the unborn Christ. Scripture uses the same word for the baby in the womb and the baby in the manger. (Jer 1:5; Luke 1:41–44; Luke 2:12)
- **God became one.** The Son of God took on flesh at conception, not at birth. (Luke 1:31; Luke 1:35)
- **Children are a gift, not a burden to be removed.** (Ps 127:3)
- **He is the fatherless child God tells us to defend.** A fatherless child is one with no one to speak for him and no one to protect him. Durbin: there is "no more fatherless child in this world" than the one abandoned in the womb. God told His own people that He would not hear their prayers while their hands were full of blood, and then said: seek justice, correct oppression, bring justice to the fatherless. The promise that scarlet sins will be white as snow comes in the very next breath. (Isa 1:15–18)
- **He is your neighbor.** Love your neighbor as yourself. (Mark 12:31; Luke 10:36–37)

## What God says about shedding innocent blood
- **"You shall not murder."** (Ex 20:13)
- **The image of God is why murder is a capital matter.** (Gen 9:6)
- **God hates hands that shed innocent blood.** (Prov 6:16–17)
- **Sacrificing children brings guilt on a whole land.** Israel offered its sons and daughters, and the land was polluted with blood. (Ps 106:37–38; Lev 18:21; Jer 7:31)
- **The law of Moses protected the child in the womb.** Harm to the child was answered life for life. (Ex 21:22–25)

## Why abolition, and not regulation
- **God does not ask a nation to sin less.** He says to stop, and to do justice. (Isa 1:16–17; Amos 5:15; Amos 5:24)
- **You would accept it nowhere else.** No one would praise a law that allowed slavery so long as the quarters were clean, or trafficking so long as the victims were over sixteen. A law that says a child may be killed before so many weeks, or must be buried properly afterward, is the same kind of law. Durbin: "You don't change it through compromise, you change it through the truth." We may not do evil that good may come. (Rom 3:8)

## The same protection
Everything the law does for a child after birth, it should do for him before birth, and for the same reason: he is one of us. We do not ask for a special law for the unborn. We ask that they no longer be left out of the law that already protects everyone else. (Lev 19:15; Prov 31:8–9; Ps 82:3–4)

## If the child would suffer
This is the argument you will hear most: he will be poor, sick, unwanted, born to addicts, or live only minutes. It says that because a child may suffer, it is kinder to kill him first. It does not hold.
- **We never reason this way about anyone else.** We do not kill the homeless man, the addict or the veteran on the street because their lives are hard. We help them. *"You know what you can't do to someone who is suffering? Kill them."*
- **Move the child one day past birth.** A mother learns on the day her son is born that his father is going to prison and his life will be hard. May she kill him to spare him? If not, what changed except which side of the womb he is on?
- **No one can see a child's future.** Durbin's son Augustine was diagnosed in the womb with severe spina bifida, and his birth mother said no one would want him now. A Christian friend stood between her and the abortion clinic and found a family. He was born whole.

- **The answer to a hard life is help.** That is where the church comes in: with a home, with money, with adoption. (James 1:27; Ps 68:5–6; Gal 6:2)
- **Suffering is not the last word.** God gives life, numbers its days and can redeem any of them. (Ps 139:16; John 9:1–3; Rom 8:18)

# MacArthur on Rome
> What John MacArthur says about Roman Catholicism, in his own words.

These lines come from John MacArthur's sermons on the Pope and the papacy, the Mass, the priesthood, Galatians 5 and the idolatry of Mary worship (Grace to You). Use them to know where our teachers stand and why. With a Catholic friend, open the Bible first and ask questions; these are for your own conviction and for when someone asks, "Is it really that serious?"

## His heart
He begins with grief, not anger: "We find no delight in people who are condemned to hell." And he ends with a plea: "People need to be rescued from this system." (Ezek 33:11; Rom 10:1; 2 Tim 2:24–26)

## The system
MacArthur calls Roman Catholicism "an apostate, corrupt, heretical, false Christianity" and "a front for the kingdom of Satan." On adding works to faith he says, "There is no room for any alteration of the gospel." Of Rome and Eastern Orthodoxy together: "It is not to be joined, it is to be cursed." He is using Paul's word, anathema. (Gal 1:6–9; Gal 5:2–4; 2 Cor 11:13–15; 2 Pet 2:1)
- **Backing:** Trent placed its curse on justification by faith alone [Sess. 6, can. 9, 12, 14] and has never withdrawn it. Paul put his curse on any gospel that adds to faith.

## The Mass
He calls transubstantiation "mystical hocus-pocus, mumbo-jumbo for sure," and says that phrase came from the Latin words of the Mass, *hoc est corpus meum*. He says the Mass is paid for: "You pay for a Mass. That's how the coffers of the Catholic Church are filled." And he reminds us that English believers were burned alive for denying it. (Heb 9:25–28; Heb 10:10–14; Heb 10:18)
- **Backing:** Rome's own law lets a priest accept an offering to apply a Mass for a named intention [Code of Canon Law, can. 945]. Trent calls the Mass propitiatory for the living and the dead [Sess. 22, can. 3].
- **Backing:** Archbishop John Tillotson gave the same origin for "hocus-pocus" in 1694. Rest the argument on Hebrews, and use the phrase as MacArthur's.
- **Backing:** Foxe's *Book of Martyrs* records about 280 people burned under Mary Tudor (1555–1558), many of them for rejecting the Mass.

## Mary
"Pagan goddess worship dressed up in Roman Catholic fantasy." He traces it to the old mother-and-child cults (Ishtar, Astarte, Isis) and to the "queen of heaven" whom God judged Judah for worshiping. On "Mother of God" he says, "God was never born." On the claim that we reach Christ through Mary: "This is a Satanic fabrication." On Co-Redemptrix: not formal dogma, but "unofficial reality." (Jer 7:18; Jer 44:17–19; Luke 1:46–48; Luke 11:27–28; 1 Tim 2:5; Isa 42:8)
- **Backing:** He quotes Rome's own writers, above all Alphonsus de Liguori's *The Glories of Mary*, which Rome has never condemned. Liguori is a canonized saint and a Doctor of the Church.
- **Backing:** The Catechism still calls Mary Advocate, Helper, Benefactress and Mediatrix and says she was exalted "as Queen over all things" [CCC 966, 968–969]. The 2025 Vatican note set aside a title. It removed no doctrine.

## Purgatory and indulgences
"Purgatory is what makes the whole system work." He says Rome invented it, that it is a second chance after death, and that indulgences are forgiveness for sale: the treasury of merit can be drawn on, and "you can buy it." (Heb 1:3; Heb 9:27; Luke 23:43; Acts 8:20)
- **Backing:** Rome still teaches the treasury of merit and indulgences for the living and the dead [CCC 1471–1479], and still grants them for acts such as passing through a Holy Door in a Jubilee year (2000, 2025). The open sale of indulgences is what Luther attacked in 1517.

## The Pope
He says the titles are stolen. "Holy Father" is what Jesus called God. Head of the church belongs to Christ. And Vicar of Christ, the one who stands in Christ's place: "He has stolen that from the Holy Spirit." He says there is no evidence in Scripture that Peter was ever in Rome. He reads out the papacy's record of immorality and wealth and calls its display "theater." On Antichrist he quotes Luther, Calvin, Knox, the Puritans and Spurgeon, then gives his own view: the Pope is not the final Antichrist, but "the very embodiment of antichrist" in this age, and it is "not a bad guess to see the final Antichrist as a pope." (John 17:11; Matt 23:9; Col 1:18; John 16:7; 1 John 2:18; 2 Thess 2:3–4)
- **Backing:** This was the common Protestant confession. Luther's Smalcald Articles (II.4), the Westminster Confession (25.6) and the 1689 London Baptist Confession (26.4) all name the Pope as "that Antichrist, that man of sin."
- **Backing:** Our church teaches a future, personal Antichrist. MacArthur's wording fits that: many antichrists now (1 John 2:18), the final one still to come.

## The priesthood
On required celibacy he goes to 1 Timothy 4: "Those who forbid marriage are advocating a doctrine of demons." He ties the scandals of the priesthood to that rule and to a priesthood the New Testament never created. (1 Tim 4:1–3; 1 Tim 3:2; 1 Cor 9:5; 1 Pet 2:9)

## How it began
"What was born with Constantine was Christendom rather than Christianity." He says the empire made one official religion, pagan practice came in with the crowds, and true believers were there through every century, protesting.
- **Dates to know:** Constantine legalized Christianity in 313 and called the Council of Nicaea in 325. Theodosius made it the state religion in 380. The papal claims grew after that: Leo I (440–461), then Gregory VII and Boniface VIII in the Middle Ages.

# How to pray
> Before, during and after every conversation

No argument opens a blind eye. God does, and He has told us to ask. This page is a short school of prayer: how Daniel prayed, the pattern Jesus gave, and what to pray for the people you talk to. (Dan 9:1–3; Matt 6:9–13; Rom 10:1; Col 4:2–4)

## Why pray, if God is sovereign?
- **Because He is.** Daniel set his face "to the Lord God," the Master of everything. Abner Chou: "because God is sovereign you should pray" [Abner Chou, sermon on Daniel 9:1–3]. You bring your need to the only One with the power to meet it. (Dan 9:3; Dan 4:35; Eph 1:11)
- **Because He commands it and uses it.** God ordains the means as well as the end. The prayer of a righteous man accomplishes much, and you do not have because you do not ask. (James 5:16; James 4:2; Matt 7:7–11)
- **Because salvation is His work.** Paul's heart's desire and prayer to God for Israel was for their salvation. He planted and Apollos watered, but God caused the growth. (Rom 10:1; 1 Cor 3:6–7; John 6:44; Acts 16:14)
**Ask:** *"Who am I asking God to save, by name?"*

## Learn from Daniel (Daniel 9:1–3)
Daniel's prayer in chapter 9 is one of the great prayers of the Bible, and the first three verses show how he came to it. These three points are from Abner Chou's sermon on the passage.
- **Pray according to God's providence (verse 1).** Daniel opens with a date and a king who "was made king." He read his own circumstances as God's hand: carrying out His word, in control, and caring for His servant. If you see God at work in every ordinary thing, you will never run out of reasons to pray, and praying without ceasing stops being a puzzle. (Dan 9:1; Gen 22:14; Rom 8:28; 1 Thess 5:17)
- **Pray according to God's purpose (verse 2).** Daniel prayed with the scroll of Jeremiah open. He studied humbly, carefully and across the books, noticed a single detail, the seventy years, submitted to it, and then asked God to do what God had said. The most powerful prayer is the one that agrees with God's will, and you learn His will in His word. (Dan 9:2; Jer 29:10–12; 1 John 5:14–15; John 15:7)
- **Pray knowing what prayer is (verse 3).** "I gave my face to the Lord God to seek Him." Prayer is set, undistracted attention on God Himself, not a speech about ourselves. Daniel came with fasting, sackcloth and ashes: so taken up with God that food could wait, grieved over sin, and aware of what he deserved. (Dan 9:3; Ps 27:8; Matt 6:6; Isa 66:2)
- **Keep at it.** By Chou's reckoning Daniel had been praying about these things for about twelve years, from the vision of chapter 7 to the answer of chapter 9. Jesus told a parable to show that we ought always to pray and not lose heart. (Dan 9:20–23; Luke 18:1; Col 4:2; Gal 6:9)

## What Daniel actually said (Daniel 9:4–19)
- **He began with who God is.** Great and awesome, keeping His covenant and His steadfast love toward those who love Him. (Dan 9:4)
- **He confessed sin plainly and included himself.** He piles up the words: sinned, done wrong, acted wickedly, rebelled, turned aside, not listened. No excuses, and he says "we." (Dan 9:5–6; Dan 9:20)
- **He agreed that God was right.** "To You, O Lord, belongs righteousness, but to us open shame." (Dan 9:7; Dan 9:14)
- **He asked on the ground of mercy, not merit.** He says outright that he is not asking because of any righteousness of his own, but because of God's great compassion. Every request is a request for grace, so there is no room for demanding and none for sulking when God says no. (Dan 9:18)
- **He asked for God's sake.** Hear, forgive, act, and do not delay, because Your city and Your people are called by Your name. The reason he gives is God's own honor. (Dan 9:17; Dan 9:19)
**Ask:** *"Is my prayer mostly about God, or mostly about me?"*

## The pattern Jesus gave (Matthew 6:9–13)
Jesus said, "Pray, then, in this way." It is a pattern to pray through, not only words to repeat. God comes first, then our needs.
- **"Our Father who is in heaven."** Come as a child to a Father, through the Son. (Matt 6:9; Rom 8:15; Heb 4:16)
- **"Hallowed be Your name."** Worship first. Ask that He be honored in you and in the person you will speak to. (Ps 115:1; John 12:28)
- **"Your kingdom come."** Ask Him to save sinners and to send His Son. (Matt 9:37–38; Rev 22:20)
- **"Your will be done."** Lay your plans down. Jesus prayed this way in the garden. (Matt 26:39; James 4:15)
- **"Give us this day our daily bread."** Ask for what you need today, and thank Him for it. (Phil 4:6; Matt 6:31–33)
- **"Forgive us our debts, as we also have forgiven our debtors."** Keep short accounts with God and hold nothing against anyone. (1 John 1:9; Mark 11:25; Eph 4:32)
- **"Do not lead us into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one."** Ask to be kept. You are not strong enough alone. (Matt 26:41; 1 Cor 10:13; Eph 6:10–11)

## Before you speak to someone
Pray for them:
- **That God would open their eyes and heart.** (2 Cor 4:4–6; Acts 16:14; Acts 26:18)
- **That He would grant repentance.** (2 Tim 2:25–26; Acts 11:18)
- **That He would draw them to Christ.** (John 6:44; Rom 10:1)
Pray for yourself:
- **For boldness.** The apostles prayed for it after being threatened, and Paul asked churches to pray it for him. (Acts 4:29–31; Eph 6:18–20)
- **For clear words and an open door.** (Col 4:2–4)
- **For wisdom, gentleness and love.** (James 1:5; Col 4:5–6; 2 Tim 2:24–25; 1 Pet 3:15)
- **For clean hands.** Confess your own sin first. (Ps 139:23–24; Ps 51:10–13)
**Say:** *"Father, You alone give sight. Open [name]'s eyes to see Christ. Give me Your words, courage to say them and love for the one who hears. For Your name's sake. Amen."*

## While you are talking
- **Pray short, silent prayers.** Nehemiah prayed between a king's question and his own answer. "Lord, help me." "Lord, open his heart." (Neh 2:4–5; Ps 46:1)
- **Offer to pray for them.** Many people who will not debate will let you pray for a real need. Pray aloud, briefly, to the Father in Jesus' name, and let them hear the gospel in how you pray. (1 Tim 2:1–4; James 5:16)

## Afterward
- **Give thanks, whatever happened.** The results are His. (1 Cor 3:6–7; 1 Thess 5:18)
- **Keep praying for them by name.** Write it down in your [notes](/notes). (Col 1:9; Luke 18:1)
- **Ask God to send others to water.** (Matt 9:37–38; John 4:37–38)
- **Don't carry what is God's to carry.** Cast your anxiety on Him. (1 Pet 5:6–7; Isa 55:10–11)

## What gets in the way
- **Cherished sin.** The psalmist says that if he had held on to wickedness in his heart, the Lord would not have heard. (Ps 66:18; Isa 59:1–2)
- **Wrong motives.** James says we ask and do not receive when we ask in order to spend it on our own pleasures. (James 4:3)
- **Praying to be seen, or piling up words.** (Matt 6:5–8)
- **Unforgiveness.** (Mark 11:25; Matt 6:14–15)
- **Dishonoring your wife.** Peter says it hinders a husband's prayers. (1 Pet 3:7)
- **Doubting God's goodness.** Ask in faith. (James 1:5–7; Heb 11:6)
- **Simply not asking.** (James 4:2)

## If you don't know what to say
- **Pray Scripture.** Open a psalm or one of Paul's prayers and turn it into your own words. (Ps 51; Eph 1:15–19; Eph 3:14–21; Phil 1:9–11; Col 1:9–12)
- **The Spirit helps.** He intercedes for us when we do not know how to pray as we should. (Rom 8:26–27)
- **Christ intercedes.** He always lives to make intercession for those who draw near to God through Him. (Heb 7:25; Rom 8:34)
- **Come boldly anyway.** The throne is a throne of grace. (Heb 4:14–16)

- **Watchtower / Governing Body:** the leadership that directs all Witness teaching and calls itself the "faithful and discreet slave."
- **Active force:** their term for the Holy Spirit, whom they say is not a person.
- **Kingdom Interlinear:** the Watchtower's own Greek and English New Testament. Useful because it prints the Greek with a word-for-word English line next to their NWT.
- **The 144,000:** the "anointed" who they say go to heaven. Everyone else hopes for life on a paradise earth.

## Unitarian
- **Biblical Unitarian:** holds that God is one person, the Father, and Jesus is a human Messiah.
- **Arian:** holds that the Son is God's first and greatest creature.
- **Shaliach (agency):** the Jewish idea that a sent agent stands for the one who sent him. They use it to explain away texts where Jesus speaks as God.
- **Oneness / modalism:** God is one Person who shows Himself as Father, Son and Spirit (as roles or modes, not three Persons). Oneness Pentecostals say that one Person is Jesus.
- **Homoousios:** "of the same essence," the Nicene term for the Son's relation to the Father.
- **Malak / angelos:** Hebrew and Greek for "messenger." It names a job, not a nature.

## Mormon
- **Godhead:** for them, three separate beings who are one in purpose.
- **Exaltation:** the teaching that faithful people can become gods.
- **Great Apostasy:** their claim that the true church was lost after the apostles.
- **Restoration:** their claim that Joseph Smith restored the church.
- **Standard works:** their four books of scripture: the Bible, the Book of Mormon, the Doctrine and Covenants (D&C), and the Pearl of Great Price.
- **JST:** the Joseph Smith Translation, his revision of the Bible.
- **Testimony / burning in the bosom:** an inner feeling taken as God's confirmation that the church is true.
- **King Follett discourse:** Joseph Smith's 1844 sermon teaching that God was once a man.

## Muslim
- **Tawhid:** the absolute oneness of Allah.
- **Shirk:** associating anything with Allah. The one sin Allah will not forgive if a person dies in it (Q 4:48).
- **Isa:** the Quran's name for Jesus.
- **Tawrat, Zabur, Injil:** the Torah, the Psalms and the Gospel, which the Quran says Allah revealed.
- **Tahrif:** the claim that the earlier Scriptures were corrupted.
- **Hadith:** reports of what Muhammad said and did.
- **Surah / ayah:** a chapter and a verse of the Quran.
- **Tafsir:** commentary on the Quran.
- **Hafs, Warsh, Qalun:** different transmitted readings of the Quran.

## Roman Catholic
- **Magisterium:** the Church's teaching office (the pope and the bishops in communion with him), which alone can authentically interpret Scripture and Tradition [CCC 85, 100].
- **Sacred Tradition:** teaching handed down outside Scripture and held with "equal" reverence [CCC 82].
- **Ex cathedra:** the pope defining faith or morals "from the chair," held to be infallible and "irreformable" [Vatican I].
- **Transubstantiation:** the whole substance of bread and wine becomes Christ's body and blood, "truly, really, and substantially" [Trent, Sess. 13, can. 1; CCC 1376].
- **Latria / dulia / hyperdulia:** worship due to God alone / honor to saints / the higher honor given to Mary.
- **Infused vs. imputed righteousness:** Rome says God makes us "inwardly just" [CCC 1992]. Scripture says God credits Christ's righteousness to us (Rom 4:6; 2 Cor 5:21).
- **Mortal sin:** grave sin that destroys sanctifying grace and, if unrepented, leads to hell [CCC 1861].
- **Purgatory:** final purification after death for those who die in grace "but still imperfectly purified" [CCC 1030–1031].
- **Indulgence:** remission of temporal punishment for sins already forgiven, applicable to oneself or to souls in purgatory [CCC 1471, 1498].
- **Deuterocanonical books:** what Rome calls the Apocrypha: Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, 1–2 Maccabees, and additions to Esther and Daniel [Trent, Sess. 4].
- **Judaizers:** teachers in the New Testament who believed in Jesus and said Gentiles must also be circumcised and keep the Law of Moses to be saved (Acts 15:1, 5). Galatians was written against them.
- **Necessary vs. sufficient:** Rome says grace, faith and Scripture are necessary. The Reformation said each is also enough: Scripture alone, grace alone, faith alone, Christ alone.
- **Unam Sanctam:** the 1302 bull of Boniface VIII that defined submission to the Roman Pontiff as necessary for salvation.

## Jewish
- **Tanakh:** The Hebrew Scriptures, our Old Testament: Torah (Law), Nevi'im (Prophets) and Ketuvim (Writings). Same books, different order.
- **Torah:** The five books of Moses. Also used loosely for all Jewish teaching.
- **Talmud:** The written collection of rabbinic discussion (Mishnah and Gemara), finished centuries after Jesus. Orthodox Jews treat it as the authoritative "oral Torah."
- **Mashiach:** Messiah, "anointed one." In Judaism, a human king from David still to come.
- **Shema:** "Hear, O Israel" (Deut 6:4), the daily confession that God is one.
- **Hashem:** "The Name." How observant Jews refer to God rather than pronounce His name.
- **Teshuvah:** Repentance, literally "return." With prayer and charity, the rabbinic answer to how sin is forgiven.
- **Yom Kippur:** The Day of Atonement (Lev 16). Today kept with fasting and prayer, without the sacrifices Leviticus commands.
- **Haftarah:** The set reading from the Prophets in synagogue each week. Isaiah 53 is not among the readings.
- **Targum:** An ancient Aramaic paraphrase of the Hebrew Scriptures read in synagogues.
- **Rashi:** Rabbi Shlomo Yitzchaki (died 1105), the most widely read Jewish commentator. He made "the servant is Israel" the standard reading of Isaiah 53.
- **Yeshua:** The Hebrew name of Jesus. Jewish believers in Him often use it.

## Progressive Christian
- **Progressive Christianity:** A movement that keeps Christian language while revising or dropping historic doctrine. Today's form of the theological liberalism Machen answered in 1923.
- **Deconstruction:** Taking apart the beliefs one was raised with. For some it ends in a truer faith; for many it ends in leaving the faith.
- **Red-letter Christian:** One who claims to follow Jesus' own words (printed in red in some Bibles) over the rest of Scripture.
- **Penal substitution:** Christ bore the penalty of God's wrath in the place of sinners. The heart of the gospel, and the doctrine progressives most often reject.
- **"Cosmic child abuse":** A slogan against penal substitution, from Steve Chalke's *The Lost Message of Jesus* (2003).
- **Universalism:** Everyone will be saved in the end, whatever they believed.
- **Affirming / inclusive:** Approving same-sex relationships and transgender identities as good, and treating disagreement as harm.
- **Social gospel:** Salvation understood as the reform of society rather than the forgiveness of sinners.
- **Lived experience / "my truth":** Personal experience treated as an authority that Scripture may not overrule.

## Hindu
- **Brahman:** The one ultimate reality behind everything. Impersonal in some schools, personal in others.
- **Atman:** The true self or soul, said to be one with Brahman.
- **Samsara:** The cycle of birth, death and rebirth.
- **Karma:** The moral weight of deeds that carries into the next life. Not grace: you get what you earned.
- **Moksha:** Release from the cycle of rebirth. The Hindu idea of salvation.
- **Avatar:** A god coming down in visible form, such as Krishna or Rama as forms of Vishnu. Not the same as the incarnation.
- **Bhakti:** The path of loving devotion to a chosen god.
- **Puja:** Worship offered to an image or deity, at home or in a temple.
- **Guru:** A spiritual teacher, often treated as a channel of the divine.

## Buddhist
- **Dukkha:** Suffering or unsatisfactoriness. The first Noble Truth: life is marked by it.
- **Four Noble Truths:** Suffering; its cause (craving); its end; and the path to its end.
- **Eightfold Path:** The Buddha's program of right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration.
- **Anatta:** "No self." The teaching that there is no permanent soul.
- **Nirvana:** "Blowing out." The end of craving and rebirth. Not heaven.
- **Dharma:** The Buddha's teaching, or the way things are.
- **Merit:** Good karma stored up by good deeds, giving and ritual.
- **Bodhisattva:** In Mahayana Buddhism, one who delays nirvana to help others reach it.
- **Pure Land:** A branch that trusts Amida Buddha to bring the believer to his paradise. The nearest thing in Buddhism to grace.

## New Age
- **The Universe:** An impersonal stand-in for God: a force or consciousness said to guide and provide.
- **Manifestation / law of attraction:** The belief that your thoughts and "energy" bring things into being.
- **Vibration / frequency:** Spiritual "levels." Sin is renamed low vibration.
- **Christ consciousness:** A supposed enlightened state Jesus reached and anyone can. Not the biblical Christ.
- **Spirit guides:** Spirits consulted for direction. Scripture forbids it (Deut 18:10–12).
- **Channeling / mediumship:** Receiving messages from spirits or the dead.
- **Divination:** Seeking hidden knowledge through tarot, astrology, numerology and the like.
- **Higher self:** The idea that your true inner self is divine and all-knowing.
- **Ascended masters:** Supposed enlightened beings, with Jesus listed as one of them.
- **Monism / pantheism:** All is one; all is God.

## Satanist
- **LaVeyan Satanism:** The Church of Satan, founded by Anton LaVey in 1966. Atheistic: Satan is a symbol of pride, appetite and self-rule.
- **The Satanic Temple:** A non-theistic activist group founded in 2013, known for its seven tenets and public protests. Not the same as the Church of Satan.
- **Theistic Satanism:** The minority who believe Satan is a real being and honor or worship him.
- **Luciferianism:** Honors Lucifer as a "light-bringer" and liberator rather than as evil. The serpent's side of Genesis 3.
- **Left-hand path:** An occult term for spiritualities that seek self-deification and break religious taboos.
- **Baphomet:** A goat-headed figure used as a symbol by both main Satanist groups.
- **Black mass:** A ritual that mocks Christian worship, especially the Roman Catholic Mass.
- **"Hail Satan":** A greeting or slogan. For most it is provocation, not prayer.

## Oneness Pentecostal
- **Oneness:** the teaching that God is one person who shows Himself as Father, Son and Holy Spirit, and whose name is Jesus.
- **Modalism (Sabellianism):** the ancient name for the same idea: one person in three modes or roles. The church rejected it in the third century.
- **Jesus' name baptism:** baptism with the words "in the name of Jesus Christ," held to be the only valid form and necessary for forgiveness.
- **Initial evidence:** speaking in tongues as the necessary first sign of receiving the Holy Spirit.
- **Apostolic:** what many Oneness churches call themselves, meaning they hold the doctrine and practice of the apostles in Acts.
- **Manifestation:** their word for Father, Son and Spirit, in place of "person."
- **UPCI:** the United Pentecostal Church International, the largest Oneness body.

## Eastern Orthodox
- **Holy Tradition:** "the doctrine of the faith, the law of God, the sacraments, and the ritual as handed down" [Philaret Q17]. It includes Scripture and the councils.
- **Theosis (deification):** sharing in the life of God as the goal of salvation [GOARCH, "Spirituality"]. Not sharing God's essence.
- **Synergy:** God's grace and human effort working together in salvation.
- **Theotokos:** "God-bearer," the title for Mary affirmed at Ephesus (431).
- **Ever-virgin:** Mary remained a virgin before, during and after Christ's birth [Philaret Q186].
- **Proskynesis vs. latreia:** veneration (bowing, kissing) given to icons and saints / worship given to God alone [Nicaea II].
- **Ancestral sin:** we inherit death and corruption from Adam, but not his guilt [OCA Q&A, "Original Sin"].
- **Essence / energies:** God's unknowable essence vs. His uncreated energies, in which believers participate (Gregory Palamas).
- **Toll houses:** a pious tradition about demonic testing of the soul after death; **not dogma**.
- **Dormition:** Mary's "falling asleep" (Aug. 15). Many Orthodox believe she was taken bodily to heaven, but the East never defined it as Rome did in 1950.
- **Chrismation:** anointing with holy oil (chrism) right after baptism, the Orthodox form of confirmation. It is also how most converts from other churches are received.
- **Infant communion:** baptized and chrismated infants receive communion [OCA, *The Orthodox Faith*, vol. 2].
- **Prayers for the departed:** prayers, alms and the liturgy offered for the dead, which Dositheus says deliver some souls from Hades [Decree 18].

## Atheist
- **Atheist:** does not believe in God. Some go further and say there is no God.
- **Agnostic:** says they don't know, or that no one can know.
- **Naturalism:** the view that nature is all there is.
- **Objective morality:** right and wrong that are true whatever anyone thinks.
- **Burden of proof:** whoever makes a claim has to back it up. "There is no God" is a claim.

## Words we use
- **Trinity:** one God in three Persons. See [The Trinity](/topic/the-trinity).
- **Two natures:** Jesus is one Person who is truly God and truly man.
- **Ransom:** the price that frees a captive. Scripture says no man can pay it for another and that God redeems. Jesus gave His life as the ransom.
- **Mediator:** the one who stands between God and men. Jesus is the one mediator because He is truly God and truly man.
- **Monogenes:** the unique, only begotten Son. It marks His unique, eternal relationship to the Father, never creation (LSB: "only begotten").
- **Prototokos:** Greek for "firstborn." In texts like Col 1:15 (cf. Ps 89:27) it means the preeminent heir, not the first one created.
- **Arche:** Greek for "beginning," "origin" or "ruler."
