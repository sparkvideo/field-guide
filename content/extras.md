# The Gospel
> Every conversation is headed here.

Winning the argument saves no one. Christ does. Say the whole message plainly, call for a response, and leave the results to God.

## Before you speak
- **God saves; you are faithful:** your job is to say the truth clearly. God gives the growth. (1 Cor 3:6–7; Rom 1:16; John 6:44)
- **Law before grace:** a person has to see their sin and God's judgment before the cross makes sense to them. (Rom 3:19–20; Gal 3:24)
- **Jesus is Lord:** the call is to repent and follow Christ, not to repeat a prayer or add Jesus to a life that stays the same. (Luke 9:23; Rom 10:9; Acts 20:21)
- **No false promises:** the gospel offers forgiveness and Christ Himself, not health, wealth or an easier life. (Phil 3:8; Mark 8:36; 2 Tim 3:12)

## God is holy
God created all things and made us for Himself. He is holy and just, He cannot overlook sin, and He will judge every person. (Gen 1:1; Isa 6:3; Hab 1:13; Acts 17:24–31; Heb 9:27)
**Ask:** *"What do you think happens after we die?"*

## We are sinners under judgment
Every one of us has broken God's law. We are not basically good people who slip up; we are dead in sin, and the wages of sin is death. No amount of good works can pay it off. (Rom 3:10–12; Rom 3:23; Rom 6:23; Eph 2:1–3; Isa 64:6)
**Ask:** *"Have you kept God's law perfectly?"*

## Christ died and rose
God the Son became man, lived the sinless life we have not lived, and died in the place of sinners, bearing the wrath they deserve. He was buried, rose bodily on the third day, and is Lord of all. (John 1:14; Isa 53:5–6; 2 Cor 5:21; 1 Pet 3:18; Rom 5:8; 1 Cor 15:3–4; Phil 2:9–11)
Only God can redeem a soul, and only a man can die in man's place, so the Savior is both. (Ps 49:7–9; Ps 49:15; 1 Tim 2:5–6; Heb 2:14–17)
**Ask:** *"Why did Jesus have to die?"*

## Repent and believe
God commands all people everywhere to repent. Whoever turns from sin and trusts in Christ alone is forgiven and has eternal life: by grace, through faith, not by works. Following Him costs everything, and no one who comes to Him is turned away. (Mark 1:15; Acts 17:30; John 3:16; Eph 2:8–9; Rom 10:9–13; Luke 14:27–28; John 6:37)
**Ask:** *"What is keeping you from turning to Christ today?"*

## The short version: the Romans Road
When you only have a minute, walk these in order, and call them to repent. (Rom 3:23; Rom 6:23; Rom 5:8; Acts 17:30; Rom 10:9–10; Rom 10:13; Rom 8:1)

## How to walk someone through it
For a step-by-step way to do this in a real conversation, using the Ten Commandments first and then the cross, open [The Way of the Master](/topic/the-way-of-the-master).

## If they respond
Pray with them. Get them a Bible and get them into a church that preaches it, where they can be baptized by immersion as a believer. Follow up. Don't pronounce them saved. Point them to Christ, and let assurance grow from following Him. (Acts 2:41–42; Matt 28:19–20; 2 Cor 13:5; 1 John 2:3–6; John 8:31)

## If they don't
Stay warm and leave the door open. You plant and water; God gives the growth. (1 Cor 3:6–7; 1 Pet 3:15–16; 2 Tim 2:24–26)

# The Way of the Master
> Use the law to reach the conscience, then give the gospel.

God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble (James 4:6), so: law to the proud, grace to the humble. The Ten Commandments work like a mirror: they show a person their sin so that the cross makes sense. Jesus did this with the rich young ruler. (Rom 3:19–20; Rom 7:7; Gal 3:24; Mark 10:17–22)

## 1. Start with an everyday question
Move from ordinary talk to spiritual things with one question.
**Ask:** *"What do you think happens after we die?"*

## 2. Are you a good person?
Almost everyone says yes. Let them say it. (Prov 20:6)
**Ask:** *"Would you consider yourself a good person?"*

## 3. Have you kept the Ten Commandments?
Walk through a few, one at a time, and let them answer each. (Ex 20:1–17)
- **Lying:** *"Have you ever told a lie? What does that make you?"*
- **Stealing:** *"Have you ever taken anything that wasn't yours, whatever its value?"*
- **God's name:** *"Have you ever used God's name as a curse word?"*
- **Lust:** Jesus said that looking with lust is adultery in the heart. *"Have you ever done that?"* (Matt 5:27–28)
- **Hatred:** Jesus put anger under the command "You shall not murder" (Matt 5:21–22), and John says whoever hates his brother is a murderer (1 John 3:15).

## 4. Judgment: innocent or guilty?
Sum up what they admitted in their own words, then ask. (Heb 9:27; Rom 2:15–16; James 2:10)
**Ask:** *"If God judges you by that standard, will you be innocent or guilty?"*

## 5. Destiny: heaven or hell?
Don't soften it, and don't gloat. Say it because you care where they end up. (Rev 21:8; 1 Cor 6:9–10)
**Ask:** *"Would that be heaven or hell? Does that concern you?"*

## 6. Now give the good news
You stand guilty in God's courtroom, and Someone steps in and pays your fine in full. That is what Christ did on the cross, and He rose from the dead. (Rom 5:8; 2 Cor 5:21; 1 Pet 2:24; 1 Cor 15:3–4)

## 7. Repent and trust
Believing a parachute exists won't save you; you have to put it on. Turn from sin and put your trust in Christ alone, today. (Acts 3:19; Acts 20:21; Rom 13:14; 2 Cor 6:2)
**Ask:** *"When are you going to do that?"*

## Keep in mind
- **Already humble?** If someone is broken over their sin, skip the law and go straight to grace.
- **Aim at the conscience:** answering objections clears the path, but the law and the gospel do the work. Don't spend an hour on arguments and never get here. (Rom 2:15)
- **Tone:** kind, patient, and plainly concerned for them. (2 Tim 2:24–26)

Then go back to [The Gospel](/topic/the-gospel) for the full message and what to do when they respond.

# Worldviews: how do you know?
> Everyone starts somewhere. Only God's word gives you ground to stand on.

A presupposition is what a person takes for granted before the argument starts. The atheist is not neutral, and neither are you. He already knows God and suppresses that truth (Rom 1:18–21), so you are not handing him a God he has never met. You are showing him that he leans on God every time he reasons, judges or trusts his own mind. (Prov 1:7; Col 2:3; Ps 14:1; 1 Cor 1:20)

## What you are showing
You are not saying he knows nothing or believes in nothing. He reasons, does science and knows right from wrong, because he lives in God's world and is made in God's image. You are saying his worldview cannot **account for** any of it, and yours can. He is using the tools God gave him to argue against God.
**Say:** *"We both use reason. I'm not asking whether you reason. I'm asking why reason can be trusted if your view is true."*

## Step 1: Don't give up your ground
Do not agree to set the Bible aside and argue from "neutral" facts. No one is neutral, and Scripture is the standard, not the thing on trial. You start from what God has revealed. He starts from his own guess. (Prov 26:4; 2 Cor 10:5; 1 Pet 3:15)
**Ask:** *"Would you set aside your trust in reason to prove reason? Then why should I set aside God's word?"*

## Step 2: Ask what he would accept
Before you give evidence, find out what he counts as evidence. If he has no standard, he has no basis for saying there isn't any.
**Ask:** *"What would count as evidence for you?"*
- **"Only what can be measured or tested."** → *"Did you measure that? Which experiment showed that only tested things are true?"* That rule is not something the five senses can find.
- **"I don't know what would convince me."** → *"Then how do you know there's no evidence?"*
- **"God is outside the universe, so He can't be tested."** → Evidence is more than lab results. We reason, weigh testimony and draw conclusions every day. (Acts 1:3; Heb 11:1)

## Step 3: Step into his view and show it cannot stand
Take his view on its own terms and ask what follows. One question at a time, and wait for the answer. (Prov 26:5)
- **Knowledge:** *"How do you know that? How do you know anything for certain?"* If he says we can't know anything for certain: *"Are you certain of that?"*
- **Logic:** *"Are the laws of logic made of matter? Do they change? Where do unchanging, universal laws come from in a universe of matter in motion?"* If he says God is illogical: *"That assumes logic is reliable. In your view, where does it come from?"*
- **His own mind:** *"You said 'mind.' Is that anything more than brain chemistry? If your thoughts are chemistry shaped for survival, why trust them to tell you what is true?"*
- **Science:** *"Why expect nature to act tomorrow the way it did today?"* (Gen 8:22; Col 1:17; Heb 1:3)
- **Meaning:** *"If we came from nothing and go to nothing, on what basis does anything you do matter?"* If he says nothing has meaning: *"Does that sentence have meaning?"* (Eccl 1:2; Eccl 12:13–14)

## Right and wrong
Start here when he calls something evil, unfair or hateful.
**Ask:** *"Is morality objective, or is it opinion?"*
- **"It's subjective."** → *"Then you can't say I'm wrong. You can only say you prefer something else, the way one person prefers burgers and another pizza."* Watch for him to keep saying you are wrong anyway. Point it out kindly.
- **"It comes from my experience."** → *"Someone raised to hate his neighbors has experience too. Is his hatred good?"* Experience differs from person to person, so it cannot be the standard.
- **"It comes from science."** → Science tells you what **is**. It cannot tell you what **ought** to be. *"Which experiment shows that lying is wrong?"*
- **"That's just your interpretation of the Bible."** → Everyone interprets something: he interprets data and his own experience. Disagreement does not cancel truth. *"If someone says 1 + 1 = 3, does that make the answer uncertain?"*
- **"That's true for you. This is my truth."** → An opinion is not the same as truth. Truth is what matches reality, whoever believes it. *"Can a person sincerely believe something and be wrong?"*
- **"It's objective."** → *"Then who set the standard, and why does it bind everyone?"* (Rom 2:14–15)

## Start with the Creator
Many people today have no Bible background. Words like sin, judgment and Savior mean little to someone who thinks he is an accident. Peter preached to people who knew the Scriptures and went straight to Christ and repentance (Acts 2:22–38). Paul, in Athens, began with the God who made the world and everything in it, and then said God now commands all people everywhere to repent (Acts 17:24–31). John's Gospel begins the same way: all things came into being through Christ (John 1:1–3; Col 1:16). You are not delaying the gospel. You are telling him whose world this is, so that sin makes sense and the cross is good news.
**Ask:** *"Who made you? And if you were made, who do you answer to?"*
- **Made on purpose.** He is not a cosmic accident. He was made by God, in God's image, to know Him. (Gen 1:27; Ps 100:3; Ps 139:13–14)
- **Worth.** Every person bears God's image: unborn, disabled, old, unable to contribute anything. *"If worth depends on what a person can do, is a strong man worth more than a child with Down syndrome?"* (Gen 9:6; James 3:9)
- **Guilt.** He may have been taught that guilt is only herd instinct or upbringing. *"When someone wrongs you, is that only instinct, or did he really do wrong?"* The conscience is God's law written on the heart. (Rom 2:14–15)
- **Then sin and the Savior.** The One who made him is the One he has sinned against, and the One who came to die for sinners. (Col 1:16; Col 1:19–20)

## Step 4: Bring him back to your ground
Show that what he has been borrowing belongs to God. Logic reflects God's unchanging mind, morality His holy character, the order of nature His faithfulness, and our reasoning His image in us. All things were made through the Word. (Gen 1:27; Mal 3:6; John 1:1–3; Acts 17:24–28)

## If he says
- **"So you're saying atheists can't know anything?"** → No. He knows a great deal. The question is whether his worldview can explain how.
- **"I just lack belief. I don't have to prove anything."** → *"You still reason, judge and trust your mind every day. What do those rest on?"*
- **"That's circular. You're using God to prove God."** → Every final starting point has to vouch for itself, or it isn't final. *"Can you prove your words have meaning without using words?"* The question is which starting point makes sense of everything else. His explains nothing. God's word explains logic, morality, science and him. (Heb 6:13)
- **"We both just assume our senses work."** → For him the senses are the foundation, with nothing under them. For you they are a tool, and under them is the God who made eyes, minds and the world they fit.
- **"Logic is just a fact of the universe."** → That repeats the claim; it doesn't explain it. *"Would you accept it if I said God is just a fact of the universe?"*
- **"Logic is just how humans think."** → Then it could have been otherwise, and it would not bind anyone who thinks differently, including you.
- **"Morality comes from evolution or society."** → That tells you what people **do** approve of, not what they **ought** to. Was slavery right when society approved it?
- **"I don't know where they come from, and that's fine."** → *"So you rely on them with no reason to. That is faith. I can tell you where they come from."*

## Watch out
- **Don't say "you believe in nothing."** He will name things he believes, and he'll be right. Say his worldview can't account for them.
- **Don't call him or his view dumb, and don't dismiss him.** Say "that doesn't hold together," and stay kind when he gets sharp. (2 Tim 2:24–26)
- **Don't blame evil on God's hands being tied.** When evil or hell comes up, don't say God wanted to stop it but free will prevented Him. God is sovereign over all things, He is not the author of sin, and people answer for their own sin. Use [the evil and suffering answer](/atheist/objection/if-god-good-why-there) and [the "why create me?" answer](/atheist/objection/if-god-knew-id-reject). (Eph 1:11; Gen 50:20; Acts 2:23)
- **Don't trade away creation to sound reasonable.** God made everything in six days. See [the evolution answer](/atheist/objection/evolution-explains-life-we-dont).
- **Don't treat it as a trick.** Winning the exchange saves no one. He is not confused; he is suppressing the truth, and only God opens blind eyes. (2 Cor 4:4–6; 1 Cor 2:14)
- **Evidence still belongs.** The apostles pointed to the empty tomb and the eyewitnesses. Give the evidence as God's facts, not as something a neutral judge gets to rule on. (Acts 17:31; 1 Cor 15:3–8)
- **Don't lean on quotes and numbers you can't back up.** A line credited to Darwin or a statistic from a sermon can be wrong, and one bad quote costs you the hearing. Stay with Scripture and with questions.
- **Get to the cross.** This clears the ground. Then give [The Gospel](/topic/the-gospel).

# MacArthur on Rome
> What John MacArthur says about Roman Catholicism, in his own words.

These lines come from John MacArthur's sermons on the Pope and the papacy, the Mass, the priesthood, Galatians 5 and the idolatry of Mary worship (Grace to You). Use them to know where our teachers stand and why. With a Catholic friend, open the Bible first and ask questions; these are for your own conviction and for when someone asks, "Is it really that serious?"

## His heart
He begins with grief, not anger: "We find no delight in people who are condemned to hell." And he ends with a plea: "People need to be rescued from this system." (Ezek 33:11; Rom 10:1; 2 Tim 2:24–26)

## The system
MacArthur calls Roman Catholicism "an apostate, corrupt, heretical, false Christianity" and "a front for the kingdom of Satan." On adding works to faith he says, "There is no room for any alteration of the gospel." Of Rome and Eastern Orthodoxy together: "It is not to be joined, it is to be cursed." He is using Paul's word, anathema. (Gal 1:6–9; Gal 5:2–4; 2 Cor 11:13–15; 2 Pet 2:1)
- **Backing:** Trent placed its curse on justification by faith alone [Sess. 6, can. 9, 12, 14] and has never withdrawn it. Paul put his curse on any gospel that adds to faith.

## The Mass
He calls transubstantiation "mystical hocus-pocus, mumbo-jumbo for sure," and says that phrase came from the Latin words of the Mass, *hoc est corpus meum*. He says the Mass is paid for: "You pay for a Mass. That's how the coffers of the Catholic Church are filled." And he reminds us that English believers were burned alive for denying it. (Heb 9:25–28; Heb 10:10–14; Heb 10:18)
- **Backing:** Rome's own law lets a priest accept an offering to apply a Mass for a named intention [Code of Canon Law, can. 945]. Trent calls the Mass propitiatory for the living and the dead [Sess. 22, can. 3].
- **Backing:** Archbishop John Tillotson gave the same origin for "hocus-pocus" in 1694. Rest the argument on Hebrews, and use the phrase as MacArthur's.
- **Backing:** Foxe's *Book of Martyrs* records about 280 people burned under Mary Tudor (1555–1558), many of them for rejecting the Mass.

## Mary
"Pagan goddess worship dressed up in Roman Catholic fantasy." He traces it to the old mother-and-child cults (Ishtar, Astarte, Isis) and to the "queen of heaven" whom God judged Judah for worshiping. On "Mother of God" he says, "God was never born." On the claim that we reach Christ through Mary: "This is a Satanic fabrication." On Co-Redemptrix: not formal dogma, but "unofficial reality." (Jer 7:18; Jer 44:17–19; Luke 1:46–48; Luke 11:27–28; 1 Tim 2:5; Isa 42:8)
- **Backing:** He quotes Rome's own writers, above all Alphonsus de Liguori's *The Glories of Mary*, which Rome has never condemned. Liguori is a canonized saint and a Doctor of the Church.
- **Backing:** The Catechism still calls Mary Advocate, Helper, Benefactress and Mediatrix and says she was exalted "as Queen over all things" [CCC 966, 968–969]. The 2025 Vatican note set aside a title. It removed no doctrine.

## Purgatory and indulgences
"Purgatory is what makes the whole system work." He says Rome invented it, that it is a second chance after death, and that indulgences are forgiveness for sale: the treasury of merit can be drawn on, and "you can buy it." (Heb 1:3; Heb 9:27; Luke 23:43; Acts 8:20)
- **Backing:** Rome still teaches the treasury of merit and indulgences for the living and the dead [CCC 1471–1479], and still grants them for acts such as passing through a Holy Door in a Jubilee year (2000, 2025). The open sale of indulgences is what Luther attacked in 1517.

## The Pope
He says the titles are stolen. "Holy Father" is what Jesus called God. Head of the church belongs to Christ. And Vicar of Christ, the one who stands in Christ's place: "He has stolen that from the Holy Spirit." He says there is no evidence in Scripture that Peter was ever in Rome. He reads out the papacy's record of immorality and wealth and calls its display "theater." On Antichrist he quotes Luther, Calvin, Knox, the Puritans and Spurgeon, then gives his own view: the Pope is not the final Antichrist, but "the very embodiment of antichrist" in this age, and it is "not a bad guess to see the final Antichrist as a pope." (John 17:11; Matt 23:9; Col 1:18; John 16:7; 1 John 2:18; 2 Thess 2:3–4)
- **Backing:** This was the common Protestant confession. Luther's Smalcald Articles (II.4), the Westminster Confession (25.6) and the 1689 London Baptist Confession (26.4) all name the Pope as "that Antichrist, that man of sin."
- **Backing:** Our church teaches a future, personal Antichrist. MacArthur's wording fits that: many antichrists now (1 John 2:18), the final one still to come.

## The priesthood
On required celibacy he goes to 1 Timothy 4: "Those who forbid marriage are advocating a doctrine of demons." He ties the scandals of the priesthood to that rule and to a priesthood the New Testament never created. (1 Tim 4:1–3; 1 Tim 3:2; 1 Cor 9:5; 1 Pet 2:9)

## How it began
"What was born with Constantine was Christendom rather than Christianity." He says the empire made one official religion, pagan practice came in with the crowds, and true believers were there through every century, protesting.
- **Dates to know:** Constantine legalized Christianity in 313 and called the Council of Nicaea in 325. Theodosius made it the state religion in 380. The papal claims grew after that: Leo I (440–461), then Gregory VII and Boniface VIII in the Middle Ages.

## Why people stay
From John 5, where a man healed by Jesus sides with the leaders against Him: "That is the power of false religion." People fear being put out. Expect it, and pray. (John 5:15–16; John 9:22; 2 Cor 10:4–5; 2 Cor 4:4)

Then go to [the Catholic plays](/catholic) and give [The Gospel](/topic/the-gospel).

One Being, three Persons: the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. One *what*, three *who's*. Each line below is taught in Scripture; the Trinity is what you get when you believe all of them at once.

## There is one God
(Deut 6:4; Isa 43:10; Isa 44:6; 1 Cor 8:4; James 2:19)

## The Father is God
(John 6:27; 1 Cor 8:6; Eph 4:6; 1 Pet 1:2)

## The Son is God
(John 1:1; John 8:58; John 20:28; Col 2:9; Titus 2:13; Heb 1:8)

## The Holy Spirit is God
(Acts 5:3–4; 1 Cor 3:16; Ps 139:7–8; Heb 3:7–11)
For the full argument, open [The Holy Spirit play](/holy-spirit).

## The three are distinct Persons
The Father sends the Son, the Son prays to the Father, and the Spirit is sent by both. All three are present at Jesus' baptism. (Matt 3:16–17; Matt 28:19; John 14:16–17; John 15:26; John 17:5; Gal 4:4–6; 2 Cor 13:14)

## What it is not
- **Not three gods:** that is tritheism. Scripture says there is one God.
- **Not one Person in three masks:** that is modalism. The Father, Son and Spirit speak to and about one another.
- **Not a created Son or Spirit:** that is Arianism. All things were made through the Son (John 1:3).

# Is the Bible reliable?
> Muslims, Mormons and atheists all attack this from different sides.

## We have what they wrote
The New Testament survives in thousands of Greek manuscripts, some copied within a few generations of the originals. The Dead Sea Scrolls, copied before and around the time of Christ, agree closely with the Hebrew manuscripts copied a thousand years later. (Isa 40:8; Matt 24:35; 1 Pet 1:24–25)

## It comes from eyewitnesses
The writers were eyewitnesses or wrote down what eyewitnesses told them, while many witnesses were still alive to object. (Luke 1:1–4; 2 Pet 1:16; 1 John 1:1–3; 1 Cor 15:3–8)

## Jesus treated Scripture as God's word
He quoted it as final, said it cannot be broken, and said every part of it would be fulfilled. (Matt 4:4; Matt 5:17–18; John 10:35; Luke 24:44)

## God breathed it out
Men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit, so the Scriptures as first written are without error. (2 Tim 3:16–17; 2 Pet 1:20–21)

## Prophecy written centuries ahead
The suffering Servant, the pierced hands and feet, the birthplace of the Messiah and the pierced One were all written long before Jesus was born. (Isa 53:5–6; Ps 22:16; Mic 5:2; Zech 12:10)

# Words they use
> A quick lookup for terms that come up in conversation.

## Jehovah's Witness
- **Jehovah:** their preferred form of God's name (YHWH). For them it names the Father only.
- **New World Translation (NWT):** the Watchtower's own Bible translation.
- **Watchtower / Governing Body:** the leadership that directs all Witness teaching and calls itself the "faithful and discreet slave."
- **Active force:** their term for the Holy Spirit, whom they say is not a person.
- **Kingdom Interlinear:** the Watchtower's own Greek and English New Testament. Useful because it prints the Greek with a word-for-word English line next to their NWT.
- **The 144,000:** the "anointed" who they say go to heaven. Everyone else hopes for life on a paradise earth.

## Unitarian
- **Biblical Unitarian:** holds that God is one person, the Father, and Jesus is a human Messiah.
- **Arian:** holds that the Son is God's first and greatest creature.
- **Shaliach (agency):** the Jewish idea that a sent agent stands for the one who sent him. They use it to explain away texts where Jesus speaks as God.
- **Oneness / modalism:** God is one Person who shows Himself as Father, Son and Spirit (as roles or modes, not three Persons). Oneness Pentecostals say that one Person is Jesus.
- **Homoousios:** "of the same essence," the Nicene term for the Son's relation to the Father.
- **Malak / angelos:** Hebrew and Greek for "messenger." It names a job, not a nature.

## Mormon
- **Godhead:** for them, three separate beings who are one in purpose.
- **Exaltation:** the teaching that faithful people can become gods.
- **Great Apostasy:** their claim that the true church was lost after the apostles.
- **Restoration:** their claim that Joseph Smith restored the church.
- **Standard works:** their four books of scripture: the Bible, the Book of Mormon, the Doctrine and Covenants (D&C), and the Pearl of Great Price.
- **JST:** the Joseph Smith Translation, his revision of the Bible.
- **Testimony / burning in the bosom:** an inner feeling taken as God's confirmation that the church is true.
- **King Follett discourse:** Joseph Smith's 1844 sermon teaching that God was once a man.

## Muslim
- **Tawhid:** the absolute oneness of Allah.
- **Shirk:** associating anything with Allah. The one sin Allah will not forgive if a person dies in it (Q 4:48).
- **Isa:** the Quran's name for Jesus.
- **Tawrat, Zabur, Injil:** the Torah, the Psalms and the Gospel, which the Quran says Allah revealed.
- **Tahrif:** the claim that the earlier Scriptures were corrupted.
- **Hadith:** reports of what Muhammad said and did.
- **Surah / ayah:** a chapter and a verse of the Quran.
- **Tafsir:** commentary on the Quran.
- **Hafs, Warsh, Qalun:** different transmitted readings of the Quran.

## Roman Catholic
- **Magisterium:** the Church's teaching office (the pope and the bishops in communion with him), which alone can authentically interpret Scripture and Tradition [CCC 85, 100].
- **Sacred Tradition:** teaching handed down outside Scripture and held with "equal" reverence [CCC 82].
- **Ex cathedra:** the pope defining faith or morals "from the chair," held to be infallible and "irreformable" [Vatican I].
- **Transubstantiation:** the whole substance of bread and wine becomes Christ's body and blood, "truly, really, and substantially" [Trent, Sess. 13, can. 1; CCC 1376].
- **Latria / dulia / hyperdulia:** worship due to God alone / honor to saints / the higher honor given to Mary.
- **Infused vs. imputed righteousness:** Rome says God makes us "inwardly just" [CCC 1992]. Scripture says God credits Christ's righteousness to us (Rom 4:6; 2 Cor 5:21).
- **Mortal sin:** grave sin that destroys sanctifying grace and, if unrepented, leads to hell [CCC 1861].
- **Purgatory:** final purification after death for those who die in grace "but still imperfectly purified" [CCC 1030–1031].
- **Indulgence:** remission of temporal punishment for sins already forgiven, applicable to oneself or to souls in purgatory [CCC 1471, 1498].
- **Deuterocanonical books:** what Rome calls the Apocrypha: Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch, 1–2 Maccabees, and additions to Esther and Daniel [Trent, Sess. 4].
- **Judaizers:** teachers in the New Testament who believed in Jesus and said Gentiles must also be circumcised and keep the Law of Moses to be saved (Acts 15:1, 5). Galatians was written against them.
- **Necessary vs. sufficient:** Rome says grace, faith and Scripture are necessary. The Reformation said each is also enough: Scripture alone, grace alone, faith alone, Christ alone.
- **Unam Sanctam:** the 1302 bull of Boniface VIII that defined submission to the Roman Pontiff as necessary for salvation.

## Eastern Orthodox
- **Holy Tradition:** "the doctrine of the faith, the law of God, the sacraments, and the ritual as handed down" [Philaret Q17]. It includes Scripture and the councils.
- **Theosis (deification):** sharing in the life of God as the goal of salvation [GOARCH, "Spirituality"]. Not sharing God's essence.
- **Synergy:** God's grace and human effort working together in salvation.
- **Theotokos:** "God-bearer," the title for Mary affirmed at Ephesus (431).
- **Ever-virgin:** Mary remained a virgin before, during and after Christ's birth [Philaret Q186].
- **Proskynesis vs. latreia:** veneration (bowing, kissing) given to icons and saints / worship given to God alone [Nicaea II].
- **Ancestral sin:** we inherit death and corruption from Adam, but not his guilt [OCA Q&A, "Original Sin"].
- **Essence / energies:** God's unknowable essence vs. His uncreated energies, in which believers participate (Gregory Palamas).
- **Toll houses:** a pious tradition about demonic testing of the soul after death; **not dogma**.
- **Dormition:** Mary's "falling asleep" (Aug. 15). Many Orthodox believe she was taken bodily to heaven, but the East never defined it as Rome did in 1950.
- **Chrismation:** anointing with holy oil (chrism) right after baptism, the Orthodox form of confirmation. It is also how most converts from other churches are received.
- **Infant communion:** baptized and chrismated infants receive communion [OCA, *The Orthodox Faith*, vol. 2].
- **Prayers for the departed:** prayers, alms and the liturgy offered for the dead, which Dositheus says deliver some souls from Hades [Decree 18].

## Atheist
- **Atheist:** does not believe in God. Some go further and say there is no God.
- **Agnostic:** says they don't know, or that no one can know.
- **Naturalism:** the view that nature is all there is.
- **Objective morality:** right and wrong that are true whatever anyone thinks.
- **Burden of proof:** whoever makes a claim has to back it up. "There is no God" is a claim.

## Words we use
- **Trinity:** one God in three Persons. See [The Trinity](/topic/the-trinity).
- **Two natures:** Jesus is one Person who is truly God and truly man.
- **Ransom:** the price that frees a captive. Scripture says no man can pay it for another and that God redeems. Jesus gave His life as the ransom.
- **Mediator:** the one who stands between God and men. Jesus is the one mediator because He is truly God and truly man.
- **Monogenes:** the unique, only begotten Son. It marks His unique, eternal relationship to the Father, never creation (LSB: "only begotten").
- **Prototokos:** Greek for "firstborn." In texts like Col 1:15 (cf. Ps 89:27) it means the preeminent heir, not the first one created.
- **Arche:** Greek for "beginning," "origin" or "ruler."
