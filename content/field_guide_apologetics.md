## 🎯 The Method (read once, use everywhere)

Pray first. Only the Spirit opens eyes (John 6:44; 2 Cor 4:4–6). Watch your own heart for pride; the goal is their soul, not the point (2 Tim 2:24–26). What helps in real conversations isn't knowing more verses. It's **how you run the conversation**. Use these moves every time.

1. **Phase 1 = agree on the plain text. Nothing else moves until they do.**
   Read the verse and ask a yes/no question about what it *says* (not what it means). Don't go on until they answer. *"I'm not moving to Phase 2 until you answer Phase 1."* ([▶ Islamic Dilemma](https://youtu.be/xzgCmA5f7oo?t=190))

2. **Yes/no questions, one at a time.** *"Does the Holy Spirit speak? Yes or no?"* Short questions are hard to wriggle out of. Long statements give them room to filibuster.

3. **Call out the dodge out loud.** When they redefine a word ("personification," "communicates information," "a vibe"), name it: *"I didn't ask about personification. I asked, can He speak?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/km2ea8bX8Ow?t=11))

4. **Use their rule on another verse.** When they invent a rule for one verse (e.g. "no article = 'a god'"), apply the same rule somewhere it obviously fails (John 1:6 becomes "a man sent from *a god* named *a John*"). ([▶](https://youtu.be/sQxbaeAJF-M?t=641))

5. **Hold them to their own analogy.** If they bring an analogy (king and messenger, software coder, three judges), don't swap it out. Push it until it proves your point: *"Can the agent of the king claim to BE the king?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/hRYmFJqy9is?t=484))

6. **No topic-hopping.** When a point lands, people often jump to "Is Jesus God?" or "the Trinity makes no sense." Don't follow. *"Let's finish this one first, then I'm happy to go there."* ([▶](https://youtu.be/XB83I9YYMeg?t=994))

7. **Name the goalpost shift.** *"First you wanted a Bible verse. I gave you the verse. Now it has to come from Jesus' own mouth?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/c_JWoiexhXI?t=562))

8. **"What's the only thing we do? Read."** When they say "context," make *them* show the verse in the context. Then wait. Silence works for you. ([▶](https://youtu.be/SGyVqLKrtjE?t=340))

9. **Ask the question back.** End with a question they have to answer on their own (*"Who is the Father hearing from?"*), not a speech.

10. **Stay warm.** Even when they get angry or walk away: *"I want you to be saved, brother. I love you."* ([▶](https://youtu.be/QNaYaD3ET78?t=341)) Truth with love keeps the door open (1 Pet 3:15–16).

### 🧰 Universal tools (work on any non-Trinitarian)
- **"Yahweh" isn't one-person-only.** Yahweh is the name of the one true God, and Scripture applies it to the Father, the Son and the Spirit. Their "then Jesus is his own Father" syllogism only works if Yahweh = the Father alone. A name can have more than one referent: "Judah" can mean a man *or* a tribe (Judges 1:1–2). ([▶](https://youtu.be/1Ro4BK8GvA4?t=20), [▶](https://youtu.be/SGyVqLKrtjE?t=441))
- **Word–concept fallacy.** "'Trinity' isn't in the Bible" → neither are "monotheism" or "Bible." The question is whether the *concept* is there. ([▶](https://youtu.be/X-aevlcvkv8?t=1810))
- **Three in one way, one in another.** Not a contradiction. Different categories: 3 *persons*, 1 *being*. (With a Muslim: the Quran comes in several transmitted readings, such as Hafs, Warsh and Qalun, and Muslims still say it is one Quran. [▶](https://youtu.be/HwCcC0D0M08?t=14))
- **Context decides literal vs. figurative.** Both sides agree personification exists. But personified things don't say "set apart for **me**" or "the work **I** have called them to."
- **"Firstborn" = rank, not birth order.** David was the *youngest* son and the *second* king, yet God calls him "firstborn, the highest of the kings of the earth" (Ps 89:27).

## 🔥 THE HOLY SPIRIT PLAY: "the Spirit is Yahweh" (Unitarians, JWs, Oneness, Muslims)

> **Why this works:** Unitarians spend all their energy on Jesus (agency, *shaliach*, "a god"). They usually have **no prepared answer for the Spirit**. ([▶](https://youtu.be/SGyVqLKrtjE?t=1)) Two steps: **(A) the Spirit is a Person → (B) that Person is Yahweh.**

### Step A: The Spirit is a *Who*, not a *what*

**Opener:** *"Does the Holy Spirit speak? Yes or no?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/3AF8giy2H7Y?t=0))

| Go to | The point to land | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Acts 13:2** | "Set apart for **me** Barnabas and Saul for the work to which **I** have called them." First-person speech, a choice, authority. | *"Do actions have authority, or do persons?"* / *"He said 'I' and 'me' about Himself. That's self-awareness, right?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/GIklmH4tkfU?t=8)) |
| **Acts 15:28** | "It seemed good to the Holy Spirit **and to us**." He takes part in a decision alongside the apostles. | *"Can a force agree with a council?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/_b0PY_cg7Pk?t=372)) |
| **Heb 3:7** | "As the Holy Spirit **says**…" | *"Can energy speak to people?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/3AF8giy2H7Y?t=18)) |
| **Eph 4:30; Isa 63:10; Ps 78:40** | He can be grieved and rebelled against. | *"An active force can't be offended. Whoever the Spirit is, He's someone you **can** offend."* ([▶](https://youtu.be/Fw22dTLWsUU?t=12)) |
| **John 16:13** | He "does not speak on His own; whatever He **hears** He speaks." | (vs. Oneness or "Spirit = the Father") *"Who is the Father hearing from?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/LJTQKtT4cjU?t=33)) |
| **1 Cor 1:24** | Christ is called "the **power** of God." | *"Christ is called God's power and He's a person. So being God's power doesn't rule out being a person, right?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/13zIG04ZkBE?t=2)) |

**If they say →**
- *"That's personification."* → *"I didn't ask about figurative language. Can He speak, yes or no? If I say 'my car told me it's not starting today,' did my car speak?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/km2ea8bX8Ow?t=11)). Then: personified wisdom never chooses missionaries in real time (Acts 13:2).
- *"He just communicates information / He has no mouth."* → *"Does the Father have a mouth? Does the Father speak?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/3AF8giy2H7Y?t=28))
- *"The Father told the Spirit to say it."* → The text says "for **me**… **I** have called." That's not quoting someone else. ([▶](https://youtu.be/GIklmH4tkfU?t=68))
- *"He's rational energy, like AI."* → AI is programmed. The Spirit wills, commands and is provoked. *"Can energy be rational without personhood?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/SGyVqLKrtjE?t=882))
- *"God used the Spirit's power to create."* → God also created *through Christ* (John 1:3), and Christ is a person. ([▶](https://youtu.be/Fw22dTLWsUU?t=65))

### Step B: That Person is Yahweh

**The structure:** an OT text says **Yahweh** spoke → the NT says **the Holy Spirit** said it.

| **Jer 31:31–34**: "This is the covenant **I** will make… **declares Yahweh**" | **Heb 10:15–17**: "**The Holy Spirit** also bears witness… 'This is the covenant **I** will make'" | The Spirit speaks Yahweh's covenant in the first person. |

**Old Testament only (when they say "no NT"):** Deut 6:4 → Gen 1:2 (the Spirit of God) → 2 Sam 23:2–3 → Gen 31:11–13 / 35:1 (the Angel = the God of Bethel. *"Why is God saying 'go meet the God you met'?"*) → Zech 12:10 (*"they will look on **Me** whom they pierced." "Who did they pierce?"*). *"I don't need the New Testament to prove the Trinity."* ([▶](https://youtu.be/RyWETj0uHsI?t=7))

**If they say →**
- *"The 'Spirit of Yahweh' means 'from Yahweh,' not 'is Yahweh.'"* → *"The answer is yes to all of them."* From Yahweh, and also Yahweh. Not either/or. ([▶](https://youtu.be/M2smkArcRnU?t=15)) Then: *"He's also the Spirit of Christ, right?"* (Rom 8:9). If they deny it, read the verse.
- *"OK, so the Spirit is just the Father."* → John 16:13 (He hears from another), John 14:16 (*another* Helper whom the Father sends). *"Then the Father sends Himself and listens to Himself?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/LJTQKtT4cjU?t=70))
- They admit it but walk away from the Bible ("then I can't trust the Bible"): that's 2 Tim 4:3, "itching ears." They are conceding the Bible teaches it. ([▶](https://youtu.be/6b6VOzdPJAQ?t=54))

**Closer:** *"You've agreed the Spirit speaks, chooses, and is grieved, so He's a who. And Scripture says that Who is Yahweh. So Yahweh is more than one Person. Now let's talk about the Son."*

# 🟦 JEHOVAH'S WITNESSES

**Their position:** Jehovah = the Father only. Jesus = Michael the Archangel, Jehovah's first creation, "a god." The Holy Spirit = God's impersonal "active force." The NWT is the authoritative translation; the Watchtower (Governing Body) is the "faithful and discreet slave."

**The dividing issue:** Who Jesus is. To them He is Michael, the first thing Jehovah made. Scripture says He is the Maker of all things, Yahweh the Son, and that unless a man believes He is who He says He is, he will die in his sins. With that comes how a man is saved: by faith in Christ alone, or by loyalty to an organization. (John 1:1–3; Col 1:16–17; John 8:24; Eph 2:8–9)

**Ask this question:** *"In Hebrews 1:10 the Father says to the Son, 'You, Lord, laid the foundation of the earth.' Those words come from Psalm 102, a prayer to Jehovah. Why does the Father speak to the Son with words written about Jehovah?"* (Heb 1:10–12; Ps 102:25–27)

**Scriptural response:** All things came into being through the Word, and apart from Him nothing came into being that has come into being, so He is not one of the things that came into being. Yahweh says He made all things alone. Isaiah saw Yahweh's glory, and John says Isaiah saw Jesus' glory. Thomas called Him "my Lord and my God," and Jesus blessed that confession. (John 1:3; Isa 44:24; Isa 6:1–5; John 12:41; John 20:28–29; Col 2:9)

**Common objection:** *"Jesus said, 'The Father is greater than I.'"* → Greater in position while the Son walked the earth as a servant, not greater in nature. A president is greater than I am in office and no more human than I am. The same Gospel says He made Himself equal with God, and that all are to honor the Son just as they honor the Father. (John 14:28; Phil 2:5–8; John 5:18; John 5:23)

**Gospel bridge:** *"Do you know, right now, that you have eternal life?"* The Watchtower can only offer "maybe, if you stay faithful." Jesus says the one who believes has eternal life and has passed out of death into life. He did not say "Come to an organization." He said, "Come to Me." (John 5:24; 1 John 5:13; Rom 5:1; Matt 11:28)

**Get this first:** *"If the Bible disagrees with the Watchtower, you'd side with the Bible, right?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/k_P2xx2NIVg?t=166)) Lock in that commitment before you open a verse.

### 🗡️ JW OFFENSE

#### PLAY 1: Jesus is the uncreated Creator (John 1:3 / Col 1:16)
- **Opener:** *"According to John 1:3, how many things came into existence without the Word?"* (Answer: not one.)
- **Chain:**
  1. **John 1:3**: "All things came into existence through him, and apart from him not even one thing came into existence." *"Does it say 'except himself'?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/_-JpXH9GN-E?t=644))
  2. *"If not one thing came into existence without Him, and Jesus came into existence, then Jehovah used Jesus to make Jesus?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/sQxbaeAJF-M?t=945))
  3. **Col 1:16–17 (NWT)**: point out "all *other* things," four times. *"Where is 'other' in the Greek?"* It's *ta panta*, "all things." The Watchtower's **own Kingdom Interlinear** has no "other." ([▶](https://youtu.be/HRuXGRkhcbw?t=146), [▶](https://youtu.be/k_P2xx2NIVg?t=951))
  4. **Isa 44:24**: "I, Jehovah… stretched out the heavens **by myself**, who spread out the earth. **Who was with me?**" → *"If Jehovah created alone, and all things were created through Jesus, who is Jesus?"*
- **If they say →**
  - *"'All' doesn't always mean all."* → Here the verse defines it: "in the heavens and on the earth, visible and invisible, thrones, lordships" (Col 1:16). And John 1:3 says "not **one** thing." ([▶](https://youtu.be/HMOOX0u3YMc?t=726))
  - *"He was Jehovah's master worker (Prov 8:30)."* → Working alongside doesn't mean created. And Prov 8 personifies Wisdom (a "she"). Is God ever without wisdom? ([▶](https://youtu.be/HRuXGRkhcbw?t=273))
- **Closer:** *"Everything that has ever come into existence came through Jesus. So Jesus has always existed. Do you agree?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/HRuXGRkhcbw?t=845))

#### PLAY 2: Jesus is Yahweh (the "First and Last" / Hebrews 1 chain)
- **Opener:** *"Who is the First and the Last in Isaiah 44:6?"* (They'll say Jehovah.) *"Does being the First and the Last mean you have a beginning?"* (No.)
- **Chain:**
  1. **Isa 44:6; 48:12**: Jehovah is "the first and the last; besides me there is no God."
  2. **Rev 1:17–18**: "I am the First and the Last… I **became dead**." *"When did Jehovah die?"* So the speaker is Jesus.
  3. **Rev 22:13, 16**: "Alpha and Omega, First and Last… I, **Jesus**." *"So Jesus has a beginning, but Jehovah doesn't, with the same title?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/-ph8WlJNirY?t=20))
  4. **Heb 1:8, 10–12**: the Father says to the Son, "You, Lord, in the beginning laid the foundation of the earth" — quoting **Ps 102:25–27**, which is addressed *to Jehovah*. *"Who is the Psalm talking to? And who does Hebrews apply it to?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/_-JpXH9GN-E?t=54))
  5. **Isa 45:23 → Phil 2:10–11**: "To **me** every knee will bow" (Yahweh) → "at the name of **Jesus** every knee should bow."
  6. **Isa 6:1–5 → John 12:41**: Isaiah "saw **his** [Jesus'] glory." The one Isaiah saw was Yahweh of hosts.
- **If they say →**
  - *"Jesus is the 'first and last' in a different sense."* → *"Then show me the text that makes the difference."* Same title, same book, same speaker formula.
  - *"Hebrews 1:8 should say 'God is your throne.'"* → v.8 opens "**to** the Son He says." The Father is speaking *to* the Son. And v.10 ("You, Lord…") has no throne issue at all.
- **Closer:** *"Jehovah says there's no God besides Him, and He gives His titles, His Psalm and His glory to Jesus. So either Jehovah shares His glory with a creature (Isa 42:8), or Jesus is Jehovah."*

#### PLAY 3: The Spirit is a Person and is Jehovah
**Opener:** *"Does the Holy Spirit speak? Yes or no?"*

→ Run the full [🔥 Holy Spirit Play](#holy-spirit-play). Best JW order: **Acts 13:2 → Eph 4:30 → 2 Sam 23:2–3 + Acts 4:24–25.** Witnesses often have no prepared answer at this point. ([▶](https://youtu.be/QNaYaD3ET78?t=646))

#### PLAY 4: Jesus is good, so He is God (Mark 10:18)
- **Opener:** *"Is Jesus good?"* (They'll hesitate.) ([▶](https://youtu.be/_-JpXH9GN-E?t=927))
- **Chain:** Mark 10:18, "No one is good but God alone." → *"He didn't say 'I'm not good.' He asked a question to expose the man's thinking."* → *"If Jesus is good, what does that make Him?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/_-JpXH9GN-E?t=1013))
- **If they say** *"He's not good like God"* → *"Name the moral flaw Jesus has that the Father doesn't."* ([▶](https://youtu.be/_-JpXH9GN-E?t=1082))

#### PLAY 5: Watchtower prophecy test (use when they lean on the organization)
- **Opener:** *"Has the Watchtower ever announced a date in Jehovah's name that didn't happen?"*
- **Or open with:** *"Have you heard of Beth Sarim, the mansion the Watchtower built in San Diego in 1929? Do you know who it was built for?"*
- **Chain:** "Millions Now Living Will Never Die" (Rutherford, 1920): the patriarchs were to be resurrected by **1925** → it failed → **Beth Sarim** mansion (San Diego, 1929) deeded "for Abraham, Isaac, Jacob," and Rutherford lived in it himself → sold in 1948 ([▶](https://youtu.be/mjDkOgvdCe8?t=46)) → **Deut 18:21–22**.
- **Question:** *"Are millions of people who were alive in 1920 still alive today?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/mjDkOgvdCe8?t=465)) *"When someone speaks in the Lord's name and it doesn't happen, what does Deuteronomy call him?"*
- **If they say** *"We're not prophets, just students making calculations"* → *"Announcing future dates as God's truth is prophecy by definition."* ([▶](https://youtu.be/mjDkOgvdCe8?t=423))
- ⚠️ Use this **after** the Bible plays. Leading with it feels like an attack and they shut down.

#### PLAY 6: Only God can pay the ransom (Psalm 49)
- **Opener:** *"According to Psalm 49:7, can any man redeem his brother or give God a ransom for him?"* (They'll say no.)
- **Chain:**
  1. **Ps 49:7–9**: no man can redeem his brother or give God a ransom for him. The redemption price for a soul is costly. *"How many men does the psalm say can pay it?"*
  2. **Ps 49:15**: "But **God** will redeem my soul from the power of Sheol." *"So who does the psalmist say will do the redeeming?"*
  3. **Isa 43:11**: Jehovah says, "there is no savior besides Me."
  4. **Mark 10:45; 1 Tim 2:5–6**: Jesus came to give His life as a **ransom** for many. The one mediator gave Himself as a ransom for all.
  5. **Titus 2:13–14**: our great **God and Savior**, Jesus Christ, gave Himself for us that He might **redeem** us.
  6. **John 1:1; John 1:14; Heb 2:14–17**: the Word was God and became flesh. He **partook** of flesh and blood so that through death He might free those enslaved by the fear of death. He had to be man to die in our place, and God to redeem.
- **The logic:** This is a cumulative case. Psalm 49 does not name Jesus, and it does not prove the Trinity on its own. It says no mere man can pay the ransom and that God will redeem. The New Testament then says Jesus paid it, and calls Him God.
- **If they say →**
  - *"Psalm 49 is about imperfect men. Jesus was a perfect man, a corresponding ransom for Adam."* → The psalm makes no exception for a perfect man. Its answer is "God will redeem." And every creature worships the Lamb for the people He purchased (Rev 5:9; Rev 5:13–14). *"Would Jehovah share that worship with a creature?"* (Isa 42:8)
  - *"Jehovah provided the ransom. Jesus was only the price."* → Yes, the Father sent the Son. But the One who gave Himself is called our great God and Savior (Titus 2:13–14).
  - *"Acts 20:28 should read 'the blood of His own Son.'"* → That verse speaks of God purchasing the church with blood. Some manuscripts read "church of the Lord," and some translations say "the blood of His own." Don't rest the case on this one verse; the chain above stands without it.
- **Closer:** *"If no man can pay the ransom, and God says He will redeem, who is the Jesus who paid it?"*

### 🛡️ JW DEFENSE

| They say… | Quick answer (say it out loud) | Go to | Ask back |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"John 1:1 says 'a god.' No article = indefinite."** | A predicate noun before the verb without the article describes **nature** (Harner: qualitative). "The Word was God" in nature: all that God is, the Word was. | John 1:1; **1 John 4:8** (*ho theos agapē estin*); **John 1:6** (*para theou*, no article) | *"Is God 'a love'? # 🟩 UNITARIANS (Biblical Unitarians / non-Trinitarians / Arians)

**Their position:** Yahweh = the Father alone, one person. Jesus = a human Messiah (Biblical Unitarian) or a created spirit being (Arian). No real pre-existence. Jesus is God's "agent" (*shaliach*), so whatever He says or does as God is "agency." The Spirit = God's power or presence, not a person.

**The dividing issue:** Whether Jesus is a man God empowered, or the eternal Son who became man. If He is only a creature, worshiping Him is idolatry and His death cannot pay for the sins of the world. Scripture says the Word was God and became flesh. (John 1:1; John 1:14; Phil 2:5–8; Heb 1:3)

**Ask this question:** *"Yahweh says He will not give His glory to another. Then how did Jesus share the Father's glory before the world was, and why does every creature worship the Lamb together with the One on the throne?"* (Isa 42:8; John 17:5; Rev 5:13–14)

**Scriptural response:** The Word was with God and was God, and all things were made through Him. Before Abraham was born, "I am." The Father says to the Son, "Your throne, O God, is forever." Thomas calls Him "my Lord and my God." And the Spirit is a person who can be lied to, and to lie to Him is to lie to God. (John 1:1–3; John 8:58; Heb 1:8–10; John 20:28; Acts 5:3–4)

**Common objection:** *"Jesus calls the Father 'the only true God' (John 17:3)."* → He does, and in the same sentence eternal life is knowing the Father and Jesus Christ, and two verses later He speaks of glory He had with the Father before the world was. Calling the Father the only true God no more shuts out the Son than calling Jesus "our only Master and Lord" shuts out the Father. John himself calls the Son the true God. (John 17:3–5; Jude 1:4; 1 John 5:20)

**Gospel bridge:** *"If Jesus is only a man, how can one man's death pay for the sins of the world?"* No mere man can redeem his brother. God purchased the church with His own blood, and because the Son lives forever He saves forever those who draw near to God through Him. (Ps 49:7–8; Acts 20:28; Heb 7:25; John 8:24)

**Their strength:** they've drilled the Jesus texts (agency, "the Father is the only true God," John 17:3).
**Their weak spot:** **the Holy Spirit** and the **Angel of Yahweh**. Go there early.

# 🟨 MORMONS (Latter-day Saints)

**Their position:** The Father, Son and Holy Ghost are **three separate beings** (three gods) united in purpose, the "Godhead." The Father has a body of flesh and bone (D&C 130:22). "As man now is, God once was; as God now is, man may become" (Lorenzo Snow). Exaltation: faithful humans can become gods. Joseph Smith restored the church after a "great apostasy." The Book of Mormon is another testament.

**The dividing issue:** Who God is, and what grace is. Their God was once a man and is one of many gods; the God of the Bible has always been God and knows of no other. Their gospel is grace "after all we can do" (2 Nephi 25:23); the Bible's is a gift to the one who does not work but believes. (Isa 43:10; Ps 90:2; Eph 2:8–9; Rom 4:4–5)

**Ask this question:** *"In Isaiah 43:10 God says, 'Before Me there was no God formed, and there will be none after Me.' How does that fit with God once being a man, and men becoming gods?"* (Isa 43:10)

**Scriptural response:** Yahweh says He is the first and the last, that there is no God besides Him, and that He does not know of any other. From everlasting to everlasting He is God. God is not a man. God is spirit. He does not change. (Isa 44:6, 8; Isa 45:5; Ps 90:2; Num 23:19; John 4:24; Mal 3:6)

**Common objection:** *"I have a testimony. I prayed about the Book of Mormon and I know it is true."* → I don't doubt you felt something. But Scripture says the heart is deceitful, tells us to test the spirits, and says that even an angel from heaven preaching another gospel is to be rejected. The Bereans were called noble for checking Paul against the Scriptures, not against their feelings. (Jer 17:9; 1 John 4:1; Gal 1:8; Acts 17:11)

**Gospel bridge:** *"Your scripture says you are saved by grace after all you can do. Have you done all you can do?"* Nobody has. That is why the good news is that God justifies the ungodly who believes, apart from works, and wants His people to know they have eternal life. (Rom 4:4–5; Rom 11:6; Eph 2:8–9; 1 John 5:13)

**Approach:** Missionaries are usually 19–20 and sincere, but haven't studied this deeply. Be warm. Your best tool is **Isaiah 43–45** plus **their own Book of Mormon**, which is more monotheistic than later LDS teaching.

# ✡️ JEWISH PEOPLE

**Their position:** There is one God, He gave the Torah to Israel at Sinai, and Israel is His covenant people. The Messiah has not come yet: he will be a human king from David's line who gathers Israel, rebuilds the temple and brings peace to the world, and Jesus did none of that. Man is not born guilty and is able to choose good. Sin is forgiven through repentance, prayer and deeds of charity, with Yom Kippur at the centre. Know who you are talking to: in America about 9% of Jewish people are Orthodox, 37% Reform, 17% Conservative, and 32% claim no branch at all [Pew, Jewish Americans in 2020]. Many have never read Isaiah.

**The dividing issue:** Whether a person needs a Savior from sin, and whether Jesus is that Savior, the promised Messiah. John MacArthur: "Judaism doesn't need a savior from sin. Christianity exalts the Savior from sin" [MacArthur, sermon introducing Isaiah 53]. If keeping, repenting and suffering can make a man right with God, a Messiah who dies in the sinner's place has no purpose. Scripture says no one living is righteous before God, and that the Servant bears the sin of many. (Ps 143:2; Isa 64:6; Isa 53:5–6; Isa 53:11)

**Ask this question:** *"Do you, personally, need a Savior to take your place and bear God's judgment for your sins?"* (Isa 53:6)

**Scriptural response:** Stay in their own Scriptures. Seven hundred years before Jesus, Isaiah described a righteous Servant who is rejected by his own people, pierced for their transgressions, killed, buried with a rich man, alive again to see his offspring, and who makes many righteous by bearing their sins. Moses said it is the blood that makes atonement. Daniel said the Anointed One would be cut off before the city and the temple were destroyed, and that happened in AD 70. (Isa 53:5–6; Isa 53:8–11; Lev 17:11; Dan 9:26; Zech 12:10)

**Common objection:** *"The servant in Isaiah 53 is the nation of Israel."* → Israel is called God's servant earlier in the book, but this Servant restores Israel, so he cannot be Israel. He is innocent, and Isaiah opens his book calling Israel a sinful nation. He suffers silently and willingly, for "my people," as a guilt offering. The oldest Jewish readings took him to be the Messiah [Targum Jonathan on Isa 52:13; Talmud, Sanhedrin 98b]. (Isa 49:5–6; Isa 53:8–9; Isa 1:4; Isa 53:10)

**Gospel bridge:** *"Abraham told Isaac, 'God will provide for Himself the lamb.' Isaiah says the Servant was led like a lamb to slaughter, and that Yahweh laid on him the iniquity of us all. If God has provided the Lamb, what will you do with Him?"* Abraham believed God and it was counted to him as righteousness. The same promise stands for the Jew first. (Gen 22:8; Isa 53:6–7; Gen 15:6; Rom 1:16; Rom 10:9–13)

**Where we agree:** There is one God, the Maker of heaven and earth, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. The Law, the Prophets and the Writings are His word. God chose Israel, has kept them through every attempt to destroy them, and is not finished with them. Sin is real and must be confessed to God. A Messiah from David's line is promised. Every writer of the New Testament but perhaps one was Jewish, and so is Jesus.

**Approach:** Come with love and a debt of thanks: the Scriptures, the covenants and the Messiah came to us through them. MacArthur told Ben Shapiro, "I am a Christian because of the Old Testament" [MacArthur, interview with Ben Shapiro]. Paul had great sorrow and unceasing grief for his kinsmen and prayed for their salvation, and so should you. Never say "the Jews killed Jesus" as a charge against the person in front of you: Jews and Gentiles together did what God's hand had decided beforehand, and Jesus laid down His life of His own accord. Own the history honestly: terrible things were done to Jewish people in the name of Christ, against everything Christ taught. Do not be arrogant toward the branches; you stand by faith. Use the Old Testament first, ask them to read the passage aloud, and ask "Who is this about?" before you say a word. The hope is sure. MacArthur: "God's not through with them" [MacArthur, conference message on Israel's future]. (Rom 9:1–5; Rom 10:1; Acts 4:27–28; John 10:17–18; Rom 11:18–20; Rom 11:25–27)

### 🗡️ JEWISH OFFENSE

#### PLAY 1: Who is Isaiah talking about? (the Servant of Isaiah 53)
- **Opener:** *"Would you read a passage from the prophet Isaiah with me and tell me who you think it describes?"*
- **Or open with:** *"I'm a Christian because of the Hebrew Scriptures. Can I show you the chapter that did it?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. **Isa 52:13–15**: The passage starts here, three verses before the chapter break. God says, "Behold, My Servant." He will be exalted, yet his appearance is marred more than any man. *"Have you read this before?"*
  2. **Isa 53:1–3**: He grows up like a root out of dry ground, with nothing to draw anyone to him. He is despised and rejected by his own people. *"Who is the 'we' that did not esteem him?"*
  3. **Isa 53:4–6**: He is pierced for our transgressions and crushed for our iniquities. All of us like sheep have gone astray, and Yahweh laid on him the iniquity of us all. *"Is he suffering for his own sins, or for someone else's?"*
  4. **Isa 53:7–9**: He is silent like a lamb led to slaughter, cut off from the land of the living for the transgression of "my people," given a grave with the wicked yet with a rich man in his death, though he had done no violence and spoken no deceit. *"If he dies for 'my people,' can he be the people?"*
  5. **Isa 53:10**: Yahweh was pleased to crush him, and he gives himself as a guilt offering. Then he sees his offspring and prolongs his days. *"How does a dead man prolong his days?"*
  6. **Isa 53:11–12**: The Righteous One, My Servant, will justify the many, for he will bear their iniquities. *"How does one man's suffering make many people righteous?"*
  7. **Acts 8:32–35**: An official from Ethiopia was reading this very passage and asked whom the prophet meant. Philip began from that Scripture and told him the good news about Jesus. That is the pattern.
- **If they say →**
  - *"The servant is Israel."* → Isaiah does call Israel God's servant (Isa 41:8), a servant he then calls blind and deaf (Isa 42:19). But in Isa 49:5–6 the Servant is the one who brings Jacob back and raises up the tribes of Israel. One restores the other. And this Servant is innocent (Isa 53:9), while Isaiah's Israel is "a people weighed down with iniquity" (Isa 1:4). *"When did Israel suffer silently, willingly and sinlessly, to pay for someone else's sins?"*
  - *"That is a Christian reading."* → The Aramaic Targum, read in synagogues, renders Isa 52:13 "Behold, My servant the Messiah" [Targum Jonathan]. The Talmud gives Messiah a name from Isa 53:4 [Sanhedrin 98b]. Reading the servant as the nation became the standard answer with Rashi, in the eleventh century, a thousand years after Jesus. *"Why did the reading change?"*
  - *"We never read that in synagogue."* → That is true. The weekly readings from the Prophets stop at Isa 52:12 and pick up again at Isa 54:1. Don't accuse anyone of hiding it. Just say, *"Then let's read it now."*
  - *"He was just a man who died. Many Jews were crucified."* → Only one was foretold as dying for others' sins, buried in a rich man's tomb, and alive afterward. Go to [The resurrection](/topic/the-resurrection).
- **Closer:** *"Isaiah wrote it in the past tense, as Israel looking back and saying, 'We thought God had struck him, but he was pierced for our transgressions.' Zechariah says that day is coming for the whole nation. It can be today for you."* (Zech 12:10; Zech 13:1; Isa 53:4–5)

#### PLAY 2: Where is the atonement? (sin, sacrifice and no temple)
- **Opener:** *"On Yom Kippur, how do you know your sins are actually forgiven?"*
- **Or open with:** *"The Torah gives a whole book to sacrifice. Since the temple fell, what takes its place, and who said so?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. **Isa 1:4–6; Isa 59:2**: Isaiah begins by telling the covenant people they are a sinful nation, sick from head to foot, and that their iniquities have separated them from God. Being Abraham's child did not exempt them. *"Was Isaiah wrong about them, or is he right about all of us?"*
  2. **Ps 143:2; Eccl 7:20; Isa 64:6**: No one living is righteous before God. There is not a righteous man on earth who never sins. Our righteous deeds are like a filthy garment. *"If the good deeds are stained too, what do you bring?"*
  3. **Lev 17:11**: The life of the flesh is in the blood, and God gave it on the altar to make atonement. *"God chose the means. Can we choose another?"*
  4. **Lev 16:15–16, 21–22**: On the Day of Atonement one goat dies and the sins of the people are confessed over the head of the other, which carries them away. An innocent substitute bears the guilt. *"Where is that substitute today?"*
  5. **Dan 9:26**: The Anointed One is cut off and has nothing, and then the city and the sanctuary are destroyed. The temple has been gone since AD 70. *"Would God take away the sacrifices and leave nothing in their place?"*
  6. **Isa 53:10**: The Servant gives himself as a guilt offering. The animal sacrifices were pictures; he is what they pictured. (Heb 10:4; Heb 10:11–14)
  7. **Jer 31:31–34**: God promised a new covenant, not like the one made at Sinai, in which He forgives iniquity and remembers sin no more. *"Has that covenant come? Through whom?"*

  - *"Repentance, prayer and charity atone now."* → Scripture commands all three, and never says they replace the blood. Daniel repented and prayed in exile, and still confessed that he had no righteousness of his own to plead, only God's great compassion (Dan 9:18). *"Where does God say He changed the means of atonement?"*
  - *"God doesn't want sacrifice. Hosea 6:6, Psalm 51:16."* → Those verses rebuke sacrifice offered with a heart far from God. David's psalm ends with God delighting in righteous sacrifices on His altar (Ps 51:19). God was not cancelling Leviticus; He was demanding a broken heart with it.
  - *"The prayers of our lips replace the bulls. Hosea 14:2."* → Hosea calls Israel to return with words of confession. He does not say words pay for sin. Someone still has to bear it. (Isa 53:6)

  - *"I'm Jewish. I'm already in the covenant."* → Isaiah, Jeremiah and John the Baptist all preached repentance to people in the covenant. God requires a circumcised heart. (Deut 10:16; Jer 4:4; Matt 3:9; Rom 2:28–29)

  2. **Isa 9:6–7**: A child is born, a son is given, who sits on David's throne forever, and his name is Mighty God. *"Is that title ever given to a mere man?"*

  4. **Dan 7:13–14**: One like a Son of Man comes with the clouds of heaven, and all peoples serve him in a kingdom that never ends. That service belongs to God alone.

  6. **Dan 9:24–26**: He comes to atone for iniquity, he is cut off, and then the temple is destroyed. *"The temple was destroyed in AD 70. Who came before that, claimed to be Messiah, died, and is worshiped by a billion Gentiles as Isaiah 49:6 said?"*
  7. **Zech 12:10**: God pours out the Spirit of grace on the house of David, and "they will look on Me whom they have pierced" and mourn as for an only son. The One pierced is the One speaking.
- **If they say →**
  - *"The Messiah brings world peace. There is no peace, so Jesus is not the Messiah."* → He will bring it.   - *"God is not a man. Numbers 23:19."* → That verse says God does not lie or change His mind the way a man does. It does not say God cannot take on flesh. He appeared to Abraham and ate with him (Gen 18:1–2), and Isaiah calls the child born "Mighty God" (Isa 9:6).
  - *"The Messiah is a man, not God."* → He is both. Go to [The Trinity](/topic/the-trinity) and use Ps 110:1, Isa 9:6 and Dan 7:13–14 from this play.
  - *"Isaiah 7:14 says 'young woman,' not 'virgin.'"* → The word is *almah*, a young unmarried woman, and Jewish translators before Jesus was born rendered it in Greek with the word for virgin [Septuagint]. - **Closer:** *"Moses said God would raise up a Prophet like him, and that God would hold accountable anyone who would not listen to him. I believe He has come. # 🕉️ HINDUS

**Their position:** Hinduism is a family of traditions, not one creed. Common ground: ultimate reality is Brahman, and the true self (atman) is one with it, "You are that" [Chandogya Upanishad 6.8.7]. The many gods are faces of the one reality, and many Hindus are devoted to one of them, such as Vishnu, Shiva or Krishna. Every soul is reborn again and again (samsara), and karma, the moral weight of your deeds, decides the next life. The goal is release (moksha), reached by the path of works, the path of knowledge or the path of devotion. Truth is one and the paths are many, so Jesus is welcome as one more teacher or avatar among others; Krishna says he comes age after age [Bhagavad Gita 4.7–8].

**The dividing issue:** Who God is, and how guilt is removed. Scripture says God is the personal Creator, distinct from everything He made, who will not share His glory with images. And it says sin is not a weight to be worked off over many lives but guilt before a holy Judge, which only a substitute can bear. John MacArthur puts the line where Scripture does: "all false religion is demon worship" [MacArthur, pastors' session on 2 Corinthians 6]. That is a statement about the system and what stands behind the idol, not an insult to the person in front of you. (Isa 45:5–6; Rom 1:22–25; 1 Cor 10:19–20; Heb 9:27)

**Ask this question:** *"How much karma do you have left to pay, and how would you ever know when it is paid?"* (Col 2:13–14)

**Scriptural response:** In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth: He is not the world and the world is not He. He says, "I am Yahweh, and there is no other." Idols have mouths and cannot speak. It is appointed for men to die once, and after this comes judgment. And God canceled the record of debt against us by nailing it to the cross. (Gen 1:1; Isa 45:5–6; Ps 115:4–8; Heb 9:27; Col 2:13–14)

**Common objection:** *"All paths lead to the same God. Truth is one; the wise call it by many names."* → That is itself one path's claim, and it says every path that disagrees is wrong. The paths do not even agree on whether God is personal. Jesus did not offer Himself as one of many: "No one comes to the Father but through Me." If He was wrong, He is not a great teacher. If He was right, the other paths do not arrive. See [The exclusivity of Christ](/topic/the-exclusivity-of-christ). (John 14:6; Acts 4:12; Isa 43:10–11)

**Gospel bridge:** *"Karma says you get what you deserve. What if someone took what you deserve, once, and gave you what He deserves?"* Christ suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, to bring us to God. Not many lives of paying: one death, one resurrection, and rest. (1 Pet 3:18; Heb 9:26–28; Rom 6:23; Matt 11:28)

**Where we agree:** This world is not all there is. Our actions have moral weight, and a man reaps what he sows. Something is wrong with us that we cannot shake. Devotion, discipline, family and reverence matter. Many Hindus are warm, hospitable and glad to talk about God.

**Approach:** A Hindu will usually agree with everything you say about Jesus and simply add Him to the shelf. So the work is not getting Jesus accepted; it is showing that He will not be added. Don't open by attacking their gods or their food. Ask what they hope for after death and whether they are sure. Then tell them plainly who God is, the Creator who made them, as Paul did with idol-worshipers in Athens and Lystra. Expect family cost to be heavy: baptism can mean losing a family. # ☸️ BUDDHISTS

**Their position:** There is no Creator God to answer to. The Buddha taught Four Noble Truths: life is suffering (dukkha); suffering comes from craving; suffering ends when craving ends; and the way to end it is the Eightfold Path of right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration. There is no permanent self or soul (anatta). Beings are reborn according to karma until they reach nirvana, the "blowing out" of craving and of rebirth. Each person must do it himself: "By oneself is one purified… no one can purify another" [Dhammapada 165]; "You yourselves must strive; the Buddhas only point the way" [Dhammapada 276]. Many Western Buddhists keep the meditation and ethics and hold the rest loosely.

**The dividing issue:** What is wrong with us, and who can fix it. Buddhism says the problem is desire and the cure is to put it out by your own effort. Scripture says the problem is sin against a holy God, that we cannot cure ourselves, and that God has provided a Savior. Buddhism has a path and no one to forgive you. (Rom 3:23; Jer 17:9; Rom 5:6–8; Acts 4:12)

**Ask this question:** *"When you do something wrong, who forgives you?"* (Ps 51:4)

**Scriptural response:** In the beginning God created, so there is Someone we answer to. He made man in His image, a real person who matters. Suffering came in through sin, and it is not an illusion to rise above: Jesus wept at a grave. The answer to desire is not to put it out but to have it filled: in God's presence is fullness of joy. And one day He will wipe away every tear. (Gen 1:1; Gen 1:27; Rom 5:12; John 11:35; Ps 16:11; Rev 21:4)

**Common objection:** *"Buddhism isn't a religion. It's just a practice for peace of mind."* → Then it has nothing to say about your guilt, your death or your Maker, and those are the questions that matter most. A calm mind on the way to judgment is not safety. Jesus gives peace with God first, and then the peace of God. (Heb 9:27; Rom 5:1; John 14:27; Phil 4:6–7)

**Gospel bridge:** *"The Buddha said, 'Strive.' His last words were to keep working at it. Jesus' last words on the cross were, 'It is finished.' Which do you need?"* Jesus said, "I am the way." He does not point to the path; He is the path, and He carries those who cannot walk it. (John 19:30; John 14:6; Matt 11:28–30; Eph 2:8–9)

**Where we agree:** Life in this world is marked by suffering, and nothing here lasts. Craving for things that cannot satisfy does make us miserable; Scripture says the same about covetousness and the love of the world. Compassion, self-control, honesty and a quiet mind are good. Death must be faced, not ignored.

**Approach:** Most Buddhists you will meet in America are either from Buddhist families (Thai, Vietnamese, Japanese, Chinese and others) or Westerners who came for meditation. Ask which, and ask what they actually believe; many have never thought about a Creator. Start with the Creator, as Paul did with people who had no Bible, and then the conscience. Honor their seriousness about suffering and show them the One who entered it. Don't mock the Buddha; he is dead and never claimed to save anyone. The contrast with a living Savior makes itself. (Acts 17:24–31; Rom 2:14–15; Heb 4:15; 1 Pet 3:15)

# ✨ NEW AGE / SPIRITUALISTS

**Their position:** There is no book and no creed, which is the point: "I'm spiritual, not religious." The common threads are these. All is one, and the divine is an energy or consciousness in everything, often called "the Universe." You are divine at your core, and your problem is not sin but forgetting who you are. You create your own reality by your thoughts (manifestation, the law of attraction). Truth is personal: "my truth." Guidance comes from within, and from tools such as astrology, tarot, crystals, energy healing, psychedelics, spirit guides, angels and the dead. Most believe in reincarnation and karma. Jesus is an enlightened teacher who reached "Christ consciousness," which anyone can reach.

**The dividing issue:** Who is God: the Creator, or you. Scripture draws a hard line between the Creator and everything He made, says the human heart is not divine but deceitful, and calls contact with spirits what it is. The first lie in the Bible is the New Age gospel in five words: "You will be like God." (Gen 3:4–5; Rom 1:25; Jer 17:9; Isa 45:5)

**Ask this question:** *"If your truth and my truth contradict each other, can they both be true? And how do you know the voice you are listening to is good?"* (1 John 4:1)

**Scriptural response:** God made the world; He is not the world, and neither are you. He forbids divination, fortune-telling, mediums and calling up the dead, because they are detestable to Him and because He has spoken. Satan disguises himself as an angel of light. There is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus. It is appointed for men to die once, and after this comes judgment. (Acts 17:24–25; Deut 18:10–12; Isa 8:19–20; 2 Cor 11:14; 1 Tim 2:5; Heb 9:27)

**Common objection:** *"I've had real experiences. I've felt the energy, and my readings come true."* → I believe you. The Bible never says these things are fake; it says they are forbidden. A slave girl in Philippi told fortunes accurately by a spirit, and Paul cast it out. Real is not the same as good. The test of a spirit is what it says about Jesus. (Acts 16:16–18; 1 John 4:1–3; Deut 13:1–3)

**Gospel bridge:** *"You have been told to look within. What if what is within is the problem, and the help has to come from outside you?"* You are not God, and that is good news: you don't have to hold your universe together. The God who made you came for you. He does not ask you to raise your vibration; He raises the dead. (Mark 7:21–23; Eph 2:4–5; Col 1:16–17; John 10:10)

**Where we agree:** There is more to reality than matter. The spiritual world is real. People are more than animals, the world's religion of money and status is empty, and a great deal of church life has been cold and hypocritical. We are made for something beyond ourselves.

**Approach:** This is probably the most common belief you will meet, often mixed with a church background. Many came to it through pain, illness or a bad church. Ask what they practice and what they were looking for when they started. Don't mock crystals; go to the heart: who is God, what is wrong with us, how do you know. Define every word, because they will say "Christ," "light," "spirit" and "energy" with other meanings. Take the spirits seriously, not fearfully: greater is He who is in you. Expect "that's your truth"; answer it once, kindly, and go back to Jesus. MacArthur's warning about the pull of false religion applies here in full, and he includes every system in it. (Col 2:8; 1 John 4:4; Acts 19:18–20; Jude 1:22–23)

# 🔥 SATANISTS

**Their position:** There are three kinds, and you need to know which one is in front of you. Most are atheists: the Church of Satan (Anton LaVey, 1966) does not believe in a real devil and uses Satan as a symbol of pride, appetite and being your own god; its first statement is "Satan represents indulgence instead of abstinence!" [LaVey, The Satanic Bible, 1969]. The Satanic Temple (2013) is also non-theistic and is mainly a political and activist group with seven tenets about reason, empathy and bodily autonomy, such as "One's body is inviolable, subject to one's own will alone" [The Satanic Temple, Tenet III]. A small minority are theistic: they believe Satan or Lucifer is a real being and honor or worship him, often mixed with occult practice. What all three share is the creed of the garden: no God over me; I decide good and evil.

**The dividing issue:** Who is God: the Creator, or the self. Satanism in every form is the worship of self, which is exactly what Scripture says the devil offered at the start and still offers. And Scripture says the devil is real whether a man believes in him or not: a created, fallen, lying, defeated enemy who has never loved anyone who served him. (Gen 3:4–5; Isa 14:13–14; John 8:44; Rom 1:25)

**Ask this question:** *"Jesus said everyone who sins is a slave of sin. Has doing whatever you want made you free, or does it own you?"* (John 8:34)

**Scriptural response:** Satan said in his heart, "I will make myself like the Most High," and was cast down. He is a liar and a murderer from the beginning, and he blinds the minds of the unbelieving. The Son of God appeared to destroy the works of the devil, disarmed the rulers and authorities at the cross, and through death rendered powerless him who had the power of death. The devil's end is the lake of fire, and it is shared by those who follow him. (Isa 14:12–15; John 8:44; 2 Cor 4:4; 1 John 3:8; Col 2:15; Heb 2:14–15; Rev 20:10; Matt 25:41)

**Common objection:** *"I don't believe in Satan or God. It's a symbol, a protest against religious hypocrisy and control."* → Then look at what you chose for a symbol: the first rebel, whose whole message was "be your own god." Scripture says a person does not have to believe in the devil to follow him; he only has to live for himself. All of us once walked that way. And hypocrisy is a reason to reject hypocrites, not Christ, who condemned them harder than you do. (Eph 2:1–3; 2 Cor 4:4; Matt 23:27–28)

**Gospel bridge:** *"The devil has never died for anyone. Christ did. Which master has actually loved you?"* God rescues people from the domain of darkness and transfers them to the kingdom of His beloved Son, in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins. No one has gone too far: people who practiced magic burned their books and believed. (Col 1:13–14; John 10:10–11; Acts 26:18; Acts 19:18–20; 1 Tim 1:15)

**Where we agree:** Religious hypocrisy and abuse are real and should be exposed. No one can be forced into faith; coerced religion is worthless. The individual matters, and honesty is better than pretending. Many of these people were truly wronged by someone who claimed Christ's name.

**Approach:** Do not be afraid and do not be shocked. Shock is often the point; meet it with calm and kindness. Greater is He who is in you than he who is in the world. Ask which kind they are and how they got there, because there is very often a story of church hurt, abuse or rejection behind it, and you should listen to it. With the atheist kinds, use this page's first play together with the [Atheist](/atheist) plays and [Morality and justice](/topic/morality-and-justice): they are atheists in costume. With the theistic and occult kind, take the danger seriously and keep to the gospel. We do not use rituals or formulas of deliverance, and we do not rail at the devil; even Michael the archangel said only, "The Lord rebuke you." Christ sets captives free through His word. Pray before, during and after: see [How to pray](/topic/how-to-pray). (1 John 4:4; 2 Tim 1:7; Jude 1:9; Eph 6:10–12; James 4:7)

# 🕊️ ONENESS PENTECOSTALS

**Their position:** There is one God, and He is one person. Father, Son and Holy Spirit are not three persons but three ways the one God has shown Himself, and His name is Jesus. In their words: "There is one God, who has revealed Himself as our Father, in His Son Jesus Christ, and as the Holy Spirit" [UPCI, Our Beliefs]. Many say "the Son" means the human nature of Christ, which began at Bethlehem, and "the Father" means the deity in Him. Full salvation is "repentance, baptism in water by immersion in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and the baptism of the Holy Ghost with the initial sign of speaking with other tongues" [UPCI, Fundamental Doctrine].

**The dividing issue:** Two things. First, who God is: Scripture shows the Father, the Son and the Spirit loving, sending and speaking to one another, which one person playing three parts cannot do. Second, how a sinner is saved: they add a baptismal formula and tongues to faith in Christ. (John 17:5; Matt 3:16–17; Eph 2:8–9)

**Ask this question:** *"When Jesus prayed, 'Father, glorify Me with the glory I had with You before the world was,' who was He talking to, and when did He have that glory?"* (John 17:5)

**Scriptural response:** At Jesus' baptism the Son is in the water, the Spirit descends, and the Father speaks from heaven. The Word was **with** God and **was** God. The Father sent the Son, and the Son sends another Helper. The Trinity is what you get when you believe all of it: one God, three persons. (Matt 3:16–17; John 1:1–2; John 14:16–17; Gal 4:4–6; 2 Cor 13:14)

**Common objection:** *"'Hear, O Israel, Yahweh is one.' You believe in three gods."* → We confess that verse with you. One God, one Being. The question is whether the one God is one person or three, and that has to come from the rest of Scripture, where the Father and the Son speak to and love each other. (Deut 6:4; John 17:24; John 8:17–18)

**Gospel bridge:** *"If you died tonight, what would you be counting on: Christ's finished work, or that you were baptized with the right words and spoke in tongues?"* The Son the Father sent did everything needed. A sinner is declared right by faith in Him alone. (Rom 4:4–5; John 3:16; Acts 16:31)

**Where we agree:** There is one God. Jesus Christ is fully God and fully man, born of a virgin, crucified, bodily risen and coming again. The Bible is God's word. They take holiness seriously and are often zealous and sincere.

**Approach:** They love the name of Jesus, so start there with warmth. They have usually been taught that the Trinity means three gods, so say plainly what you believe first: one Being, three persons, "one what and three who's." Then stay in John's Gospel and let Jesus' own words about His Father carry it. James White, *The Forgotten Trinity*, ch. 11, is the best short treatment. Don't mock tongues or their standards of dress; keep to who God is and what saves. See [The Trinity](/topic/the-trinity).

  - *"That was His human nature praying to His divine nature."* → Natures don't pray; persons do. And John 17:5 speaks of glory shared **before the world was**, before there was any humanity to pray. *"Who is 'Me' and who is 'You' before Bethlehem?"*
  - *"'I and the Father are one' (John 10:30)."* → Amen, one in being. But the verb is plural: "I and the Father, **we are** one." He did not say "I am the Father." And the verse before distinguishes "My hand" and "My Father's hand."
  - *"'He who has seen Me has seen the Father' (John 14:9)."* → The next verse reads, "I am in the Father, and the Father is in Me… the Father abiding in Me does His works." He reveals the Father perfectly because He is God the Son. He does not say He is the Father. (John 1:18)
  - *"Isaiah 9:6 calls Him 'Everlasting Father.'"* → It is a title of the Messiah-King: literally "Father of eternity," the one who is eternal and cares for His people as a father. The same book has Yahweh sending His Servant and His Spirit (Isa 48:16). Nowhere does the New Testament call Jesus "the Father."
- **Closer:** *"The Father so loved the world that He gave His Son. That gift only means something if the Son is someone the Father could give. Do you know Him as He has shown Himself?"* (John 3:16; 1 John 2:23)

#### PLAY 2: The Son before Bethlehem (the eternal Son)
- **Opener:** *"Did the Son of God exist before Mary conceived?"*
- **Or open with:** *"Who made all things: the Father alone, or the Father through the Son?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. **John 1:1–3, 14**: The Word was with God, was God, made all things, and became flesh. *"Is the Word a 'He' who was with God, or only a plan in God's mind?"*
  2. **Col 1:13–17**: All things were created through the **Son** and for Him, and He is before all things. *"Which Son made the world?"*
  3. **Heb 1:1–3, 8–10**: God made the world through His Son, and says **to the Son**, "Your throne, O God, is forever." *"Who is speaking to whom?"*
  4. **John 6:38; John 3:13**: "I have come down from heaven, not to do My own will, but the will of Him who sent Me." *"Who came down, and who sent Him?"*
  5. **Phil 2:5–7**: He existed in the form of God and then took the form of a slave. *"Who decided to humble Himself before becoming man?"*
  6. **Gal 4:4**: "God sent forth His Son, born of a woman." *"Was He the Son when He was sent, or only after He arrived?"*
  7. **John 11:41–42**: At Lazarus' tomb Jesus thanks the Father aloud "so that they may believe that You sent Me." Abner Chou's point on "sent" in John: what is sent already exists, no one sends himself, and the one sent represents the sender. So the Son existed before Bethlehem, is distinct from the Father, and bears His full authority. *"Can a person send himself?"*
- **If they say →**
  - *"'The Son' is the humanity that began at Bethlehem (Luke 1:35)."* → Luke 1:35 says why the child would be **called** Son of God. It does not say the Son began then. The Son made the world (Col 1:16; Heb 1:2). A human nature that did not yet exist made nothing.
  - *"The Word was God's thought or plan, not a person."* → A plan is not "with God" face to face, does not make all things, and is not called "He" and "the only begotten from the Father." (John 1:2–3, 14, 18)
  - *"God is a Spirit, and Jesus is that Spirit in flesh."* → God is spirit, yes. And the Spirit is sent by the Father and the Son and is called "another" Helper (John 14:16; John 15:26). Jesus is the Son incarnate, not the Father or the Spirit incarnate.
- **Closer:** *"The One who died for sinners is the eternal Son, who was with the Father before the world was and came down out of love. Will you trust Him as He is?"* (John 17:24; Gal 2:20)

#### PLAY 3: What saves? (Acts 2:38, the formula and tongues)
- **Opener:** *"If a man repents and trusts Christ on his deathbed and never gets to the water, is he saved?"*
- **Or open with:** *"Is a person saved by what Christ did, or by Christ plus being baptized with the right words?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. **Eph 2:8–9**: By grace, through faith, not of works. *"Is baptism something done, or something believed?"*
  2. **Rom 4:4–5**: God justifies the one who does not work but believes. *"When is he counted righteous?"*
  3. **Acts 10:43–48**: Peter preaches forgiveness to everyone who believes. The Spirit falls while he is still speaking, **before** anyone is baptized. *"Were they God's people before the water or after?"*
  4. **Acts 16:30–31**: "What must I do to be saved?" "Believe in the Lord Jesus." *"What did Paul leave out?"*
  5. **1 Cor 1:14–17**: Paul thanks God he baptized few of them: "Christ did not send me to baptize, but to proclaim the gospel." *"Could he say that if baptism were part of the gospel?"*
  6. **1 Cor 12:13, 29–30**: All believers were baptized by one Spirit into one body, and "All do not speak with tongues, do they?" *"If every believer has the Spirit and not all speak in tongues, can tongues be the proof?"*
  7. **Matt 28:19**: Jesus' own command: "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit." *"Whose words should a church baptize with?"*
- **If they say →**
  - *"Acts 2:38: be baptized 'for the forgiveness of your sins.'"* → Peter's very next sermons promise forgiveness to those who repent and believe, with no mention of baptism (Acts 3:19; Acts 10:43). Baptism is the public confession of the repentance that receives forgiveness. Cornelius had the Spirit before the water.
  - *"The apostles always baptized in Jesus' name, never 'Father, Son and Spirit.'"* → "In the name of" means by the authority of and with reference to Jesus, as against John's baptism or a Jewish washing. Luke words it four different ways (Acts 2:38; Acts 8:16; Acts 10:48; Acts 19:5), which is not how anyone records a fixed formula.
  - *"Matthew 28:19 says 'name,' singular, and that name is Jesus."* → One name because one God. Then three are listed, each with "the": the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit. That is the Trinity in one sentence.
  - *"You haven't received the Holy Ghost unless you've spoken in tongues."* → Anyone who does not have the Spirit does not belong to Christ at all, and every believer is sealed with the Spirit when he believes. (Rom 8:9; Eph 1:13–14) Our church also holds that the sign gifts ceased with the apostles.
  - *"John 3:5: born of water and the Spirit."* → Jesus expected Nicodemus, a teacher of Israel, to know this from the Old Testament: clean water and a new Spirit in Ezekiel 36:25–27. It is the new birth God gives, and the same chapter says whoever believes has eternal life. (John 3:10, 16)
- **Closer:** *"Christ said, 'It is finished.' You can rest on that today, without one more step to make it count. Will you turn from sin and trust Him alone?"* (John 19:30; Rom 5:1)

# 🌈 PROGRESSIVE CHRISTIANS

**Their position:** There is no creed, on purpose: belief matters less than behavior. Jesus is a teacher and example whose way leads to wholeness, and other religions are valid paths too; their own statement says "the Spirit moves in beneficial ways in many faith traditions" [ProgressiveChristianity.org, Core Values]. The Bible is a human record of people's experience of God, inspiring but mistaken in places and to be corrected by modern knowledge and experience. Their own writers say many of them reject original sin, hell, the virgin birth, the bodily resurrection, and that Jesus died bearing God's wrath in the sinner's place. Sin is mostly injustice in society, salvation is healing here and now, and love means affirming people as they are.

**The dividing issue:** Authority, and then the gospel. Who decides what is true: God speaking in Scripture, or me and my generation sitting in judgment on Scripture? Once the Bible is negotiable, sin shrinks, the cross loses its purpose, and what is left is a different gospel with Christian words. J. Gresham Machen made the case a century ago in *Christianity and Liberalism*: this is not a variety of Christianity but another religion. (2 Tim 3:16–17; John 17:17; Gal 1:6–9; 1 Cor 15:3–4)

**Ask this question:** *"When you and the Bible disagree, which one gives way, and how did you decide that?"* (Isa 8:20)

**Scriptural response:** Jesus treated Scripture as the unbreakable word of God, down to the smallest stroke. He taught that He is the only way to the Father, that He came to give His life as a ransom, and He warned of hell more than anyone in the Bible. You cannot keep His ethics and discard His claims; they come from the same mouth. The apostles handed on as of first importance that Christ died for our sins and was raised. (John 10:35; Matt 5:17–18; John 14:6; Mark 10:45; Matt 25:46; 1 Cor 15:3–4)

**Common objection:** *"God is love. A loving God doesn't need blood to forgive, and wouldn't send anyone to hell."* → Scripture says God is love in the same letter that says He sent His Son to be the propitiation for our sins. That is how we know what love is. A God who shrugs at evil is not loving; ask anyone who has been wronged. At the cross justice and mercy meet: God is just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus. (1 John 4:8–10; Rom 3:25–26; Rom 5:8; Ex 34:6–7)

**Gospel bridge:** *"If Jesus is mainly an example, the news is: try harder to be like Him. How is that going? The gospel is that He did what we have not done and bore what we deserve. Would you rather have a model you can't match, or a Savior?"* (Rom 3:23–24; 2 Cor 5:21; 1 Pet 2:24; Eph 2:8–9)

**Where we agree:** Jesus is worth following. Hypocrisy, greed and abuse in churches are real evils, and Jesus condemned them more fiercely than any critic. Christians must care for the poor, the widow, the orphan and the stranger. Love of neighbor is the second great commandment. Honest questions deserve honest answers.

**Approach:** Many are here because they were hurt, or because nobody answered their questions. Listen first and ask what happened. Do not defend what was truly sinful in a church; agree with Scripture against it. Then separate two things: what Christians did, and what Christ said. Keep bringing the conversation back to Jesus' own words, because they still claim Him. Define terms as you go: they use "gospel," "love," "resurrection" and "inspired" with different meanings, so ask, "What do you mean by that?" Don't get pulled into politics. The issue is authority and the cross. Speak the truth in love, with gentleness, as one sinner saved by grace to another person God made. (Eph 4:15; 2 Tim 2:24–26; Jude 1:3; 1 Pet 3:15)

### 📇 Progressive Christian Cheat Card
1. **John 10:35**: *"Jesus said Scripture cannot be broken. Was He wrong?"*
2. **John 14:6**: *"Did Jesus say He is a way, or the way?"*

4. **1 John 4:10**: *"How does John define love here? What word does he use for the cross?"*

6. **Gal 1:8**: *"Is there such a thing as a different gospel? What does Paul say about it?"*

<a id="prog-offense"></a>
### 🗡️ PROGRESSIVE CHRISTIAN OFFENSE

#### PLAY 1: Which Jesus? (the Jesus of the Gospels, all of Him)
- **Opener:** *"You follow Jesus. What do you think was the most important thing He taught?"*
- **Or open with:** *"If Jesus said something you disagreed with, would you change your mind or would He be wrong?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. **Matt 5:17–18**: Jesus said not the smallest letter or stroke will pass from the Law until all is accomplished. **John 10:35**: "the Scripture cannot be broken." *"What was Jesus' view of the Bible?"*
  2. **Matt 19:4–6**: Asked about marriage, He went to Genesis as history and as God's own voice: "Have you not read… He who created them from the beginning made them male and female." *"Did He treat Genesis as myth?"*
  3. **Mark 7:20–23**: He said evil comes from within, out of the heart of man. *"Did Jesus think people are basically good?"*
  4. **John 14:6; John 8:24**: "No one comes to the Father but through Me." "Unless you believe that I am He, you will die in your sins." *"Is that one path among many?"*
  5. **Matt 25:41, 46; Mark 9:47–48**: He spoke of eternal fire and eternal punishment. *"Who is speaking in these verses?"*
  6. **Mark 10:45; Matt 26:28**: He said He came to give His life as a ransom for many, and that His blood is poured out for forgiveness of sins. *"What did Jesus think His death was for?"*
  7. **Luke 24:25–27, 39**: Risen, with flesh and bones, He rebuked His disciples for being slow to believe all that the prophets have spoken.
- **If they say →**
  - *"The Gospel writers put those words in His mouth."* → Then on what basis do you keep the Sermon on the Mount? The same witnesses give us both. You cannot trust them for "love your enemies" and distrust them two chapters later. Go to [Is the Bible reliable?](/topic/is-the-bible-reliable).
  - *"I follow the red letters, not Paul."* → The red letters say Scripture cannot be broken, and Jesus personally chose and sent His apostles: "He who receives whomever I send receives Me." Peter calls Paul's letters Scripture. (John 13:20; 2 Pet 3:15–16; 1 Cor 14:37)
  - *"Jesus welcomed everyone and never judged."* → He welcomed sinners and called them to repent. To the woman caught in adultery: "Go, and from now on sin no more." He came to call sinners to repentance. (John 8:11; Luke 5:32; Mark 1:15)
  - *"That's your interpretation."* → Then read the verse aloud and tell me another meaning the words can bear. Go to [Faith and reason](/topic/faith-and-reason).
- **Closer:** *"Jesus asked, 'Why do you call Me, Lord, Lord, and do not do what I say?' The real Jesus is better than the edited one, because the real one can save. Will you take Him as He is?"* (Luke 6:46; John 6:68)

#### PLAY 2: What did the cross do? (substitution, not just an example)
- **Opener:** *"Why did Jesus have to die? What did His death actually accomplish?"*
- **Or open with:** *"If Jesus had died of old age after a life of teaching, would anything be missing?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. **Rom 3:23; Rom 6:23**: All have sinned, and the wages of sin is death. *"Is that true of you?"* If sin is only "out there" in systems, use the commandments: go to [The Way of the Master](/topic/the-way-of-the-master).
  2. **Isa 53:5–6, 10**: The Servant is pierced for our transgressions, Yahweh lays our iniquity on him, and Yahweh is pleased to crush him. *"Whose idea was the cross?"*
  3. **John 10:17–18**: "No one takes My life from Me. I lay it down of Myself." The Son is not a victim of the Father. He is God the Son, giving Himself.
  4. **2 Cor 5:21; 1 Pet 2:24**: He who knew no sin was made sin on our behalf. He bore our sins in His body on the tree. *"What happened to those sins?"*
  5. **Rom 3:25–26**: God put Christ forward as a propitiation, a sacrifice that satisfies His wrath, so that He would be both just and the justifier. *"How can God forgive and still be just? This is how."*
  6. **1 Pet 2:21–24**: Yes, He is our example. Peter says so, and in the same breath says He bore our sins. Scripture gives both; the progressive version keeps one.
  7. **Gal 2:21**: If righteousness comes through what we do, then Christ died needlessly.
- **If they say →**
  - *"Penal substitution is cosmic child abuse."* → That picture has an angry Father and an unwilling third party. Scripture has one God: the Father giving His Son in love, and the Son laying down His own life. "God was in Christ reconciling the world to Himself." (John 3:16; John 10:18; 2 Cor 5:19; Gal 2:20)
  - *"That theory was invented by Anselm or the Reformers."* → Isaiah 53 was written seven centuries before Christ, and Paul says he received it and passed it on as of first importance: Christ died for our sins. (1 Cor 15:3; Rom 4:25)
  - *"The cross shows God's solidarity with victims."* → He does draw near to the broken. But solidarity does not take sin away. A doctor who catches your disease to keep you company has not cured you. (Heb 9:22; Heb 9:26)
  - *"A loving God just forgives."* → Even we don't believe that. When a judge frees the guilty we call it corruption. Go to [Morality and justice](/topic/morality-and-justice).
- **Closer:** *"On the cross He said, 'It is finished.' Not 'follow My example,' but paid. Are you trusting what He finished, or what you are doing?"* (John 19:30; Rom 4:4–5)

#### PLAY 3: Who decides? (Scripture, or me)
- **Opener:** *"How do you decide which parts of the Bible to keep?"*
- **Or open with:** *"Is there anything the Bible teaches that you don't like but believe anyway?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. **Gen 3:1**: The first temptation was a question: "Has God really said?" *"Is it possible that question is still being asked?"*
  2. **2 Tim 3:16–17; 2 Pet 1:20–21**: All Scripture is breathed out by God. Men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit. It is God's word through men, not men's guesses about God.
  3. **Jer 17:9; Prov 14:12**: The heart is deceitful above all else. There is a way that seems right to a man, and its end is death. *"If my heart is the judge, who checks the judge?"*
  4. **Judges 21:25**: Everyone did what was right in his own eyes. Scripture records that as a tragedy, not a goal.
  5. **Isa 5:20**: Woe to those who call evil good and good evil. Every generation is sure its own morals are the enlightened ones. *"Which generation got to be right, and why ours?"*
  6. **2 Tim 4:3–4**: A time will come when people gather teachers to suit their own desires and turn from the truth. *"How would a person know if that was happening to them?"*
  7. **Matt 4:4; John 17:17**: Jesus answered the devil with "It is written," and prayed, "Your word is truth."
- **If they say →**
  - *"The Bible was written by men of their time."* → By men, yes, carried along by the Spirit. Jesus quoted those men and said, "God said." If He is Lord, His view of the Bible settles it. (Matt 15:4; Matt 22:31)
  - *"The Bible is full of contradictions and errors."* → Ask for one, and look at it together. Go to [Is the Bible reliable?](/topic/is-the-bible-reliable).
  - *"The church used the Bible to defend slavery, and we corrected it. This is the same."* → Men twisted Scripture to defend man-stealing, which Scripture punishes with death, and it was Bible-believing Christians who led abolition by appealing to Scripture. That is the Bible correcting sinners. Today's revisions go the other way: correcting the Bible to suit the age. (Ex 21:16; 1 Tim 1:10; Gal 3:28)
  - *"The Spirit is still speaking and doing a new thing."* → The Spirit does not contradict what He has already said. Test every spirit by the word He gave. (1 John 4:1; Isa 8:20; Gal 1:8)
- **Closer:** *"If your god never disagrees with you, you may be worshiping an idealized version of yourself. The God who is there speaks, and He has not stuttered. Will you let Him be God?"* (Isa 55:8–9; Luke 6:46)

| **"The church hurt me. Christians are hypocrites."** | Listen, and grieve what was truly wrong. Jesus reserved His hardest words for religious hypocrites, so He agrees with you about them. But a counterfeit proves there is a real thing. Don't let people who disobeyed Christ keep you from Christ. | Matt 23:27–28; Ezek 34:2–4; Matt 11:28–30; John 6:68 | *"Was it Jesus who hurt you, or people who weren't acting like Him?"* |
| **"I'm deconstructing. I'm not sure what I believe."** | Asking questions is not sin. Test everything, and hold fast what is good. But examine the doubts as hard as the beliefs: many are tearing down a caricature. Offer to read one Gospel together and look at Jesus Himself. | 1 Thess 5:21; Acts 17:11; Jude 1:22; John 20:27–31 | *"What is the one question that, if it were answered, would matter most?"* |
| **"Faith is a journey. Certainty is arrogance."** | Humility is believing what God has said, not doubting it. John wrote "so that you may know that you have eternal life." It is not arrogant to take God at His word; the arrogance would be correcting Him. | 1 John 5:13; John 8:31–32; 2 Tim 1:12; Luke 1:3–4 | *"Are you certain that we can't be certain?"* |

**Typical exits:** "That's just your interpretation." (Ask: *"What do you think the verse means? Let's read it again."*) · "I can't believe in a God like that." (Answer gently: *"The question isn't which God we'd prefer, but which God is there. Would you read Mark with me?"*) · "You're being judgmental." (Say: *"I'm under the same verdict. I'm telling you where I found mercy."*) · "We'll have to agree to disagree." (Leave one verse, Mark 10:45, and the question, *"Why did Jesus say He came?"*)

# 📜 HEBREW ROOTS / TORAH OBSERVANT

**Their position:** They believe Jesus, whom they call Yeshua, is the Messiah, and most affirm He is God. What sets them apart is the claim that the Law of Moses is still binding on every believer, Jew and Gentile: the seventh-day Sabbath, the feasts of Leviticus 23, the food laws of Leviticus 11, and for some, circumcision and tassels. Their key texts are "I did not come to abolish the Law" (Matt 5:17–19) and "sin is lawlessness" (1 John 3:4). They say the church went pagan early, blaming Constantine for Sunday worship, Christmas and Easter, and that Paul has been misread. Most say they keep Torah out of obedience, not to be saved. There is no central body, so views range widely; a fringe goes on to reject Paul or the Trinity.

**The dividing issue:** Whether the covenant made at Sinai binds the Christian, and whether law-keeping is added to Christ for a believer's standing or growth. The apostles faced this exact question and answered it. To put believers back under that yoke is what Paul calls turning to a different gospel. (Acts 15:10–11; Gal 3:1–3; Gal 5:1–4; Col 2:16–17)

**Ask this question:** *"In Acts 15 the apostles met to decide exactly this: must Gentile believers keep the Law of Moses? What did they decide?"* (Acts 15:5; Acts 15:28–29)

**Scriptural response:** Peter called it a yoke that neither the fathers nor the apostles had been able to bear, and said we are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus. The Law was a tutor to lead us to Christ, and now that faith has come we are no longer under a tutor. Food, festivals, new moons and Sabbaths are a shadow; the substance belongs to Christ. Jesus declared all foods clean. The new covenant has made the first obsolete. (Acts 15:10–11; Gal 3:24–25; Col 2:16–17; Mark 7:18–19; Heb 8:13; Rom 10:4)

**Common objection:** *"Jesus said He did not come to abolish the Law. Not the smallest letter will pass away."* → He said He came to fulfill it, and that nothing would pass "until all is accomplished." Fulfilling is not abolishing, and it is not leaving unchanged. The Law and the Prophets pointed to Him, and He brought them to their goal. You already agree some of it is no longer practiced, since you do not offer sacrifices. The only question is who has authority to say what changed, and the answer is Christ and His apostles. (Matt 5:17–18; Luke 24:44; Rom 10:4; Heb 10:1)

**Gospel bridge:** *"When you fail to keep it, and you do, where is your standing with God?"* Paul says that whoever takes on circumcision is obligated to keep the whole Law, and that if righteousness comes through the Law, Christ died needlessly. Jesus offers a different yoke: "Come to Me… and you will find rest for your souls." There is now no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus. (Gal 5:3; Gal 2:16; Gal 2:21; Matt 11:28–30; Rom 8:1–4)

**Where we agree:** The Old Testament is the word of God and profitable for every believer. Jesus is the Jewish Messiah, and the church has often neglected the Hebrew Scriptures. The Law is holy, righteous and good. Grace is not permission to sin, and those who love Christ obey Him. God is not finished with Israel.

**Approach:** Find out which kind you are talking to, because Scripture treats them differently. One who enjoys the feasts as pictures of Christ and binds no one is using his freedom, and Romans 14 says to leave him alone. One who tells other believers they must keep Torah is doing what Paul opposed in Galatians. One who makes it necessary for salvation, or denies the Trinity or Paul, has another gospel. They usually know the Old Testament better than the average Christian, so know Acts 15, Galatians, Colossians 2 and Hebrews 7–10 before you start, and never mock the Law. Our own position: the church is distinct from Israel and is not under the Mosaic covenant but under the law of Christ. (Rom 14:5–6; Gal 2:4–5; Gal 1:6–9; 1 Cor 9:20–21; Gal 6:2)

### 🗡️ HEBREW ROOTS OFFENSE

#### PLAY 1: What did the apostles decide? (Acts 15 and Galatians)
- **Opener:** *"Has the question of Gentiles and the Law of Moses ever been officially settled? Where?"*
- **Or open with:** *"Do you keep Torah to be right with God, or because you are right with God? And what about a believer who doesn't?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. **Acts 15:1, 5**: The claim on the table: it is necessary to circumcise them and to direct them to keep the Law of Moses. That is the Torah-observant position, stated by believers.
  2. **Acts 15:7–11**: Peter answers. God gave the Gentiles the Spirit and cleansed their hearts by faith. "Why do you put God to the test by placing upon the neck of the disciples a yoke which neither our fathers nor we have been able to bear?" We are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus, in the same way as they.
  3. **Acts 15:19–20, 28–29**: The decision: do not trouble the Gentiles who are turning to God. "It seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us to lay upon you no greater burden than these essentials." Four things, for the sake of fellowship with Jews. No Sabbath, no feasts, no food laws.
  4. **Acts 21:25**: Years later the same decision is repeated, with nothing added.
  5. **Gal 2:3–5**: Titus, a Greek, was not compelled to be circumcised, and Paul did not yield for an hour, "so that the truth of the gospel would remain with you."
  6. **Gal 2:11–14**: Paul opposed Peter to his face for pressuring Gentiles "to live like Jews." *"What was Peter doing wrong, in Paul's eyes?"*
  7. **Gal 3:1–3; Gal 5:1–4**: "Having begun by the Spirit, are you now being perfected by the flesh?" Do not be subject again to a yoke of slavery. You who are seeking to be justified by law have been severed from Christ.
- **If they say →**
  - *"Acts 15:21 says Moses is read every Sabbath, so the Gentiles would learn the rest in time."* → That verse gives the reason for the four requests: Jews in every city would be offended otherwise. The letter itself says "no greater burden," and Acts 21:25 repeats it years later with nothing added. If more was coming, the apostles never sent it. (Acts 15:28; Acts 21:25)
  - *"Galatians is about circumcision for salvation, or man-made rules, not God's Torah."* → Paul names the covenant: the Law that came four hundred and thirty years after Abraham, from Mount Sinai. And he rebukes them for observing days, months, seasons and years. (Gal 3:17; Gal 4:9–11; Gal 4:24–25)
  - *"We keep Torah out of love. 'If you love Me, keep My commandments.'"* → Good, if it is truly not for standing with God. But Jesus said what His commandment is: believe in Him and love one another. And growth, like salvation, is by the Spirit and not by the Law. If you teach others they must, you bind what the apostles loosed. (John 14:15; 1 John 3:23; John 13:34; Rom 7:6)
  - *"Paul himself kept the Law. He took vows in the temple."* → He tells us why: "To the Jews I became as a Jew… though not being myself under the Law," to win them. He was free to keep customs and free not to, and he says he is under the law of Christ. (Acts 21:23–26; 1 Cor 9:19–21)
- **Closer:** *"The Holy Spirit and the apostles said 'no greater burden.' Christ says, 'My yoke is easy.' Why would you take up a yoke He lifted?"* (Acts 15:28; Matt 11:28–30; Gal 5:1)

#### PLAY 2: Shadow and substance (Sabbath, feasts, food and the new covenant)
- **Opener:** *"What do you think the feasts and the Sabbath were pointing to?"*
- **Or open with:** *"Jesus is from the tribe of Judah. Under the Torah, can a man from Judah be a priest?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. **Col 2:16–17**: "Let no one judge you in food or drink or in respect to a festival or a new moon or a Sabbath day, things which are a mere shadow of what is to come; but the substance belongs to Christ." The whole calendar, yearly, monthly and weekly. *"Who is judging whom here, if I am told I must keep them?"*
  2. **Heb 10:1**: The Law has a shadow of the good things to come and not the very form of things. You don't embrace a shadow when the person has arrived.
  3. **Heb 7:11–14**: Our Lord descended from Judah, a tribe Moses never connected with priests. "When the priesthood is changed, of necessity there takes place a change of law also." *"If the Law is unchanged, Jesus cannot be your High Priest. If He is your High Priest, the Law has changed."*
  4. **Heb 8:6–13**: Jesus mediates a better covenant. God promised a new one, "not like the covenant which I made with their fathers," and in calling it new He has made the first obsolete.
  5. **Mark 7:18–19; Rom 14:14**: Nothing that goes into a man from outside can defile him. "Thus He declared all foods clean." Paul is persuaded in the Lord Jesus that nothing is unclean in itself.
  6. **Rom 14:5–6**: One person regards one day above another, another regards every day alike. Each must be fully convinced in his own mind. That sentence could not be written if the Sabbath were binding.
  7. **Eph 2:14–15; 2 Cor 3:7–11**: Christ made Jew and Gentile one new man, abolishing in His flesh the law of commandments contained in ordinances. The ministry engraved on stones was glorious and is fading; what remains is more glorious.
  8. **Heb 4:9–10; Matt 11:28**: The Sabbath rest that remains is entered by resting from our works. The day pointed to a Person.
- **If they say →**
  - *"Peter's vision in Acts 10 was about people, not food."* → It was about people, and the food laws were the wall between them. That is why Peter was then criticized for eating with Gentiles. And Mark 7:19 speaks of food directly. (Acts 10:28; Acts 11:2–3; Mark 7:19)
  - *"The Sabbath is from creation and is a sign forever."* → Scripture says it is a sign between God and the sons of Israel. No one is commanded to keep it before Sinai, the apostles did not lay it on Gentiles, and Paul lists it among the shadows. (Ex 31:13; Ex 31:16–17; Col 2:16)
  - *"Constantine changed the Sabbath to Sunday."* → Christians were meeting on the first day in the New Testament itself, and Justin Martyr describes Sunday worship around AD 155, more than a century and a half before Constantine's edict of 321. (Acts 20:7; 1 Cor 16:2; Rev 1:10)
  - *"Sin is lawlessness, so we must keep Torah."* → Lawlessness is rebellion against God, and every Christian must flee it. The same letter says what His commandment is. Love fulfills the Law, and the Law's righteous requirement is fulfilled in those who walk by the Spirit. (1 John 3:4; 1 John 3:23; Rom 13:8–10; Rom 8:3–4)
  - *"The new covenant is the Torah written on the heart."* → Jeremiah says it is "not like" the Sinai covenant, and Hebrews draws the conclusion: the first is obsolete, with a new priesthood and a once-for-all sacrifice. What God writes on the heart is His will, kept from the inside by His Spirit. (Jer 31:31–33; Heb 8:13; Ezek 36:26–27)
- **Closer:** *"Everything you love in the Torah was pointing at Him. The Sabbath, the Passover lamb, the Day of Atonement, the priest: He is all of it. You have the substance. Why go back to the shadow?"* (Col 2:17; Luke 24:27; Heb 10:1)

# ⛪ ROMAN CATHOLICS

**Their position:** Revelation reaches us through Scripture *and* Sacred Tradition, and "both… must be accepted and honoured with equal sentiments of devotion and reverence" [CCC 82; Trent, Sess. 4]. Only the Magisterium (the pope and the bishops in communion with him) can interpret either one authentically [CCC 85, 100]. Justification "is conferred in Baptism" and "makes us inwardly just" [CCC 1992]. No one can merit the first grace, but the justified "can then merit for ourselves and for others… the attainment of eternal life" [CCC 2010]. The Mass is "one single sacrifice" with the cross, offered "in reparation for the sins of the living and the dead" [CCC 1367, 1414]. Mortal sin destroys sanctifying grace, and it is restored through confession to a priest [CCC 1446, 1456, 1861]. Those who die "imperfectly purified" undergo purgatory before heaven [CCC 1030–1031].

**The dividing issue:** How a sinner is made right with God, and who has the final word. Rome: justification is given in baptism, increased by the sacraments and your cooperation, lost by mortal sin, and finished after purgatory, on the authority of Scripture, Tradition and the Magisterium together. Scripture: God declares the ungodly righteous through faith alone, on the ground of Christ's finished work alone, and Scripture is the final authority. (Rom 4:4–5; Rom 5:1; Gal 2:16; Gal 2:21; 2 Tim 3:16–17)

**Ask this question:** *"If you stood before God tonight and He asked, 'Why should I let you into My heaven?' what would you say?"* (Rom 3:28)

**Scriptural response:** A man is justified by faith apart from works of the Law. To the one who does not work but believes, his faith is counted as righteousness. It is the gift of God, not of works. By one offering Christ has perfected for all time those who are being sanctified. There is now no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus. (Rom 3:28; Rom 4:4–5; Eph 2:8–9; Heb 10:10–14; John 19:30; Rom 8:1)

**Common objection:** *"James 2:24 says a man is justified by works and not by faith alone."* → James and Paul answer different questions. Paul asks how a sinner is declared right before God. James asks how a claim to faith is shown to be alive: "Show me your faith." Abraham was counted righteous when he believed, decades before he offered Isaac. Works are the fruit of saving faith, never the ground of it. (James 2:18; Gen 15:6; Rom 4:2–5; Eph 2:10)

**Gospel bridge:** *"The Mass is offered again and again, and purgatory still waits at the end. Do you know that you have eternal life?"* Jesus said, "It is finished." Whoever comes to Him He will never cast out, and having been justified by faith we have peace with God now. Will you rest on what He finished? (John 19:30; John 6:37; Rom 5:1; 1 John 5:13)

**Where we agree:** They confess one God in three Persons, the full deity and humanity of Christ, the virgin birth, the bodily resurrection, the return of Christ, and eternal judgment [CCC 1035, 1040]. They affirm that Scripture is inspired, and that "no one can merit the initial grace" [CCC 2010]. Say this plainly. The disagreement is not over *whether* Jesus is God; it is over *how a sinner is made right with Him*, and over *who has the final word*.

**Approach:** Most Catholics you'll talk to are family and friends who have never read the Catechism or Trent. Many are sincere and devout, and they are trusting a system that cannot save. Be gentle with the person and give no ground on the gospel. For the strongest statement of the case, see [MacArthur on Rome](/topic/macarthur-on-rome). The goal is "not just to win arguments, but more importantly to win souls". Two things keep it clear. First, they use the same words with a different meaning: "grace," "faith," and "justification" all mean something different in Rome. Define terms before you argue. Second, keep coming back to two questions: *What is your final authority?* and *Is Christ's work enough by itself?*.

### 🗡️ CATHOLIC OFFENSE

#### PLAY 1: Credited, not earned (justification)
- **Opener:** *"Do you know for sure that if you died today you'd be with God?"* (Most will say "I hope so" or "No one can know." The rest of the play explains why the answer can be yes.)
- **Or open with:** *"What do you think makes a person right with God: what Christ did, what we do, or both?"*
- **Or open with:** *"If God asked you, 'Why should I let you into My heaven?', what would you say?"* (Listen for "I" answers: I tried, I went to Mass, I was baptized. The only answer that stands is Christ and His righteousness.)
- **Chain:**
  1. **Rom 3:23–24**: All have sinned, and are justified as a *gift*, by His grace. *"Is a gift paid for in any part?"*
  2. **Rom 3:28**: Justified by faith *apart from* works of the Law. *"What does 'apart from' leave out?"*
  3. **Rom 4:4–5**: To the worker, wages are owed. To "the one who does not work, but believes," faith is credited as righteousness. *"Which one is Abraham?"*
  4. **Rom 4:6–8**: David speaks of righteousness credited "apart from works." *"Is that righteousness *in* David, or credited *to* David?"*
  5. **2 Cor 5:21; Phil 3:9**: Our sin was credited to Christ, and His righteousness to us, "not… my own… but… through faith." *"Whose righteousness do you want to stand in on Judgment Day?"*
  6. **Rom 5:1**: "Having been justified by faith, we have peace." *"Is that peace now, or only after purgatory?"*
  7. **Rom 6:23; Luke 18:9–14**: Death is a *wage*. Eternal life is a *free gift*. The tax collector brought no record, only a plea for mercy, and he went home justified. *"Can the same thing be both a wage you earn and a gift you're given?"*
- **If they say →**
  - *"'Works of the law' means only the Jewish ceremonies."* → Rom 4:4–5 talks about *any* work that earns wages. Eph 2:8–9 says "not of works," and Titus 3:5 says not by "deeds which we have done in righteousness." *"Are deeds done in righteousness ceremonies?"*
  - *"We believe in grace too. The Catechism says no one can merit the first grace."* → They will quote [CCC 2010]. Read the rest of it. The same paragraph says the justified then *merit* "the attainment of eternal life." Rom 11:6 says grace and works can't be mixed: "otherwise grace is no longer grace."
  - *"Trent only condemns a dead faith, with no cooperation."* → Read the canon they mean. Trent condemns anyone who says justification is "by faith alone… [so] that nothing else is required to co-operate" [Trent, Sess. 6, can. 9]. It also condemns justification by "the sole imputation of the justice of Christ" [can. 11]. That is exactly Rom 4:5. Sproul: if the Reformers were right, Trent anathematized the gospel.
  - *"Faith alone means you can live however you want."* → Saving faith comes with repentance and produces works (Eph 2:10; Titus 2:11–14; Rom 6:1–4). Works are the *fruit* of justification, not its *cause*. Trent condemns that very sentence [Sess. 6, can. 24].
  - *"That sounds too easy."* → It cost Christ everything; it costs us our pride. Paul had the best religious record of anyone, and he counted all of it as loss so that he could be found in Christ with a righteousness that was not his own (Phil 3:4–9). The jailer asked what he must do, and the answer was "Believe in the Lord Jesus" (Acts 16:30–31). *"What would you have to give up to come to God with empty hands?"*
- **Closer:** *"God's offer is Christ's righteousness credited to you the moment you turn from sin and trust Him alone (Mark 1:15; Rom 4:5). Not Christ plus your record. Would you trust Him that way today?"*

#### PLAY 2: Once for all (the Mass)
- **Opener:** *"When Jesus said 'It is finished' on the cross, what was finished?"* (John 19:30)
- **Or open with:** *"Is the sacrifice of the Mass a real sacrifice for sins?"* (A Catholic who knows the faith will say yes [Trent, Sess. 22, can. 1, 3].)
- **Chain:**
  1. **Heb 7:27**: Unlike the priests, He offered Himself "once for all." *"How many times?"*
  2. **Heb 9:25–28**: Not "often," or He would have to suffer often. He was "offered once to bear the sins of many." *"Can a sacrifice for sin be offered without suffering, according to v. 26?"*
  3. **Heb 10:11–12**: The priests "stand daily" offering sacrifices that never take away sins. Christ offered one sacrifice and "sat down." *"Why does the writer contrast standing with sitting?"*
  4. **Heb 10:14**: By one offering He "has perfected for all time" those who are sanctified. *"What's left for a priest to add?"*
  5. **Heb 10:18**: "Where there is forgiveness of these things, there is no longer any offering for sin." *"So what is offered on the altar each Sunday?"*
  6. **Heb 9:11–12; Rom 6:9–10**: He entered the holy place once, "having obtained eternal redemption," and He is "never to die again." Rome says that at each Mass "the work of our redemption is carried out" [CCC 1364]. *"Is redemption obtained, or still being carried out?"*
  7. **1 Cor 11:26; Luke 22:19**: The Supper *proclaims* His death "until He comes" and is done "in remembrance." *"Is a proclamation the same thing as an offering?"* At the table Jesus said, "given for you." *"In the upper room, who is giving to whom?"*
- **If they say →**
  - *"It's not a new sacrifice. It's the same sacrifice made present."* → That is what Rome says [CCC 1366–1367]. Trent still calls the Mass "propitiatory," "offered for the living and the dead, for sins" [Sess. 22, can. 3], and the Catechism says it is "offered in reparation for the sins of the living and the dead" [CCC 1414]. Heb 10:18 says that where forgiveness exists, *no* offering for sin remains. *"If sins still need an offering, were they forgiven at the cross?"*
  - *"Malachi 1:11 predicts a pure offering among the nations."* → In context it is the worship of God's name spreading among the Gentiles. The NT calls praise (Heb 13:15) and our bodies (Rom 12:1) sacrifices. It never calls the Supper a sacrifice for sin. (Inference from the NT pattern. The NT does not quote Mal 1:11.)
  - *"Jesus said 'This is My body.'"* → He also said "I am the door" (John 10:9). He said it while His body stood in front of them, holding the bread. Our church holds that the elements are representative, *and* that the Supper is a real communion with the risen Christ (1 Cor 10:16). See the John 6 row in the defense table.
- **Why it matters:** The English Reformers were burned chiefly over this doctrine. It isn't a side issue.
- **Closer:** *"Hebrews is clear that His sacrifice is never repeated. If His one offering is enough, you don't need another one. You need Him. Will you rest on what He finished (Heb 10:14)?"*

#### PLAY 3: One Mediator (Mary and the saints)
- **Opener:** *"When the disciples asked how to pray, who did Jesus tell them to talk to?"* (Matt 6:9: "Our Father.")
- **Or open with:** *"What did Mary herself tell people to do about Jesus?"* (John 2:5: "Whatever He says to you, do it.")
- **Chain:**
  1. **1 Tim 2:5**: "One mediator also between God and men, the man Christ Jesus." *"Did Paul leave room for a second?"*
  2. **Heb 4:14–16**: Because of our High Priest, *we* draw near to the throne "with confidence." *"Do we need someone to approach Jesus for us?"*
  3. **Heb 7:25**: He "always lives to make intercession." *"Is His intercession ever not enough?"*
  4. **Luke 11:27–28**: A woman blesses Mary's womb. Jesus answers, "On the contrary, blessed are those who hear the word of God and keep it." *"Why did Jesus redirect the praise?"*
  5. **Luke 1:47**: Mary calls God "my Savior." *"Who needs a Savior?"* (Rom 3:23)
  6. **Acts 10:25–26; Rev 22:8–9**: Peter and an angel both refuse religious bowing. "Worship God." *"If an apostle and an angel refused it, should Mary receive it?"*
  7. **Luke 1:38; Matt 12:46–50; Acts 1:14**: Mary calls herself the Lord's slave. Jesus says whoever does His Father's will is His brother and sister and mother. After the ascension she prays *with* the disciples, no one prays *to* her, and she is not named again. *"Where in the New Testament does anyone pray to Mary?"*
- **If they say →**
  - *"We don't worship Mary. We give her hyperdulia, not latria."* → The distinction is Rome's, not the Bible's. Scripture doesn't grade religious service this way: Paul uses the *dulia* word for serving the living God (1 Thess 1:9) and for the bondage of serving "those which by nature are no gods" (Gal 4:8). Rome does teach it, and Vatican II says her role "neither takes away from nor adds anything to" Christ's [LG 62]. Then ask where Scripture teaches *any* category of religious honor or prayer directed to a creature. Peter and the angel refused even the bowing (Acts 10:25–26; Rev 22:8–9).
  - *"Asking Mary to pray is like asking you to pray."* → Scripture commands believers on earth to pray for one another (James 5:16). It never models or commands praying to the departed. It warns against seeking the dead (Deut 18:10–11; Isa 8:19). Catholics would say the saints are alive in Christ, not "the dead," so present this as an inference. Also, a creature who hears millions of silent prayers at once would need a divine attribute.
  - *"The Church calls her Mediatrix [CCC 969], but in a subordinate way."* → *"Is 'one mediator' in 1 Tim 2:5 a ranking, or a number?"*
  - *"Rev 5:8 shows saints offering prayers in heaven."* → It shows the prayers of God's people presented before Him. It doesn't tell anyone to pray *to* the elders. Ask: *"Who is the prayer addressed to?"*
- **Closer:** *"Jesus said, 'Come to Me, all who are weary' (Matt 11:28). Mary pointed to Him too (John 2:5). Will you go straight to Christ, turning from sin and trusting Him alone?"*

#### PLAY 4: The rule that tests every rule (authority)
- **Opener:** *"Did Jesus ever correct religious leaders who had tradition on their side?"* (Yes: Mark 7.)
- **Or open with:** *"If a church teaching and the Bible clearly disagreed, which one would you follow?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. **Mark 7:8–9, 13**: "Neglecting the commandment of God, you hold to the tradition of men… invalidating the word of God." *"Did their tradition claim to come from God?"*
  2. **Matt 22:29, 31**: "You are mistaken, not understanding the Scriptures… have you not read what was spoken to you by God?" *"What did Jesus hold them responsible to know?"*
  3. **Acts 17:11**: The Bereans checked an *apostle* against the Scriptures, and are called noble. *"If Paul could be checked, who can't be?"*
  4. **2 Tim 3:15–17**: Scripture makes us "wise for salvation" and the man of God "fully equipped." *"What's missing from 'fully equipped'?"*
  5. **Gal 1:8–9**: "Even if we, or an angel from heaven" preach another gospel, let him be accursed. *"What does Paul put above even himself?"*
- **If they say →**
  - *"Who gave you the Bible? The Church!"* → See the defense table. God gave it, and His people *received* it. The OT was Scripture before any church existed (Matt 22:31; Rom 3:2).
  - *"Without the Magisterium, your reading is just your private opinion."* → Everyone reads, including Catholics who read the Catechism. The question is whether Scripture is clear enough to obey (2 Tim 3:15: Timothy understood it as a child). Vatican II itself says the Magisterium "is not above the word of God, but serves it" [DV 10]. *"Then can Scripture correct it?"*
  - *"The early church fathers weren't 'Bible-only.'"* → Cyril of Jerusalem told his catechumens: "Even to me… do not give absolute credence, unless you receive the proof… from the divine Scriptures".
- **Closer:** *"If Scripture is the one rule that can't err, let's let it settle the biggest question: how a sinner is made right with God. Would you read Romans 3–5 with me this week?"*

#### PLAY 5: You can know (assurance)
- **Opener:** *"Can a person know they have eternal life? Yes or no?"*
- **Or open with:** *"Has a priest ever told you that you're definitely going to heaven?"* (Rome says no one has absolute certainty of persevering without a special revelation [Trent, Sess. 6, can. 16; CCC 2005].)
- **Chain:**
  1. **1 John 5:11–13**: "These things I have written… so that you may *know* that you have eternal life." *"Did John say 'hope' or 'know'?"*
  2. **John 5:24**: The one who hears and believes "has eternal life, and does not come into judgment, but has passed out of death into life." *"What tense is 'has'?"*
  3. **John 10:27–29**: "No one will snatch them out of My hand." *"Who's holding whom?"*
  4. **Rom 8:1, 33–34**: "No condemnation." "Who will bring a charge against God's elect? God is the one who justifies." *"If God has declared you righteous, who can overturn it?"*
  5. **Heb 10:14**: "Perfected for all time." *"Then what is purgatory finishing?"*
- **If they say →**
  - *"Claiming certainty is presumption."* → It would be, if it rested on your performance. It rests on Christ's promise (John 6:37–40). Doubting His word is not humility.
  - *"Mortal sin can make you lose grace [CCC 1861]."* → True faith perseveres because God keeps it (Phil 1:6; 1 Pet 1:5; 1 John 2:19). Our church teaches that the redeemed are kept by God's power forever.
  - *"Purgatory isn't hell. It's a purification for the saved [CCC 1030–1031]."* → *"Christ 'made purification of sins' (Heb 1:3) and His blood 'cleanses us from all sin' (1 John 1:7). What part of 'all' is left?"* MacArthur: deny justification by faith alone "and you must devise an explanation" for how we reach heaven's perfection.
- **Closer:** *"God wants you to know (1 John 5:13). That certainty comes from trusting Christ alone, not from keeping yourself in a state of grace. Will you repent and trust Him today?"*

# ☦️ EASTERN ORTHODOX

**Their position:** Revelation is preserved "by two channels: holy tradition and holy Scripture," and the Church is "the sure repository of holy tradition" [Philaret, *Longer Catechism*, Q16, Q18]. Scripture is to be believed "not otherwise than as the Catholic Church has interpreted," and the Church "cannot err" [Confession of Dositheus, Decrees 2, 12]. Salvation is theosis, "to share in the life of God," and grace and human effort work together (synergy) [GOARCH, "Spirituality"; Philaret Q3–5]. A man is justified "not… through faith alone, but through faith which works through love, that is to say, through faith and works" [Dositheus, Decree 13]. The OCA's current adult manual adds: "Justification therefore is not a single, once-for-all event" [OCA, *Essential Orthodox Christian Beliefs* (2023), p. 218]. Icons receive veneration, "not the full adoration (*latria*)… paid only to the divine nature" [Nicaea II, Definition]. Orthodoxy has no single catechism. Above, conciliar sources (Nicaea II, Dositheus) carry more weight than catechisms and manuals (Philaret, OCA), and you should say which one you're quoting.

**The dividing issue:** Authority, and justification. Orthodoxy says the Church cannot err and that Scripture must be read as the Church has interpreted it, so nothing stands above the Church to correct it. And it makes justification a lifelong process of faith and works, where Scripture speaks of a verdict God has already given to the one who believes. (2 Tim 3:16–17; Mark 7:8–9; Rom 5:1; Rom 4:5)

**Ask this question:** *"When a tradition and Scripture seem to disagree, what do you use to test the tradition?"* (Acts 17:11)

**Scriptural response:** The Bereans tested an apostle by the Scriptures and were called noble for it. Jesus rebuked religious leaders for setting aside God's word for the sake of their tradition. Scripture is breathed out by God and makes the man of God complete. And those whom God justified, He also glorified: having been justified by faith, we have peace with God. (Acts 17:11; Mark 7:8–13; 2 Tim 3:15–17; Rom 5:1; Rom 8:30; Heb 10:14)

**Common objection:** *"The Church gave you the Bible. Without her you wouldn't know which books are Scripture."* → The church received and recognized God's word; she did not create it or stand over it. A man who recognizes the king does not make him king. God's people knew the Law, the Prophets and the Psalms as Scripture before any council met, and Jesus held them accountable to it. His sheep hear His voice. (Luke 24:44; Rom 3:2; John 10:27; 1 Thess 2:13)

**Gospel bridge:** *"Has God already declared you righteous, or are you still waiting to find out?"* Paul counted all his religious standing as loss so that he might be found in Christ, not having a righteousness of his own but the righteousness that comes from God on the basis of faith. That can be settled today. (Phil 3:8–9; Rom 5:1; Rom 8:1; 1 John 5:13)

**Where we agree:** They hold firmly to the Nicene Trinity, the full deity and humanity of Christ (Chalcedon), the virgin birth, the bodily resurrection, Christ's return, and eternal judgment. They reject the papacy and the Immaculate Conception, and generally reject purgatory as Rome defines it, though they pray for the departed [Dositheus, Decree 18]. Their liturgy is full of Scripture. And justification was never the fight in the East that it was with Rome, so some Orthodox writers sound close to us on it. Say this out loud.

**Approach:** Most American Orthodox you'll meet are either cradle Orthodox (it's family and ethnicity) or converts, often young men drawn by beauty, history and seriousness. With converts, don't attack the liturgy. Ask what *authority* tests it. White's warning: Orthodoxy "is an enigma, not only to most in the West, but to itself," and "is not merely Romanism without a Pope". So don't assume Catholic answers fit. Use their own sources (Philaret Q23 is your best friend), keep pressing Scripture as the test, and land on the gospel every time.

### 📇 Orthodox Cheat Card
1. **2 Tim 3:16–17**: *"If Scripture equips the man of God for every good work, what's still missing?"*
2. **Mark 7:8–13**: *"Can a tradition that's very old still cancel God's word?"*
3. **Ex 20:4–5**: *"Does the command forbid only making images, or also bowing to them?"*
4. **Isa 53:5–6, 10**: *"Who laid our iniquity on the Servant, and who was pleased to crush Him?"*
5. **Rom 5:1**: *"'Having been justified': is that finished or still in progress?"*
6. **Eph 2:1–5**: *"Before God made us alive, were we sick or dead?"*

<a id="eo-offense"></a>
### 🗡️ ORTHODOX OFFENSE

#### PLAY 1: Their own test of tradition (Philaret Q23)
- **Opener:** *"Would you agree that we must only follow tradition that agrees with Scripture?"* (Their own catechism says so: "We must follow that tradition which agrees with the divine revelation and with holy Scripture" [Philaret Q23].)
- **Or open with:** *"If an old tradition and a clear verse disagreed, which would win?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. **Philaret Q23** (not Scripture, but theirs): tradition must agree with Scripture. *"So Scripture is the test of tradition?"*
  2. **Mark 7:8–13**: Jesus judged a long, respected tradition by Scripture and found it "invalidating the word of God." *"Was age a defense?"*
  3. **Acts 17:11**: The Bereans tested even Paul by the Scriptures. *"Is anyone above that test?"*
  4. **2 Tim 3:15–17**: Scripture makes "wise for salvation" and "fully equipped." *"So it can do the testing?"*
  5. **Gal 1:8–9**: Even an angel's gospel is tested by the apostolic one. *"Then let's test how we're saved by Scripture. Agreed?"*
- **If they say →**
  - *"Only the Church can interpret Scripture [Dositheus, Decree 2]."* → Then Philaret's test can never fail, because the Church grades its own test. Cyril of Jerusalem (an Eastern father) told his hearers not to believe even him "unless you receive the proof… from the divine Scriptures," and Basil said hearers should examine teachers, "receiving what is in conformity with the Scriptures and rejecting what is opposed to them".
  - *"Basil said unwritten tradition has the same force [quoted in Philaret Q24]."* → Basil said both. Ask: *"Which of the two lets you correct an error if one creeps in?"*
  - *"1 Tim 3:15: the Church is the pillar of truth."* → See the defense table.
- **Closer:** *"If Scripture tests tradition, let it test the biggest question: how is a sinner made right with God? Romans says by faith in Christ, apart from works (Rom 3:28; 4:5), with repentance (Acts 20:21). Will you read Romans 3–5 with me?"*

#### PLAY 2: Justified now (against salvation as a lifelong process)
- **Opener:** *"Can a person be declared righteous by God today, completely, and know it?"*
- **Or open with:** *"Your church's manual says justification 'is not a single, once-for-all event' [OCA EOCB, p. 218]. Can we look at what Paul says?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. **Luke 18:13–14**: The tax collector cried for mercy and "went down to his house justified." (The OCA manual cites this verse too [EOCB p. 218].) *"Was he justified on the way home, or still in process?"*
  2. **Rom 4:4–5**: Credited to "the one who does not work, but believes." *"Where is synergy in that sentence?"*
  3. **Rom 5:1**: "Having been justified by faith, we have peace." *"Past or future?"*
  4. **Rom 8:1, 33–34**: "No condemnation." "God is the one who justifies; who is the one who condemns?" *"Is that a courtroom verdict?"*
  5. **Heb 10:14**: "Perfected for all time." *"How long is 'for all time'?"*
  6. **Eph 2:8–10**: Saved by grace through faith, not of works, *for* good works. *"Where do works go: before or after?"*
- **If they say →**
  - *"Faith working through love (Gal 5:6), so faith and works [Dositheus 13]."* → Gal 5:6 describes what living faith *does*. The same letter says we're justified "by faith in Christ and not by the works of the Law" (Gal 2:16), and condemns adding anything (Gal 3:1–3).
  - *"Salvation is healing and union, not a legal transaction."* → Scripture uses both: a verdict (Rom 8:33–34) *and* new life (2 Cor 5:17). We agree on union and transformation (2 Cor 3:18). We don't let healing replace the verdict.
  - *"Faith alone is easy believism."* → No. Saving faith repents and submits to Christ as Lord (Luke 13:3; Rom 10:9). Works are fruit, not a co-cause.
  - *"Isn't that the Protestant reading? The fathers taught synergy."* → Busenitz collects patristic witnesses to justification by faith, e.g., Clement of Rome: "not justified by ourselves… but by that faith". Letham notes some honored Orthodox writers who sound the same note.
- **Closer:** *"The tax collector didn't bring a process, just a plea, and went home justified. Will you stop leaning on your progress and trust Christ's finished work today (Luke 18:13–14; Rom 4:5)?"*

#### PLAY 3: Icons and the second commandment
- **Opener:** *"Does the second commandment forbid only making images, or also bowing down to them?"* (Ex 20:5)
- **Or open with:** *"When Israel made the golden calf, who did Aaron say they were worshiping?"* (Ex 32:4–5: a "feast to Yahweh.")
- **Chain:**
  1. **Ex 20:4–5**: Not just making; "you shall not worship them or serve them." *"Is the act of bowing itself in view?"*
  2. **Deut 4:15–19**: "You did not see any form on the day Yahweh spoke," so don't make one. *"Why does Moses ground it in what they *didn't* see?"*
  3. **Ex 32:4–5**: The calf was meant to honor Yahweh, and God still judged it. *"Does good intention make a bowed-to image acceptable?"*
  4. **2 Kings 18:4**: Hezekiah smashed the bronze serpent, an object *God commanded* (Num 21:8–9), because Israel burned incense to it. *"If a God-commanded image became an idol when venerated, what about one God never commanded?"*
  5. **John 4:23–24**: The Father seeks those who worship "in spirit and truth." **Col 1:15**: Christ *is* the image of the invisible God. *"Do we need another image to reach the true Image?"*
  6. **1 John 5:21**: "Guard yourselves from idols," written to Christians after the Incarnation.
- **If they say →**
  - *"We give icons veneration, not worship (latria); the honor passes to the prototype [Nicaea II; CCC 2132 says the same]."* → That is their position. The commandment forbids the bowing, not just a wrong inner attitude. Schooping, a former Orthodox priest, asks how mere "veneration" of an image becomes worship of Christ: if it doesn't, it's too little for Christ; if it does, it's worship given through an image.
  - *"God commanded cherubim in the tabernacle (Ex 25:18)."* → Yes, images can exist. But no one was ever told to bow to, kiss or pray before them, and the bronze serpent shows what happens when they do (2 Kings 18:4).
  - *"The Incarnation made God visible, so we can picture Him."* → God made Himself visible *in Christ* (John 14:9). Ortlund documents that early Christians did not bow to images, and that icon veneration came later; he notes that Epiphanius opposed it in the 4th century.
  - *"Nicaea II anathematizes people who refuse to venerate icons."* → It does: "If anyone does not salute such representations… let him be anathema" [Nicaea II, anathemas]. Ask gently: *"Was every Christian before 787 who didn't kiss icons under a curse?"*
- **Closer:** *"Christ is the Image of God, and He invites you to come to Him directly, by faith, with no object in between (Heb 4:16; 10:19–22). Will you?"*

#### PLAY 4: He bore our sins (penal substitution)
- **Opener:** *"In Isaiah 53, whose iniquity did Yahweh lay on the Servant?"*
- **Or open with:** *"Was Jesus punished in our place, or only defeating death for us?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. **Isa 53:5–6**: "Pierced through for *our* transgressions… Yahweh has caused the iniquity of us all to fall on Him." *"Is that substitution?"*
  2. **Isa 53:10**: "Yahweh was pleased to crush Him… a guilt offering." *"Who is acting in this verse?"*
  3. **Gal 3:13**: Christ redeemed us from the curse "having become a curse for us." *"Whose curse did He bear?"*
  4. **2 Cor 5:21; 1 Pet 2:24; 3:18**: Made sin for us; bore our sins in His body; "the just for the unjust." *"In whose place?"*
  5. **Rom 3:25–26**: A propitiation, so that God is "just and the justifier." *"Why does God's justice need to be shown at the cross?"*
- **If they say →**
  - *"That makes the Father a vengeful pagan god punishing a 'whipping boy' [OCA EOCB, pp. 186–88]."* → We reject that caricature too. Father and Son act together in love (John 10:17–18; Rom 8:32; 1 John 4:10). The OCA manual itself affirms that justification "stands alongside others" as a biblical metaphor [EOCB p. 188]. This isn't a conciliar dogma for them, so ask what *this* person believes.
  - *"It's Anselm's medieval theory."* → Isaiah 53 is seven centuries before Christ. Schooping, a former Orthodox priest, shows a substitutionary reading in Chrysostom and Theophylact.
  - *"The East teaches Christus Victor."* → So does Scripture (Col 2:15; Heb 2:14–15). But Col 2:14 says the victory came by canceling "the certificate of debt" against us. Both are true together.
- **Closer:** *"He bore the penalty in your place, and He offers you His righteousness as a gift (2 Cor 5:21). Will you turn from your sin and receive it by faith?"*

#### PLAY 5: Dead, not just sick (ancestral sin)
- **Opener:** *"Before God made us alive, how does Paul describe us: sick or dead?"* (Eph 2:1)
- **Or open with:** *"Do you believe we inherit only death from Adam, or also guilt?"* (The OCA says "only Adam and Eve are guilty of that sin" [OCA Q&A, "Original Sin"].)
- **Chain:**
  1. **Eph 2:1–3**: "Dead in your trespasses… by nature children of wrath." *"Does a neutral nature deserve wrath?"*
  2. **Rom 5:12, 18–19**: "Through one transgression there resulted condemnation to all men… through the one man's disobedience the many were made sinners." *"Condemnation is a guilty verdict, not only a sickness, right?"*
  3. **Ps 51:5**: "In sin my mother conceived me." *"When does David trace his sin back to?"*
  4. **John 3:3–7**: "You must be born again." *"Can the dead cooperate in their own birth?"*
  5. **Eph 2:4–9**: "But God… made us alive… by grace you have been saved… not of works." *"Who did the raising?"*
- **If they say →**
  - *"That's Augustine. The East never accepted original guilt."* → Paul wrote Rom 5 centuries before Augustine. Also note that the Confession of Dositheus says infants "are subject to original sin" and need its remission [Decree 16], so Orthodox sources themselves aren't unanimous.
  - *"Then infants who die go to hell?"* → Our hope rests in God's mercy and character (2 Sam 12:23). It doesn't rest on denying what Rom 5 says about Adam.
- **Closer:** *"Dead people don't need coaching. They need resurrection, and only Christ gives it, by grace through faith (Eph 2:4–9). Will you ask Him for that life?"*

# ❔ AGNOSTICS

**Their position:** "I don't know whether there is a God." Some mean it softly: "I haven't seen enough to decide, and I'm open." Some mean it hard: "No one can know." Most hold that the evidence is not clear either way, that the many religions cancel each other out, and that the honest thing is to withhold judgment. In practice most live as though there is no God while leaving the question open.

**The dividing issue:** Whether God can be known. The agnostic says He has not made Himself clear. Scripture says He has: in what He made, in the conscience, in His word and finally in His Son, so that not knowing is not a neutral place to stand. A life lived with the question left open is a life lived without Him. (Rom 1:19–20; Heb 1:1–2; Matt 12:30; John 3:19–20)

**Ask this question:** *"Are you saying you don't know, or that nobody can know? And what would it take for you to know?"* (John 7:17)

**Scriptural response:** What can be known about God is evident, because God made it evident. The heavens declare His glory day after day. He is not far from each one of us. He promises, "You will seek Me and find Me when you search for Me with all your heart." And John wrote his Gospel so that a reader may believe that Jesus is the Christ and have life in His name. (Rom 1:19–20; Ps 19:1–2; Acts 17:27; Jer 29:13; John 20:30–31)

**Common objection:** *"If God wanted me to believe, He would make it obvious."* → He has. He made a universe, gave you a conscience, and walked into history, died and rose with witnesses. People who saw Jesus' miracles in person still did not believe. Jesus said that if someone will not hear Scripture, he will not be persuaded even if someone rises from the dead. The obstacle is rarely the evidence. (John 12:37; Luke 16:31; John 5:40; Rom 1:20)

**Gospel bridge:** *"You don't have to know everything to know enough. Do you know you have done wrong? Do you know you will die?"* Those two are not open questions. God has fixed a day of judgment and given proof by raising Jesus from the dead, and He receives everyone who comes: "the one who comes to Me I will never cast out." (Heb 9:27; Acts 17:30–31; John 6:37; Isa 55:6–7)

**Where we agree:** Honesty about what you know matters. Blind belief is no virtue, and plenty of religious claims are false. Humility before big questions is right. A person should want reasons.

**Approach:** Find out which kind you are talking to. The open kind is often the easiest person to talk with: thank them for their honesty and invite them to look at the best evidence, Jesus Himself, by reading a Gospel. The hard kind has made a claim, and you can gently ask how he knows it. Either way, don't let the conversation stay in the head. The agnostic is not undecided about how to live; he has already chosen. Aim at the conscience as you would with an atheist, and see the [Atheist](/atheist) plays for more. (Acts 17:11; Rom 2:14–15; 1 Pet 3:15)

<a id="agn-card"></a>
### 📇 Agnostic Cheat Card
1. **Rom 1:19–20**: *"What does Paul say every person already knows, and from what?"*
2. **Jer 29:13**: *"What does God promise the one who really seeks Him?"*
3. **John 7:17**: *"What does Jesus say comes before knowing?"*
4. **John 20:30–31**: *"Why was this Gospel written? Have you read it?"*
5. **Luke 16:31**: *"Would more evidence really settle it?"*
6. **2 Cor 6:2**: *"When does God say the right time is?"*

<a id="agn-offense"></a>
### 🗡️ AGNOSTIC OFFENSE

#### PLAY 1: Can anyone know? (God has spoken)
- **Opener:** *"When you say you don't know, do you mean you haven't looked into it, or that it can't be known?"*
- **Or open with:** *"If God did exist and wanted to be known, how would He do it?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. If they say no one can know: that is itself a claim to know something about God, namely that He cannot or has not made Himself known. *"How do you know that?"*
  2. **Ps 19:1–2; Rom 1:19–20**: Creation is God speaking without words. His eternal power and divine nature are clearly seen in what has been made. *"When you look at the night sky, what do you honestly think?"*
  3. **Rom 2:14–15**: The conscience bears witness. You know some things are truly wrong. *"Where does that come from?"* See [Morality and justice](/topic/morality-and-justice).
  4. **Heb 1:1–2**: God spoke long ago through the prophets, and in these last days has spoken in His Son. If there is a God who made speaking creatures, it is not strange that He speaks.
  5. **John 1:18; John 14:9**: No one has seen God at any time; the only begotten God has explained Him. "He who has seen Me has seen the Father." *"If you wanted to know what God is like, would you look at Jesus?"*
  6. **Acts 17:30–31**: God has given proof to all by raising Him from the dead. That is a claim about history, and it can be examined. See [The resurrection](/topic/the-resurrection).
  7. **John 20:30–31**: These things are written so that you may believe. *"Would you read John's Gospel and tell me who you think Jesus is?"*
- **If they say →**

  - *"You can't be certain either."* → My certainty does not rest on my mind but on God's word. Luke wrote so that we may know the certainty of these things, and John so that we may know we have eternal life. (Luke 1:3–4; 1 John 5:13; 2 Tim 1:12)
- **Closer:** *"God is not hiding. He is 'not far from each one of us.' The question is not whether He can be known, but whether you want to know Him. Do you?"* (Acts 17:27; Jer 29:13)

- **Chain:**
  1. The opener matters. If the answer is "no" or a long pause, the problem was never evidence. Say so kindly.
  2. **John 3:19–20**: This is the judgment: the Light has come into the world, and men loved the darkness rather than the Light, for their deeds were evil. *"Is there anything you would have to give up if it were true?"*
  3. **John 7:17**: "If anyone is willing to do His will, he will know about the teaching, whether it is of God." Willingness comes before knowing.
  4. **Matt 12:30**: "He who is not with Me is against Me." There is no neutral seat. Not deciding is deciding.
  5. **Prov 27:1; James 4:14**: Do not boast about tomorrow. You are a vapor that appears for a little while. *"What if the question closes before you answer it?"*
  6. **Heb 9:27; Luke 12:20**: It is appointed for men to die once, and after this comes judgment. "This very night your soul is required of you."
  7. **Isa 55:6–7; 2 Cor 6:2**: Seek Yahweh while He may be found. Now is the acceptable time. He will have compassion and abundantly pardon.
- **If they say →**
  - *"I'm keeping an open mind."* → Good. An open mind, like an open mouth, is meant to close on something solid. Scripture describes people who are always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth. (2 Tim 3:7; Acts 17:11)
  - *"I'll look into it someday."* → You plan for things far less certain than death. What would it cost to read one Gospel this month? (Luke 12:20; Ps 90:12)
  - *"It doesn't really affect my life."* → If there is a God, nothing affects it more: why you exist, what is right, and what happens when you die. "What does it profit a man to gain the whole world and forfeit his soul?" (Mark 8:36; Eccl 3:11)
  - *"I asked God to show Himself once and nothing happened."* → He has not promised a sign on demand; He has given His word and His Son. Faith comes from hearing the word of Christ. Have you read it, asking Him to show you? (Rom 10:17; Luke 16:31; Jer 29:13)
- **Closer:** *"You've been honest with me, so let me be honest with you: you already know enough to be accountable, and God has done enough for you to be forgiven. Will you come to Him?"* (Rom 1:20; John 6:37)

# 🧭 DEISTS

**Their position:** There is a Creator, and reason and nature are enough to know it. But He does not step into the world: no miracles, no revelation, no incarnation, no answered prayer. The Bible is a human book, Jesus was a great moral teacher, and religion's doctrines were added by men. God wants people to be good, and good people can expect to be all right in the end. Few people use the word today, but a great many hold the belief: God exists, wants us to be nice and happy, stays out of the way unless needed, and takes good people to heaven. Sociologist Christian Smith named that "moralistic therapeutic deism" (2005).

**The dividing issue:** Whether God has spoken and acted. The deist's God made the world and went quiet. The God of Scripture upholds all things by the word of His power, feeds the sparrows, has spoken through prophets, and came in person. With that goes the gospel: the deist needs only to be decent; Scripture says he needs to be forgiven. (Heb 1:1–3; Col 1:17; John 1:14; Rom 3:23)

**Ask this question:** *"If God cared enough to make you, why would He never say anything to you?"* (Heb 1:1–2)

**Scriptural response:** God did not leave Himself without witness: He gives rain and fruitful seasons. Not a sparrow falls apart from the Father, and the hairs of your head are numbered. He spoke through the prophets and, last of all, in His Son, who is the exact representation of His nature. The Word became flesh and dwelt among us. And He has fixed a day when He will judge the world. (Acts 14:17; Matt 10:29–30; Heb 1:1–3; John 1:14; Acts 17:30–31)

**Common objection:** *"Miracles break the laws of nature. A reasonable person can't accept them."* → You already believe the greatest miracle: that God made everything out of nothing. One who can do that can raise a dead man. The laws of nature describe how God ordinarily runs His world; they do not bind the Lawgiver. The question is not whether miracles can happen but whether one did. See [The resurrection](/topic/the-resurrection). (Gen 1:1; Jer 32:17; Acts 26:8)

**Gospel bridge:** *"You believe God is just. Then what does a just God do with the wrong you have done?"* Nature can tell you there is a God, and conscience can tell you that you are guilty. Neither can tell you how to be forgiven. That takes news from God, and He sent it: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. (Rom 1:20; Rom 3:19; Rom 10:17; Rom 5:8)

**Where we agree:** There is a Creator, and creation shows it. Reason is a gift and should be used. There is real right and wrong, and God cares about how we live. Superstition and priestly abuse of power are real evils.

**Approach:** You are halfway there, so start with thanks: this person already grants God. Then press the two places deism is thin. First, it is odd for a God who makes persons to be silent toward them. Second, "be good" is no comfort to anyone honest about himself. Most deists have never examined the resurrection; they dismissed it in advance. Ask them to weigh it as history. With the churchgoing kind, who believes this while calling himself a Christian, use the commandments and the cross: see [The Gospel](/topic/the-gospel). (Acts 17:22–31; Rom 2:4; Acts 26:25–26)

  3. **Luke 18:19; Rom 3:10–12**: "No one is good except God alone." None is righteous, not even one.
  4. **Rom 2:5–6**: A just God will repay each person according to his works. *"You believe God is just. Is that good news for you?"*
  5. **Mark 2:5–7; John 8:58**: Jesus did not only teach. He forgave sins as only God can, and said, "Before Abraham was born, I am." A mere moral teacher who says that is not moral. *"Was He lying, deluded, or telling the truth?"*
  6. **Mark 10:45**: He said He came to give His life as a ransom for many. His own account of His mission is the cross, not the Sermon on the Mount.
  7. **1 Cor 15:3–6; Acts 17:31**: He died for our sins, was buried, was raised and was seen by more than five hundred. God gave proof to all by raising Him.
- **If they say →**

  - *"The miracles and the claims to be God were added later by the church."* → Paul's letters, written within about twenty-five years of the cross, already worship Jesus as Lord and report the resurrection from eyewitnesses then living. There was no time for legend. (1 Cor 15:3–8; Phil 2:6–11)
  - *"Jefferson cut out the miracles and kept the morals."* → He did, with a razor. What he kept is a teacher who claims authority over every conscience and then dies for nothing. The Jesus of history comes whole or not at all. (John 10:37–38; Luke 24:25–27)
  - *"A good God would not condemn decent people."* → A good judge does not ignore crimes because the criminal was polite. And He has done more than be fair: He bore the sentence Himself. See [Hell and eternal judgment](/topic/hell-and-eternal-judgment). (Prov 17:15; Rom 3:25–26)
- **Closer:** *"Deism gives you a Maker and a standard, and leaves you alone with your record. The gospel gives you a Father and a Savior. Which do you need?"* (Rom 5:8; John 1:12)

# 🧑 SECULAR HUMANISTS

**Their position:** There is no God and no life after death, and human beings are the product of unguided evolution. Knowledge comes through science and reason. Right and wrong are worked out from human need and experience, not handed down. People are basically good and can be improved by education, science and fair institutions. Meaning is something we make. Their own summary: "No deity will save us; we must save ourselves" [Humanist Manifesto II, 1973]. Many would simply say, "I believe in science and being a good person."

**The dividing issue:** Who is the measure, God or man, and whether man can save himself. Humanism keeps the Bible's high view of human worth and throws away its foundation, the image of God. It also throws away the Bible's diagnosis, that the human heart is the problem, and so trusts the patient to cure himself. (Gen 1:27; Jer 17:5; Jer 17:9; Rom 1:25)

**Ask this question:** *"You believe every human being has dignity and equal worth. If we are accidents of chemistry, where does that worth come from?"* (Gen 1:27)

**Scriptural response:** God created man in His own image. That is why human life is sacred, why it is wrong to curse or kill a man, and why all nations are of one blood. Yet the heart is deceitful above all else, and none is righteous. Do not trust in princes or in mortal man, in whom there is no salvation: his spirit departs, and his plans perish. (Gen 1:26–27; Gen 9:6; Acts 17:26; Jer 17:9; Rom 3:10–12; Ps 146:3–4)

**Common objection:** *"I can be good without God."* → You can do many good things, and I don't doubt you do. Scripture says why: you are made in God's image, and His law is written on your heart. But the question is not whether you can behave without believing. It is what makes anything good at all, and whether you are good by God's measure. Jesus said no one is. See [Morality and justice](/topic/morality-and-justice). (Rom 2:14–15; Luke 18:19; Rom 3:23)

**Gospel bridge:** *"The manifesto says we must save ourselves. After a century of more education, science and wealth than any people in history, how is that going? How is it going in your own heart?"* We know the good and do not do it. The kindness of God appeared, and He saved us, not by deeds we had done, but by His mercy. (Rom 7:18–19; Rom 7:24–25; Titus 3:4–5; John 8:36)

**Where we agree:** Human beings matter. Cruelty, oppression and ignorance are evils worth fighting. Science is a good tool and truth should be followed. This life is precious. Hypocrisy in religion deserves criticism.

**Approach:** The humanist is living on borrowed capital: human rights, equality and compassion grew in soil the Bible plowed, and they do not grow from "matter in motion." Jeff Durbin's line to a skeptic says it well: "you're acting like a Christian." Affirm the good they love, then ask what it rests on. Do not argue that atheists are immoral; argue that their morality has no foundation and their optimism about man has no evidence. Then go to the conscience and the cross. The [Atheist](/atheist) plays and [Worldviews: how do you know?](/topic/worldviews-how-do-you-know) carry the rest. Remember that humanism is itself a faith with a creed, and its first manifesto called it religious. (Acts 17:22–31; Col 2:8; 1 Pet 3:15)

#### PLAY 1: Where does human worth come from? (the image of God)
- **Opener:** *"What makes a human life valuable?"*
- **Or open with:** *"Why is it wrong to treat people as things?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. Let them answer: reason, feeling, consciousness, contribution. Each of those comes in degrees, so worth would come in degrees: the infant, the disabled and the dying would have less. *"Do you believe that?"* They do not, and that is to their credit.
  2. If we are the unplanned result of matter, time and chance, then "equal dignity" is a feeling one species has about itself. Nature knows nothing of equality; it knows strong and weak.
  3. **Gen 1:26–27**: God created man in His own image, male and female. Worth is given, not earned, and it does not come in degrees.
  4. **Gen 9:6; James 3:9**: That image is the reason murder is a crime against God and why it is wrong even to curse a man.
  5. **Acts 17:26**: He made from one man every nation. Human equality and the unity of the race are Bible doctrines before they were modern ideals.
  6. **Ps 8:3–5**: Small against the heavens, yet crowned with glory and majesty. Scripture explains both our smallness and our greatness.
  7. **Mark 8:36–37; John 3:16**: Jesus put the value of one soul above the whole world, and God gave His Son for it. *"Nothing has ever set human worth higher than the cross."*
- **If they say →**
  - *"We give each other worth by agreement."* → Then a society can agree to take it away, and has. Hitler's Germany agreed that Jews had none. If agreement is the standard, that was good for them at the time, and no outsider could call it evil. A right that the majority grants, the majority can revoke. Only a worth given by God stands over every government. (Acts 5:29; Prov 22:2)
  - *"Morality evolved because cooperation helps us survive."* → That would explain why we feel obligations, not why we have any. And evolution "favors" cruelty as readily as kindness when it works. See [Morality and justice](/topic/morality-and-justice).
  - *"Religion has done more harm than good."* → Much evil has been done in God's name, against Christ's commands. But the worst slaughters of the last century were carried out by regimes that had officially abolished God. And look at who built the hospitals and ended the slave trade. Judge Christ by Christ. (Matt 7:16–20; James 1:27; John 13:35)
  - *"Science tells us what is true."* → Science tells us what is, never what ought to be, and "only science gives knowledge" is not itself a finding of science. See [Faith and reason](/topic/faith-and-reason).
- **Closer:** *"You are right about human dignity and wrong about where it comes from. The God whose image you bear is the reason you care. Would you want to know Him?"* (Gen 1:27; Acts 17:27–28)

#### PLAY 2: Can we save ourselves? (the heart, and death)
- **Opener:** *"Do you think people are basically good?"*
- **Or open with:** *"What would it take to fix the world?"*
- **Chain:**
  1. Ask why, if people are basically good, every society needs locks, police, passwords and contracts, and why no one has to teach a child to be selfish.
  2. **Gen 6:5; Gen 8:21**: Every intent of the thoughts of man's heart was only evil continually; the intent of man's heart is evil from his youth. That was true before the flood and after it.
  3. **Jer 17:9; Mark 7:21–23**: The heart is deceitful and desperately sick. Evil comes from within. Better schools make cleverer sinners; the most educated nation in Europe built the death camps.
  4. **Rom 7:18–19**: "The good that I want, I do not do." *"Is that your experience? Do you live up to your own ideals?"*
  5. **Ps 146:3–4; Eccl 2:16**: Whatever progress we make, each of us dies and his plans perish, and the wise man dies like the fool. Humanism has no answer to the grave. *"If death wins, in what sense have we saved ourselves?"*
  6. **Ezek 36:26; John 3:3**: What we need is not improvement but a new heart, and that is what God promises: "I will give you a new heart." "You must be born again."
  7. **1 Cor 15:21–22; Rom 5:19**: By a man came death, and by a Man came the resurrection of the dead. Christ is the true human being, the one who obeyed, and He gives His record and His life to those who trust Him.
- **If they say →**
  - *"Evil comes from poverty, ignorance and bad systems."* → Those make things worse, and Christians should fight them. But the comfortable and educated sin as freely as anyone, only with better lawyers. Systems are made of hearts. (James 4:1–2; Mark 7:21)
  - *"We make our own meaning."* → A meaning you invent is a story you tell yourself, and it ends when you do. Solomon tried pleasure, work, wisdom and wealth and called it vapor. Meaning has to be given by the One who made you. (Eccl 1:2–3; Eccl 12:13–14; John 17:3)
  - *"This life is all there is, so make the most of it."* → Paul agrees that would follow: "If the dead are not raised, let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die." But Christ has been raised. See [The resurrection](/topic/the-resurrection). (1 Cor 15:32; 1 Cor 15:20)
  - *"I don't need saving."* → From what you have done, and from what is coming. Walk through a few commandments: see [The Way of the Master](/topic/the-way-of-the-master). (Rom 3:19; Heb 9:27)
- **Closer:** *"'No deity will save us; we must save ourselves.' That is not good news; that is a burden no one has carried. Here is good news: 'When the kindness of God our Savior appeared, He saved us.' Would you lay the burden down?"* (Titus 3:4–5; Matt 11:28)

# ⬛ ATHEISTS / AGNOSTICS

**Their position:** Atheist: usually "I don't believe in God" (some say "there is no God"). Agnostic: "I don't know" or "we can't know." Most hold that the universe and life came from natural causes alone and the Bible is a human book. Many see right and wrong as human-made.

**The dividing issue:** Whether God has made Himself known. They say there is not enough evidence. Scripture says every person already knows God from creation and conscience and holds that truth down, because to admit Him is to admit we answer to Him. The problem is not the evidence; it is the heart. (Rom 1:18–21; Rom 2:14–15; Ps 14:1; John 3:19–20)

**Ask this question:** *"Is anything truly right or wrong, for everyone, everywhere? In a universe of only matter and chance, where does that come from?"* (Rom 2:14–15)

**Scriptural response:** In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. The heavens declare His glory, and His eternal power and divine nature are clearly seen in what has been made, so that men are without excuse. The fear of Yahweh is the beginning of knowledge, and in Christ are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge. God now commands all people everywhere to repent, and has given proof by raising Jesus from the dead. (Gen 1:1; Ps 19:1; Rom 1:19–20; Prov 1:7; Col 2:3; Acts 17:30–31)

**Common objection:** *"There's no evidence for God."* → Everything is evidence: that there is a universe at all, that it is ordered, that you have a conscience, and that the laws of logic you are using right now are not made of atoms. The real question is who gets to set the standard of proof. Jesus said that those who will not hear Scripture will not be persuaded even if someone rises from the dead. See [Worldviews: how do you know?](/topic/worldviews-how-do-you-know) and [Faith and reason](/topic/faith-and-reason). (Luke 16:31; Rom 1:20)

**Gospel bridge:** *"You have a conscience. Have you kept even your own standard?"* Walk through a few commandments: see [The Way of the Master](/topic/the-way-of-the-master). It is appointed for men to die once, and after this comes judgment. The same God showed His love in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. (Rom 2:15–16; Heb 9:27; Rom 5:8; Acts 17:30–31)

**Mindset:** Scripture says every person knows God and suppresses that knowledge (Rom 1:18–21). The problem isn't missing evidence; it's the heart. The first move is to **make them define their position**, and they usually back off from "God doesn't exist" to "I don't know." Then aim at the conscience. ([▶](https://youtu.be/cPUJ9-TG54U?t=14))

### 📇 Atheist Cheat Card
1. *"Are you claiming God doesn't exist, or that you don't know?"* (Sets the burden.)
2. *"Have you ever seen something this ordered come from something non-intelligent?"*
3. *"Life only comes from life. Where did the first life come from?"*
4. *"No one dies for what they **know** is a lie."* (1 Cor 15:3–8)
5. *"If heaven and hell were real, wouldn't you want to know before you die?"*
6. **Rom 1:18–21**: they know God and suppress it. Appeal to the conscience, not just the intellect.

<a id="ath-offense"></a>
### 🗡️ ATHEIST OFFENSE

#### PLAY 1: Pin down the position
- **Opener:** *"Are you claiming God doesn't exist, or that you don't know?"*
- *"When you say you're an atheist, are you saying God doesn't exist, or that you lack belief?"* → If it's a claim: *"How do you know?"* If it's lack of belief: *"So you don't know. Would you want to know if God has spoken?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/cPUJ9-TG54U?t=14))
- **If they say** *"I just believe in one less god than you."* → *"You already reject gods who aren't there. I'm asking about the One you already know is there."* (Rom 1:19–21) ([▶](https://youtu.be/cPUJ9-TG54U?t=327))

#### PLAY 2: Design + life from life
- **Opener:** *"Have you ever seen something this ordered come from something non-intelligent?"*
- *"Have you ever experienced something as ordered and designed as this coming from something that isn't intelligent?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/cPUJ9-TG54U?t=216))
- *"Life always comes from life. That's a scientific principle (biogenesis)."* ([▶](https://youtu.be/cPUJ9-TG54U?t=293)) Evolution talks about life that already exists **changing**. It doesn't explain where life **came from**.
- *"Our planet is set for life: its distance from the sun, its tilt, its moon, its magnetic field. When you find that many settings exactly right, do you assume a setter?"* Give this as God's world speaking, not as a neutral coin toss. He formed the earth to be lived in. (Isa 45:18; Ps 19:1–4)
- **Ps 19:1; Rom 1:20; Acts 17:24–28** ("in Him we live and move and have our being").

#### PLAY 3: The "borrowed capital" questions (presuppositional)
- **Opener:** *"Can I ask you about three things we both rely on every day: logic, right and wrong, and your own reasoning?"*
- **Or open with:** *"Where do you get this idea of bad from?"* (When they call something evil or unfair.)
- **Or open with:** *"What would count as evidence for you?"* (When they say there's no evidence for God.)
- **Or open with:** *"Is morality objective, or is it opinion?"* (When they say you're wrong about a moral issue.)
- **Logic:** *"Are the laws of logic physical? Do they change? Where do universal, unchanging laws come from in a universe that's just matter in motion?"*
- **Morality:** *"Is it **objectively** wrong to torture a child for fun? Wrong according to whom?"* → if they say it's subjective, then it's just preference. If they say it's objective, they need a transcendent standard. **Rom 2:14–15**, the law is written on the heart.
- **Reason:** *"If your brain is just chemistry shaped for survival, not truth, why trust it to reason that God doesn't exist?"*
- **Goal:** show they **use** God's world (logic, morality, reason) while denying Him (Rom 1:18–21; Prov 1:7). For the full approach, open [Worldviews: how do you know?](/topic/worldviews-how-do-you-know).

#### PLAY 4: The Resurrection (the center)
- **Opener:** *"If Jesus really rose from the dead, would you become a Christian?"*
- **Or open with:** *"What about the people who didn't believe in Jesus, persecuted His followers, then said, 'I saw Jesus'?"* (When they say it's a game of telephone.)
- *"If Christ didn't rise from the dead, Christianity is false. Period."* (1 Cor 15:14–19) ([▶](https://youtu.be/rgTyjyoS1DE?t=1690)) Christianity puts itself on a testable, historical claim, and God has given proof to all by raising Him from the dead (Acts 17:31).
- **Facts:** crucified publicly under Pilate · the tomb was guarded · the disciples fled as cowards, then died boldly · hostile skeptics were converted (Paul the persecutor, James the doubting brother). *"Why would eyewitnesses and enemies suffer and die for what they **knew** was a lie?"* ([▶](https://youtu.be/rgTyjyoS1DE?t=438))
- **Closer:** *"If heaven and hell were real, wouldn't you want to know now? You wouldn't want to wait until you die to find out."* ([▶](https://youtu.be/cPUJ9-TG54U?t=430))
